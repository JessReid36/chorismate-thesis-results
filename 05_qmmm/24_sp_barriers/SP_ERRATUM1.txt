Stationary-point barriers (SP) - ERRATUM 1 to SP_PLAN.txt and SP_AMENDMENT1.txt. Written 14 October 2026 from a
line-by-line check of both files against the repos, the library and the ORCA outputs, asked for by Jessica while the
Amendment 1 inputs were being committed (Project doc claude/SP_FINAL_CHECK_20261014.md); committed before any
Amendment 1 run is submitted. It corrects statements only: no criterion, rule, threshold, input, code or run changes,
and it decides nothing. Both files stay as committed (sha256 at the end); where they and this erratum differ on a fact,
this erratum holds. Part B lists what the package's amendment has to settle. The progress readout that
SP_AMENDMENT1.txt quotes (WHY) and A1 checks is committed beside this file, verbatim (SMOKE1_PROGRESS_20261013.txt).
Retained-final.

A6 The TS core step's Hessian and time (SP_AMENDMENT1.txt, A1.3: "eight core steps each with a 144-gradient Hessian";

one-sided differences (18c_reduced_region/ts_reduced.out: "Number of displaced atoms ... 24", "Central differences ...

18c's takes about 1 h, not 2-5 h.

A7 The environment step's time (SP_AMENDMENT1.txt, A1.3: "5000 steps at the expected 30-65 s per step (up to 90 h)",
and the TS job's rough worst case, "about 150 h"). The pilot's L-Opt runs (8 cores) report 51.7-65.4 s per step (T1)

Longer later environment steps after a near-5000-step first one would reach the walltime, which A1.3 counts as a
failure; smoke test 2 measures it.

A8 Literature the amendment missed (SP_AMENDMENT1.txt, LITERATURE: "No paper in the library converges a TS with the
alternating form"). True of the library's papers themselves, but one of them reports a chorismate-mutase TS found
that way:
- Ranaghan et al. 2004 (library), "Comparison with previous QM/MM modelling", on Hall, Hindle, Burton & Hillier 2000:
  "the QM and MM regions are optimized 'asynchronously and self-consistently'. The QM region was held fixed whilst
  purely MM (not QM/MM) optimizations were carried out and then QM atoms were optimized in a frozen field of static MM
  atoms. Varying MM/CHELPG atomic charges of the QM atoms at each point along the reaction path were used to optimize
  the MM region. The structures of chorismate, the TS and prephenate were optimized and characterized as stationary
  structures at the B3LYP/6-31G(d) level. The model system was taken from the same crystal structure of BsCM as used
  here (2CHT), and comprised 24 QM atoms and 4117 MM atoms." Ranaghan et al. add that Hall et al.'s barrier of 1.4
  kcal/mol "is probably a QM/MM barrier (i.e. no MM energy included)" and lower than most studies, and that some
  residues were oriented differently, "possibly due to choice of active site and QM/MM method used in optimization of
  structures". Kastner et al. 2007 cite Hall et al. 2000, with Zhang et al. 2000, for the alternating scheme (their
  refs 12 and 10). Hall et al. 2000 is not in the library and has not been read. Unlike Amendment 1, their MM steps
  were MM-only, with fitted charges on the QM atoms; ORCA's L-Opt computes the full QM/MM energy and gradient.
- Kastner, Senn, Thiel, Otte & Thiel 2006 (library; p-hydroxybenzoate hydroxylase), an adiabatic TS search that begins
  with the environment: "L-BFGS steps were performed for all atoms of the active region except the reaction core until
  the environment was converged to within a maximum gradient component of 0.45 x 10-3 Eh a0-1. One partitioned
  rational function optimizer (P-RFO) step was then performed for the reaction core, using an explicit Hessian.
  Continuing with the L-BFGS optimization, this process was iterated until the largest gradient component in the core
  was less than 1.35 x 10-3 Eh a0-1" (a nine-atom core in an active region of about 3300 atoms).
- Murphy, Philipp & Friesner 2000 (library; Kastner et al. 2007's ref 11, adiabatic; minimisation only): "the MM space
  is first minimized with a frozen QM region. Following this MM minimization, ... a single geometry step is taken in
  the QM region with the MM region held frozen."
Amendment 1's cycles begin with the environment, as Kastner et al. 2006, Murphy et al. 2000 and Hall et al. 2000 (as
Ranaghan et al. describe it) do; Zhang et al. 2000 begin with the QM sub-system.

A10 Chorismate-mutase practice (SP_PLAN.txt, LITERATURE: chorismate-mutase QM/MM studies "trace the path by restrained
minimisation along r = d(C-O) - d(C-C), not by a saddle-point search"). This holds for Ranaghan et al. 2004 (a harmonic
restraint on the difference of the breaking C-O3 and forming C3-C9 bonds) and Claeyssens et al. 2005 ("restrained
optimizations in both directions along the reaction coordinate"), and in substance for Senn et al. 2009, whose
potential-energy profiles were "reaction-path calculations using the same RC definition as for the US simulations",
with maxima located by cubic-spline fits (how the coordinate was held is not described). It is not true of every
chorismate-mutase study: Hall et al. 2000 (A8) optimised and characterised the TS as a stationary structure. Ranaghan
et al.'s 3.0 kcal/mol is the barrier increase from their boundary restraints "compared to the barriers found with
fewer restraints" (AM1/CHARMM).

A11 Ryde 2016, section 4, item 7 (SP_PLAN.txt, LITERATURE and PACKAGE). In full: "If relaxation of the MM system
significantly changes the structures and energies, the approach needs to be partly modified. The first step is to
optimize the MM system for one state and employ this MM structure for all the other states. If the results still
depend on whether the MM system is relaxed or not, or if the results depend on the state for which the MM system is
relaxed, the QM system needs to be extended until the energies are stable." SP_PLAN's "the model must be extended"
should read "the QM system needs to be extended until the energies are stable". The PACKAGE's response when the
surroundings check fails ("both sets are carried forward") is this project's choice, not Ryde's prescription (B4).

A12 Context of three citations (SP_PLAN.txt, LITERATURE). Cooper & Kastner 2014's 8 A protocol comes from force-field
calculations "to mimic QM/MM optimizations" (cyclophilin A; a truncated system of about 4600 atoms, about 360 of them
optimised; transition states by a dimer method in DL-FIND, default criteria). Kastner et al. 2007's 8 A fluorinase
region (3960 degrees of freedom) is from their energy-minimisation test (section III.D, started from a structure
optimised under a constraint near the TS), not from a saddle-point search. Rod & Ryde 2005's 59 and 26 kJ/mol are
forward barriers of catechol O-methyltransferase with the MM environment as "a static set of point charges", the same
for every point of the path, averaged over 10 configurations from reactant- and product-complex simulations (their
Figure 9).
