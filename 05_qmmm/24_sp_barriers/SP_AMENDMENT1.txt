DECISION (Jessica, 13 October 2026)
Replace SP_PLAN's optimisations of the whole region (P3 TS, P6 R_IRC and P_IRC, P7 R0) by the alternating scheme of
QM/MM optimisation: the 24 substrate atoms (the core) and the rest of the region (the environment) are optimised in
turn, each with the other held fixed, until neither changes. Smoke-test it again on frame 42436 at 8 and 6 A.

LITERATURE (full papers in the library; DOIs below)
Kastner et al. 2007, section I: "In microiterative schemes, the regions are optimized separately. One can either use an
adiabatic scheme ... that is, fully optimize the outer region after each optimization step of the inner region, or use
an alternating scheme ..., in which each region is completely optimized with the other one frozen."
Senn & Thiel 2009, section 3.2: "Mutual convergence of the optimizations can be achieved in essentially two ways: 1)
The adiabatic approach, in which the environment is fully relaxed in each macrostep. 2) The alternating scheme, in which
the core and environment optimizations are alternately iterated to convergence, with the core atoms being kept fixed
during the microiterations and vice versa." For transition states: "A core fragment is defined that contains the atoms
immediately involved in the reaction. For these core degrees of freedom, the Hessean is calculated, and a traditional
second-order algorithm takes an optimization step towards the saddle point. With the core kept frozen, the environment
fragments are then fully relaxed ... This scheme thus searches for a first-order saddle point controlled by the
(low-dimensional) core Hessean, while minimizing the energy with respect to the degrees of freedom of the environment."
Zhang, Liu & Yang 2000 (one of Senn & Thiel's references for the alternating scheme), section II.B: the QM sub-system is
optimised with the MM atoms fixed (quasi-Newton, redundant internal coordinates), the MM sub-system with the QM atoms
fixed (truncated Newton, Cartesians), "(3) Repeating previous steps until both meet convergence criteria at the same
geometry"; for their enzyme reactant, with "those atoms within 20 A ... allowed to move (3008 atoms)", "the convergence
is quite rapid, no more than eight cycles. And the energy and gradient decrease monotonically and quickly."
Where these papers locate transition states, they use the adiabatic form: Senn & Thiel's description above (one step
towards the saddle, then the environment fully relaxed), and Senn et al. 2009's fluorinase work ("In
microiterative transition-state searches, the core region, for which the Hessean is calculated, included the halide,
C(5')H2, and S"). Zhang et al. use the alternating form for minima and for the points of a restrained path, and take the
TS as that path's maximum. No paper in the library converges a TS with the alternating form. The ORCA 6.0 manual (QM/MM
chapter, interface to GROMACS) documents a third variant for a TS: "This microiterative scheme can also be used to
perform a transition state optimization ... you can carry out an optimization of the MM system and at the same time
perform a transition state optimization of the QM system with ORCA", ORCA performing "a QM geometry optimization every
time it is called by Gromacs". Not in the library and not read (cited as existing only): Prat-Resina, Bofill,
Gonzalez-Lafont & Lluch 2004 (Int. J. Quantum Chem. 98, 367; the assessment of microiterative options for TS searches in
enzymes that Senn & Thiel cite); Prat-Resina, Gonzalez-Lafont & Lluch 2003 (doi 10.1016/S0166-1280(03)00307-5); Turner,
Moliner & Williams 1999 (doi 10.1039/a808364i); Billeter, Turner & Thiel 2000 (doi 10.1039/a909486e).
How this amendment uses them: ORCA 6.0.1 has no microiterative driver of its own outside its GROMACS interface, which
has never been set up for this system; writing one would mean writing our own optimiser. So the adiabatic form
(environment re-relaxed after every core step) is not available here; the alternating form is, as separate ORCA jobs.
The core is the QM region, as in Senn & Thiel's description ("The core contains (at least) the QM atoms"). For the TS,
each core step is a full TS search of the 24 substrate atoms with their exact Hessian (18c's input, which converged
here); the environment steps are the C4 pilot's T1 step. Zhang et al. evaluate the environment steps with fitted ESP
charges for the QM density, and Kastner et al. with ESP charges plus energy and gradient corrections, a scheme that
"retains the accuracy of a standard QM/MM optimization scheme" (their section IV); ORCA's L-Opt computes the full QM/MM
energy and gradient at every step (the pilot's outputs time an SCF and a gradient per step), so no such approximation
enters. In the limit of full convergence the energy gradient vanishes for core and environment alike, in either form;
here the alternation stops at the tolerances of A1.3, so the point is stationary to those tolerances (A1.7). The core is
a saddle point (TS) or minimum of the substrate with the environment fixed, and P4/P8 characterise it as SP_PLAN does.
Senn & Thiel cite an assessment of microiterative options for an enzyme reaction; the convergence of the alternating
form for a TS is tested here, not assumed: the step fails if it does not converge (A1.3).

A1.1 PROTOCOL CHANGE (replaces the optimisation in P3, P6, P7; the starts and SP_PLAN's checks stay; A1.2 adds checks)
Each of TS, R_IRC, P_IRC and R0 is found by cycles K = 1, 2, ..., 8, each cycle two ORCA jobs:
  environment step: L-Opt (ORCA's Cartesian L-BFGS, run by its MD module), active atoms = the region without the 24
    substrate atoms 6207-6230, so the substrate is fixed; TightSCF; TolMaxG 1.0e-4, TolRMSG 3.0e-5 Eh/bohr (the C4 and
    pilot T1 thresholds, equal to TightOpt's gradient thresholds); MaxIter 5000 (see A1.3).
  core step: active atoms = the 24 substrate atoms alone, the environment fixed. TS: OptTS TightOpt, Calc_Hess true,
    Hybrid_Hess over the 24 atoms (P3's settings: an exact substrate Hessian at the start of every core step), MaxIter
    500. R_IRC, P_IRC, R0: Opt TightOpt, MaxIter 500.
Cycle 1's environment step starts from the step's SP_PLAN start (TS: region atoms from the band's climbing image in the
MD-structure surroundings; R_IRC/P_IRC: the IRC ends; R0: region atoms from band image 0); every later environment step
from the previous cycle's core result; every core step from its cycle's environment result. Every start file is checked
against the sha256 recorded when the step that wrote it passed.

A1.2 CHECKS OF EVERY STEP (spa_stage.py; a failed check fails the stationary point and stops its job)
environment step: ORCA terminated normally; "The minimization has converged", with the largest and RMS gradients ORCA
prints beneath it (if it prints none, those of the CSV's last step) at or below their thresholds; the energy over the
last 50 optimiser steps fell by at most 2.5e-4 Eh (C4's plateau test; not applicable to a step of 50 or fewer optimiser
steps, recorded as n/a); the substrate and every atom outside the region unmoved (2e-6 A). It also records the
environment gradient where the step began (reported only; A1.3).
core step: ORCA terminated normally; "THE OPTIMIZATION HAS CONVERGED"; every atom other than the 24 substrate atoms
unmoved; the substrate reactant-like for R_IRC and R0, product-like for P_IRC (SP_PLAN's distance classes and checks)
and, added here, neither reactant- nor product-like for the TS (SP_PLAN's P3 had no class test for the TS; a core TS
search that ends in a minimum would fail P4 or P5 later, this stops the alternation at once). Every TS known in this
project lies well inside the class (frame 820: O-C 2.10, C-C 2.53 A; smoke test 1's 8 A start 2.17 / 2.47 A).

A1.3 CONVERGENCE OF THE ALTERNATION (Zhang et al.'s "both meet convergence criteria at the same geometry"; the numbers
are fixed here, before any Amendment 1 input exists)
The alternation has converged after cycle K >= 2 when all three hold:
  (a) cycle K's environment step changed the energy by at most 0.10 kcal/mol (first minus last optimiser energy, in
      absolute value; a rise is also printed as such);
  (b) cycle K's core energy differs from cycle K-1's by at most 0.10 kcal/mol;
  (c) no substrate atom moved more than 0.02 A between cycle K-1's and cycle K's core geometries.
The stationary point is then cycle K's core geometry and its energy, the last FINAL SINGLE POINT ENERGY (QM/MM) of that
core step. Not converged after cycle 8: the stationary point fails ("the alternation did not converge in 8 cycles").
Why an energy test, and why these numbers: Zhang et al. state the rule in words ("until both meet convergence criteria
at the same geometry") with an MM criterion of RMS gradient "less than 0.1 kcal/(mol*A)" (0.418 kJ/mol/A). Applied to
the C4 pilot's six T1 runs - the environment relaxed around the climbing-image substrate, from the NEB's environment -
that criterion was already met where each run began (RMS 0.090-0.212 kJ/mol/A, from line 2 of each energy file, 5e-6 to
9e-6 A from the start; largest components 1.28-2.25 kJ/mol/A), yet the environment then gave up 0.27, 0.58, 0.96, 1.00,
1.18 and 3.71 kcal/mol over 104-449 steps. An RMS-gradient test at that level would have called all six relaxed. Kastner
et al. 2007 (section III.C) report the same effect: a standard optimisation under loose criteria ended 23-24 kJ/mol
above the tightly converged one, "caused by the relatively loose convergence criteria applied to the whole system".
Zhang's wording with ORCA's own thresholds instead (the environment step's first point already within TolMaxG and
TolRMSG) would be the most literal test, but the pilot's six environment steps (T1) end at 90-98% of the
largest-gradient threshold, so the core's own step tolerance (TightOpt, about 5e-4 A) can push a converged environment
back over it. The rule above therefore measures what the barrier depends on: how much energy the environment and the
core still gave up in the last cycle, and how far the substrate still moved. The environment gradient where each
environment step began is recorded and printed as Zhang et al.'s criterion, reported only. The numbers: 0.10 kcal/mol is
a tenth of the 1.0 kcal/mol tolerance used for C4's reactant tests (A1 of C4_CRITERIA.txt and the pilot's |dR|) and
would have rejected all six T1 starts above; 0.02 A is a fifth of the "about 0.1 A" by which Zhang et al. stepped their
reaction coordinate; 8 cycles is the most Zhang et al. needed ("no more than eight cycles"). Within every step the
gradient criteria are TightOpt's, as SP_PLAN fixed. MaxIter 5000 for the environment step instead of the pilot's 2000:
the pilot's and C4's starts were already relaxed by the NEB (pilot 104-449 steps; the 13 C4 step-2 runs reported on 11
October 325-710 steps), whereas cycle 1 here starts from a region that smoke test 1 showed relaxing by 184-313 kcal/mol;
5000 steps at the expected 30-65 s per step (up to 90 h) fit the 176 h walltime with room for the later, short cycles.
The thresholds that decide convergence are unchanged; but as in SP_PLAN, a step that reaches its MaxIter, and a job that
reaches the 176 h walltime, fail the stationary point, so these limits also decide what counts as feasible. All cycles
of one stationary point run in one job (A1.4); a rough worst case for the TS job (a 5000-step first environment step,
eight core steps each with a 144-gradient Hessian, seven later environment steps) is about 150 h.

A1.5 SMOKE TEST 2 AND THE REGION RULE
Frame 42436 again, both radii, S = md: run folders f42436_r8_a1 and f42436_r6_a1, with the same region (region.txt
identical to f42436_r8 / f42436_r6) and start coordinates identical to smoke test 1's (the bundle checks both before
committing the inputs). The region rule is SP_PLAN's, with "the TS, R_IRC, P_IRC and R0 optimisations passed their
checks" read as "the TS, R_IRC, P_IRC and R0 alternations converged within 8 cycles, every step of every cycle passing
its checks (A1.2)"; spa_report.py smoke prints the verdict. The barrier values play no part in the rule.
Smoke test 1's runs (f42436_r8, f42436_r6) are exploratory from the commit of this amendment: they decide nothing, and
whatever they reach is reported beside the Amendment 1 runs (spa_report.py prints E(TS) and E(R0) of each Amendment 1
run minus those of the SP_PLAN run from the same start file, when both passed; reported only). Whether the running ones
are stopped is Jessica's decision; their outputs are committed when they end, whatever they show.
Machinery stops, fixed here: the job script stops a job before any check can run, and no record is written for that
step, when there is too little free disk before a step; when the disk or quota filled during ORCA ("No space left on
device" or "Disk quota exceeded" in the ORCA output, or under 1 GB free right after ORCA); when ORCA reports an input
error ("INPUT ERROR" or "UNRECOGNIZED OR DUPLICATED KEYWORD") before its first energy; and when a start file is missing,
exists already or is not the one recorded. A step that failed a check is also counted as a machinery stop when its ORCA
output or its job's output reports a full disk or quota. spa_report.py prints "not decided" for that radius; the run
folder is then renamed (suffix _stop1), generated again (byte-identical to the committed inputs) and submitted once more
from the beginning, and the stop and its cause are reported. A second machinery stop of the same radius counts as a
failure of the rule for that radius (spa_report.py applies this when the _stop1 folder exists). An input error means the
input is wrong: it is corrected in a disclosed amendment and the radius run again. Every other end - a check that fails,
ORCA ending abnormally for any other reason, MaxIter, the walltime - is a failure as defined above.
The 30-frame package uses this amendment.

A1.6 EXPECTED COST AND SIZES (estimates; the runs record the real ones)
Per step: energy and gradient took 53-66 s per cycle in smoke test 1 at 16 cores (once 210 s, 6 A R0); the pilot's L-Opt
took 52-65 s per step at 8 cores (its "Performance" lines). Cycle 1's environment step, a few hundred to a few thousand
steps: about 4 h to 3.5 days; later environment steps start near a minimum (tens to hundreds of steps). TS core step:
the substrate's numerical Hessian (144 displaced gradients) then tens of cycles, about 2-5 h; minimum core steps under 2
h. Per stationary point, probably 1-3 days, at most about 7 (the walltime). Files: an environment step writes a
full-system DCD trajectory of 0.67 MB per step (up to 3.3 GB at 5000 steps) and a full-system PDB, both deleted when the
step passes; kept per cycle are the full-system end geometry (env.xyz about 2.4 MB, core.xyz about 3.8 MB), the energies
per step (CSV) and the outputs (xz above 50 MB). Kept per run folder: about 0.1-0.3 GB. Disk checked before every step
(20 GB free for alternation steps, since four jobs start together and each environment step can write 3.3 GB; df
measures the filesystem, not a quota). On a failed check nothing in that step is deleted.

A1.7 LIMITATIONS ADDED
At convergence the environment was minimised for cycle K-1's core geometry, which differs from the final one by at most
0.02 A (A1.3 c), and the core step held the environment fixed; the final point is therefore a stationary point to the
tolerance of A1.3, not to TightOpt's tolerance over the whole region at once. As in SP_PLAN, P4 tests the substrate
block of the Hessian with the environment fixed; the environment's response to motion along the reaction mode is not
part of that test.
