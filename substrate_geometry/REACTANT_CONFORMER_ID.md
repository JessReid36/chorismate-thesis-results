# Reactant conformer identity: confirmed reactive CHAIR (not DIAX)

**Question:** the bare reactant (enzyme-inherited, C1-C6 = 3.12 A) is folded/near-attack --
but is it the *reactive* pseudo-diaxial chair (CHAIR), or the *non-reactive* DIAX
conformer? Both are folded and pseudo-diaxial, so the near-attack C1-C6 distance alone does
not distinguish them.

**Discriminator (Guo et al. PNAS 2001):** DIAX differs from CHAIR by having the side-chain
**carboxylate**, rather than the side-chain **methylene**, positioned over the ring forming
carbon. So the test is: which side-chain group sits over the ring forming carbon (our atom
12)?

**Measurement (from our geometry):**
- methylene C0 -> ring forming carbon C12 = **3.12 A** (poised to attack)
- side-chain carboxylate C4 -> ring forming carbon C12 = **4.28 A** (further away)

The **methylene** is the group over the ring forming carbon -> this is the **reactive
CHAIR** conformer, not DIAX.

Atom roles were assigned rigorously from connectivity (6-membered carbocycle
[8,10,12,13,15,17]; ether O = atom 7; enolpyruvyl side chain C0(=CH2)-C3-O7; forming bond
C0-C12), not by guessing atom-number correspondence with the paper's numbering.

## Conclusion
The inherited-from-enzyme reactant is the genuine reactive chair conformer (methylene
poised over the ring at 3.12 A), consistent with the enzyme having pre-organised the
substrate into the reactive form. This is a belt-and-suspenders confirmation on top of the
simpler observation that 3.12 A is near-attack and clearly not one of the extended ~5 A
non-reactive DIEQ/DIAX forms.

**Note:** the CHAIR-vs-DIAX distinction is methylene-vs-carboxylate over the ring, NOT the
near-attack distance -- both CHAIR and DIAX are folded/pseudo-diaxial.
