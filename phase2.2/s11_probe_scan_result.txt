THREE FINDINGS

1. NO ELECTRONIC OVER-POLARISATION ANYWHERE FROM 1.5 TO 10 A, AT FIXED GEOMETRY.
   Loewdin charge shift on the nearest atom is monotone and small, -0.226 e even at
   1.5 A, shrinking smoothly outward. Collapse would show an ACCELERATING shift.
   Log-log slopes at short range are SHALLOWER than -2 (-1.76 at 1.5->2.0), not
   steeper; over-polarisation steepens them. The HOMO is pulled down at close range,
   i.e. the probe binds the unbound dianion rather than distorting it.

   THIS RESULT IS SCOPED TO FIXED GEOMETRY AND SAYS NOTHING ABOUT THE COLLAPSE
   RECORDED IN step_7_relaxed_validation_and_point_charge_collapse.md. That collapse
   is GEOMETRIC - a substrate oxygen migrating onto a designed +1 and ending 0.879 A
   away, 0.641 A inside its own van der Waals contact - and occurs during RELAXED
   OPTIMISATION. Nothing in this scan was allowed to move. See section 11.

2. A BARE +1 UNDERSHOOTS THE REAL ARGININE BY A FACTOR OF 3.5.
   Mean ddE at 3.2 A is -1.53 kcal/mol against Burschowsky's measured differential of
   -5.3 (5.9 TS stabilisation minus 0.6 ground-state binding). So one point charge
   delivers 29 per cent of what the guanidinium achieves. Expected, since the real
   group is delocalised, carries several hydrogen-bond donors and sits in a protein.
   It also means a single charge cannot reach the Burschowsky benchmark.

3. FRAME 43087 CHANGES SIGN BEYOND 4 A, AND THIS IS THE DESIGN-RELEVANT FINDING.
   Two frames behave as dipole-like coupling predicts, slope near -2, same sign
   throughout. The third gives -0.47 at 3.2 A, a quarter of the strongest frame, and
   crosses to POSITIVE between 3.6 and 4.0 A. Its Loewdin and HOMO behaviour are
   normal, so this is not a model failure: it is a genuinely different electrostatic
   environment in which the dipole term nearly cancels and higher-order terms dominate.

   A CHARGE PLACED BEYOND 4 A TO HELP TWO OF THESE FRAMES WOULD ACTIVELY HURT THE
   THIRD. This is measured evidence for the mean-penalised-by-spread objective of
   section 3.3, which until now rested on the Shaik screening argument alone.
