Stage 4d - EXPLORATORY note (10 October 2026). Not pre-registered: written after STAGE4D_REPORT.txt was seen. The
verdicts stand as recorded there: machinery checks PASS (Phase 1's QM/MM reactant reproduced to 1.5e-8 Eh); PC1 FAIL
(the whole enzyme, frozen at the reactant's environment, +5.26 kcal/mol, outside [-13.3, +3.3]); PC2 FAIL (Arg7 +1.05
and Arg116 +0.27, where Szefczyk et al. 2004 find both catalytic); PC3 PASS. The reversed-charge runs (NC1) have
HOMO-LUMO gaps of 0.55 and 0.59 eV, below the screen's 1.0 eV, so their values are unresolved and not used. Every
number below is in STAGE4D_EXPLORATORY_REPORT.txt (cs4d_explore.py on the committed inputs and outputs) or
STAGE4D_REPORT.txt.

1. PC1 failed on the steric term, not on electrostatics. Frozen at the reactant's environment, the enzyme's QM part
   (electrostatics with polarisation) is -2.00 kcal/mol, inside the band; the LJ term is +7.26, and +5.76 of it is
   Glu78 alone: with the protein held at the reactant, the TS's hydroxyl hydrogen comes to 1.38 A from Glu78's OE1
   (1.53 A in the reactant). In the TS's own environment the substrate-environment LJ change is -0.62. A protein
   frozen at the reactant does not make room for the TS; letting it relax removes the penalty. Any frozen-geometry
   total can carry such a steric term, which is what PC1's consequence (frozen values rank designs only and are
   never compared with an enzyme number) and reporting commitment 3 (the relaxed barrier decides) guard against.

2. Measured the way Claeyssens et al. 2005 measured it, the enzyme's electrostatics come out at their size. With
   each state in its own environment (reactant in env_R, TS in env_TS), frame 41786's TS stabilisation splits into
   -4.87 kcal/mol electrostatic including polarisation (Claeyssens et al., B3LYP/6-31G(d)/CHARMM27: 4.7 on average
   over 16 paths, from the same decomposition), +5.23 substrate-environment van der Waals, and -1.17 for the rest
   (mainly the protein's own MM energy change); together Phase 1's -0.81. Frame 41786 is the sixth weakest of 43
   frames because of its van der Waals term, not its electrostatics. One frame against a 16-path average: an
   agreement in size, not a test.

3. PC2. Arg90 and Lys60' agree with Szefczyk et al. in sign; Arg90 alone is -5.64 kcal/mol and -5.84 in situ (their
   MP2 value -9.06; Burschowsky et al. 2014 find Arg90 contributes "up to 5.9 kcal/mol" to TS stabilisation, a free
   energy in water, so comparable only qualitatively). Arg7 (+1.05) and Arg116 (+0.27) disagree; Arg63' (not judged)
   is +3.54 against their -1.40. What the data rule out and what they leave open:
   - Not the residues' places. Arg7 and Arg90 both bind the enolpyruvyl carboxylate (O1, O2), and Arg90 also the
     ether O3, as Arg-7 and Arg-90 share one carboxylate of the transition-state analogue in the crystal structure
     (Chook et al. 1993); Arg63' binds the ring carboxylate (O5, O6), and in Ranaghan et al. 2004's QM/MM model too
     "arginines 7, 63, and 90 interact with the carboxylate groups". Arg116 makes no contact (second shell; in the
     crystal it sits 3.1-4.1 A from the other carboxylate).
   - Not the frozen environment. The four residues in contact move at most 0.18 A between env_R and env_TS, and
     Arg7's hydrogen bond to O1 lengthens in both (1.94 A at the reactant; 2.11 frozen and 2.15 in its own TS
     environment). Ranaghan et al. 2004 report that Arg7's hydrogen bonds shorten towards the TS on their path; here
     they do not.
   - Not frame 41786 alone, for Arg7 and Arg63'. The first-order field (A_v2) at the grid sites nearest their charge
     centres is positive, so a cation there raises the barrier: near Arg7 in 29 and 30 of 30 frames (22 of 30 at its
     third site), near Arg63' in 30 of 30 (23 of 30 at its third); near Arg90 it is negative in 30 of 30. For Arg116
     the sign varies (negative in 18-24 of 30 frames), so its disagreement may be specific to this frame.
   - Not obviously the substrate's geometry: breaking and forming distances 1.475 and 3.158 A (reactant) and 2.191
     and 2.470 A (TS), against Szefczyk et al.'s 1.45 and 3.44, 2.17 and 2.58.
   - Open: the electronic-structure method. Szefczyk et al.'s residue values rest on HF/6-31G(d) wavefunctions (from
     the point-charge potential up to MP2 every level gives Arg7 the same, catalytic, sign), on a structure from
     an RHF/6-31G(d)/CHARMM22 path. The enzyme's TS stabilisation (QM/MM against QM energy) is 19.7 kcal/mol at that
     level on that path (Ranaghan et al. 2004) and 4.2 at B3LYP/6-31G(d) on 16 others (Claeyssens et al. 2005; 4.7 for
     its electrostatic part): the literature's own values differ fourfold between methods and paths. This model is
     B3LYP. Whether the sign of the field at the carboxylates depends on the method can be tested at this geometry.

4. Why it matters for the design. In this model a cation next to either carboxylate raises the barrier and an anion
   there lowers it (Stage 4c's E3, -1 next to O5: -11.7 kcal/mol with LJ). The enzyme puts cations there (Arg7,
   Arg63'), and Szefczyk et al.'s catalytic field marks Arg7's place as one where a cation lowers the barrier. An
   optimiser working from this field will favour anions beside the carboxylates.
   If that sign is a property of the method rather than of the reaction, designs would be built on it. It has to be
   settled before the optimiser stage (PC2's consequence) and before Stage 5 rebuilds the A matrix at the same level.

5. PC3. A +1 point at a residue's charge centre gives 0.71 (Arg90) and 0.49 (Arg7) of the residue's own effect, with
   its sign; moved to the grid at contact distance it gives 1.14 times more (about 0.8 of Arg90); replacing Arg90 by
   a sphere inside the enzyme loses 2.40 of Arg90's 5.84. A sphere understates a real arginine.
   The six residues alone sum to -1.86 kcal/mol (QM part) against the whole enzyme's -2.00: in this model the
   enzyme's small net electrostatic effect is a balance of catalytic residues (Arg90 -7.29, Glu78 -4.42) and
   anti-catalytic ones (Arg63' +5.98 and Arg7 +2.57, which bind the carboxylates, and Lys60' +1.07). The large
   design numbers come from the arrangement (several charges, all placed to help, none binding the substrate), not
   from the sphere.

6. Reading taken forward (consequences in the plan update):
   - frozen totals are screening numbers only and are never compared with an enzyme number (PC1);
   - before the optimiser stage and before Stage 5, test whether the field's sign at the carboxylates depends on the
     electronic-structure method (PC2's re-examination; proposed as Stage 4e);
   - r90 0.71, r7 0.49, g 1.14 and SUB_R90 - ENZ +2.40 are quoted beside every design magnitude (PC3).
