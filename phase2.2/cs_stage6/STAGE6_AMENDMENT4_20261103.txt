A4.1  WHAT THIS DECIDES, AND WHY IT HAS TO BE AN AMENDMENT RATHER THAN A NOTE

STAGE6_ERRATUM1.txt did not merely record the relaxation protocol's coordinates - it CHOSE them.
Its section E6.1, "THE RUN THAT IS MEANT, AND THE PROTOCOL AS IT WILL BE", rejected armb_anch
(which had anchored four ring carbons and optimised in ORCA's default redundant internals) and took
phase2.2/wp4_s13_rerun/armb_freecart/job.inp instead, quoting it in full:

      %geom
        MaxIter 1000
        coordsys cartesian
        ProjectTR false
      end

So "Cartesian coordinates" in STAGE6_CRITERIA.txt line 83 is a deliberate choice backed by a
converged run, not a slip. Changing it is therefore a change to the protocol.

STAGE6_RELAXATION_STATE_20261028.txt says exactly that, in its section R4:

      "Cartesian coordinates are how the frozen surroundings are held, so moving to redundant
      internals is a change to the protocol and not a correction to it. Until that is decided,
      Loss, G0 and kappa are not merely unmeasured - they are unmeasurable [...] Deciding it
      requires a dated amendment, as HANDOVER_20261026.md section 8 item 10 already says. This
      note does not propose one and does not decide it."

STAGE6_RELAX_TS_NOTE1_20261103.txt then made the change and recorded it as a note. That was the
wrong instrument: a note records, an amendment decides, and this is a decision. This amendment is
that decision, taken on the same three changes NOTE 1 describes, with nothing new added to them.
NOTE 1 is not edited; it stands as the record of the refusals.


A4.2  WHY THE PROTOCOL AS PRE-REGISTERED CANNOT BE EXECUTED

ORCA 6.0.1 refuses OptTS with coordsys cartesian at input parsing, before any calculation. Verbatim
from relax/S6-C/TS_opt/job.out, job 423856, 3 November 2026:

      WARNING: You cannot use transition state optimization for a Cartesian optimization!!!
               Please choose optimization with redundant coordinates (!OPT)
        ===>   Skipping actual calculation
      [file orca_main/main_input_check.cpp, line 8049]: Error (ORCA_MAIN): ... aborting the run

and from relax/n10_A/TS_opt/job.out, job 423860, the same day, at line 7942 of the same source
file, the Hessian half of the same refusal. Neither output is committed; NOTE 1 is their record.

This is not a convergence failure or a resource problem. The reactant side of the protocol runs
exactly as pre-registered and is unaffected: S6-C/R_opt converged in 95 cycles on 21 October 2026
in Cartesians, and both reactant relaxations are running in Cartesians now.


A4.3  WHAT CHANGES, AND ONLY ON THE TRANSITION-STATE SIDE

From this date, the relaxation's TS side is specified as:

      ! B3LYP D3BJ def2-SVP def2/J RIJCOSX TightSCF [QMMM] OptTS NumFreq
      %geom
        MaxIter 1000
        Calc_Hess true
        Hybrid_Hess {the 24 substrate atoms} end
      end

   1. NO coordsys cartesian and NO ProjectTR. The optimisation runs in ORCA's default redundant
      internal coordinates.
   2. Calc_Hess true with Hybrid_Hess over the 24 substrate atoms, which supplies the Hessian a
      saddle-point search needs.
   3. NumFreq, not Freq: numerical frequencies.

THE REACTANT SIDE IS NOT TOUCHED. It keeps coordsys cartesian and ProjectTR false as ERRATUM 1
E6.1 set them, so that it remains the protocol WP4's converged armb_freecart demonstrated.

The two sides of the barrier therefore use different optimisation coordinates. That is stated here
rather than left to be noticed, and A4.5 says why it does not affect what is measured.

A4.5  WHY THIS DOES NOT CHANGE WHAT IS MEASURED

A stationary point is a property of the potential energy surface. The coordinates an optimiser
walks in change the route and whether it arrives; they do not move the minimum or the saddle, and
they do not change the energy at it. What is measured here is a difference of four converged
energies, so it is coordinate-independent at convergence, and the reactant and TS sides using
different coordinates does not make their energies incomparable.

What holds the spheres still is NOT the coordinate system. STAGE6_CRITERIA.txt line 83 states it:
"ActiveAtoms = the substrate only; the spheres fixed in the force field". Removing coordsys
cartesian from the TS side leaves ActiveAtoms and the force field untouched, so the spheres stay
exactly as frozen as before. R4's phrase "Cartesian coordinates are how the frozen surroundings are
held" is corrected here: ActiveAtoms is how they are held.

The criteria's test on the relaxed transition state is UNCHANGED and is not weakened: "a TS that
does not return exactly one imaginary mode is reported as such and is not used for a relaxed
barrier". Under NumFreq that test is actually applied, where under the refused input no frequency
was computed at all.

ONE THING THE PRE-REGISTERED CHOICE COSTS, measured rather than argued: on WP4's own three arms,
the same systems from the same starting geometries, redundant internals converged in far fewer
cycles than Cartesians (wp4_results.tsv) -

      arma   30 cycles against 213, a factor of 7.10
      armb   25 cycles against 195, a factor of 7.80
      armc  130 cycles against 222, a factor of 1.71
      totals 185 against 630, a factor of 3.41

so the TS side's forced move to redundant internals is also the cheaper one. This is stated because
it must not be mistaken for the reason: the reason is that the pre-registered form does not run.


A4.6  DIRECTION

Nothing in this amendment is chosen by the barrier any design reaches, and nothing in it favours a
lower one. The coordinate system does not move the stationary point; the Hessian provision does not
change the energy at it; and the only criterion this touches - the imaginary mode count - becomes
testable where it previously was not. The geometries, the arrangement, the tolerance and its floor
were all fixed before any relaxation ran and are untouched here.

The one direction worth naming: redundant internals is the more PERMISSIVE choice in the sense that
a search can converge where the pre-registered form cannot start. That permissiveness is about
whether a number exists at all, not about which number it is.


A4.7  THE RESIDUAL RISK ON THE CONTROL, STATED BEFORE IT IS RUN

Hybrid_Hess is how ORCA names an exact-over-these-atoms Hessian when part of the system is
molecular mechanics. The control S6-C has no MM region: its 24 atoms are the whole system, and
18c_reduced_region/ts_reduced.out line 176, "Adapting HybridHessAtoms definition to QMMM optRegion",
is a remapping that cannot happen without a QM/MM block. Listing all 24 atoms therefore asks for
the full Hessian, which is what the control needs, and it keeps the two arms' %geom blocks
identical.

No committed run of this project uses Hybrid_Hess without a QM/MM block, so this one line of the
control's input has no precedent here. If ORCA refuses it, the fix is to delete that line from the
control's input ONLY. That is a no-op for the control, because the list names every atom it has and
Calc_Hess true alone computes the same full Hessian. It would be recorded as NOTE 1's further
entry, not applied quietly.


A4.8  WHAT THIS AMENDMENT CANNOT SETTLE

1. Whether either TS search converges, and whether what it reaches is a first-order saddle. The
   criteria's own test decides that and this amendment does not pre-empt it.
2. The imaginary mode count may not be one. 18c_reduced_region returned TWO on the Hessian computed
   on the way in, at 313.30 and 18.65 cm-1, and ONE at the stationary point, at 313.07 - its line
   3843 against its line 97306. Which block is read decides the answer; the reader takes the one
   after the stationary point. If two appear there it is reported, not resolved.
3. Nothing about the reactant side, which runs under the protocol as pre-registered.
4. Nothing about Loss's sign. STAGE6_LOSS_SIGN_FINDING_20261103.txt records a separate and
   unresolved problem in STAGE6_CRITERIA.txt lines 109 and 139, and this amendment does not touch
   it. A relaxation that runs is not the same thing as a tolerance that can fail.
5. Nothing about the catalytic verdict, which remains SP_AMENDMENT2.txt A2.5's chain: an optimised
   transition state with one counted imaginary mode, an IRC in both directions, the ends minimised,
   and B against the reference by the same chain in the same medium. No barrier is quoted here.
