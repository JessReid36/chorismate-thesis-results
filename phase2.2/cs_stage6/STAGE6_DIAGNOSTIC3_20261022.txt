Stage 6 - DIAGNOSTIC 3: does the designed charge need Pauli repulsion? Written 22 October 2026, after
DIAGNOSTIC2_RESULT.txt and before Stage 7's constraints are fixed.

THIS IS A DIAGNOSTIC AND NOT A METHOD CHANGE. STAGE6_CRITERIA.txt and STAGE5_CRITERIA.txt are untouched,
PHASE22_PLAN.txt section 2.2 stays in force, and no number these runs produce replaces a reported number or alters
a criterion. It exists because the answer decides whether the charge bound in Stage 7 is a measured property of the
substrate or a workaround for a defect in the charge model, and those are different things.

D3.1 WHY

DIAGNOSTIC2_RESULT.txt measured the response at site 1690 to a bare point charge, both signs, four magnitudes, five
conditions. At q <= 0.50 e every condition is internally consistent: even/q^2 is constant to between 0.6 and 4.7
per cent and odd/q to between 0.03 and 0.94 per cent. At q = 1.00 e, two of the five stop being consistent, and
they are the two with diffuse functions and no continuum:

   ma-def2-SVP in vacuo   even/q^2  -1.480 (q<=0.50)  ->  +0.972 (q=1.00)   sign inverted
                          ddE(+q)   -3.9781 at 0.50   ->  -3.8579 at 1.00   NON-MONOTONE: more charge, less effect
   def2-SVPD   in vacuo   even/q^2  -1.532            ->  -0.737            halved
                          odd/q     -7.4706           ->  -6.7768           down 9 per cent

def2-SVP shows none of it, and neither CPCM condition does.

IT MUST NOT BE CONCLUDED FROM THIS THAT def2-SVP IS CORRECT. It has no diffuse functions, so the substrate's
density has nowhere to leak, and it therefore cannot exhibit the effect whether or not the effect is real. The
diffuse sets are the more faithful description of an anion's density - Rienstra-Kiracofe et al., Chem. Rev. 2002,
102, 231, p. 244: "It is common knowledge that a proper description of the electronic structure of atomic and
molecular anions requires basis sets with diffuse functions."

The named explanation is a defect in the CHARGE MODEL, not in the basis. Marefat Khah, Reinholdt, Olsen, Kongsted
and Hattig, J. Chem. Theory Comput. 2020, doi:10.1021/acs.jctc.9b01162, held as acs.jctc.9b01162.pdf:

   "In most QM/MM approaches, the MM environment is described by classical mechanical potentials acting only on
    the nuclei, e.g., pair-wise 6-12 Lennard-Jones potentials. The missing effect of the Pauli repulsion on the
    electronic wavefunction leads to the so-called electron spill-out (ESO) problem. This problem manifests itself
    as an unphysical leaking of the electron density into the MM environment. The ESO diminishes the accuracy of
    the calculations and in some cases causes completely unphysical results; in particular, when diffuse basis
    functions are required to capture the physical character o[f the states]"

and their own symptom, in a different observable but the same shape as ours:

   "Some of the excitation energies that should be above 5 eV collapse with PE to unphysically low values if
    diffuse basis functions allow the electrons to spill out into the environment. This problem disappears if the
    ECPs are added."

This applies to us exactly, and STAGE6_FINDINGS_20261022.md Finding 4 is the proof that it applies: at a fixed
geometry the Lennard-Jones sphere and the bare point charge at this very site differ by exactly L, the classical
Lennard-Jones energy, with identical electronic deviation - "necessarily, since the Lennard-Jones term never
enters the QM Hamiltonian." Our charge sites therefore have a wall for nuclei and NO wall for electrons.

Two consequences worth stating plainly, because both change earlier reasoning in this project:

  (a) Capping |q| to stay below where the anomaly appears would be avoiding the regime in which the model is
      wrong, not fixing it. If the charge bound is to be lowered it must be because the SUBSTRATE cannot be
      described there, not because the charge model cannot.

  (b) PHASE22_PLAN.txt section 2.3 called replacing point charges with Gaussian-smeared charges "the main physical
      change", and CHARGE_MODEL_DECISIONS.md line 158 records that smearing is unavailable in ORCA, a loss this
      project has carried since 30 September. The same paper settles that it would not have helped here:
      "[smearing] can, in effect, lift the electrostatic singularities that occur at MM sites with point
      multipoles and provide a more accurate description of the continuous charge distribution in the shortrange
      including charge penetration effects. However, these methods do not capture the effects of the Pauli
      exchange repulsion on the electronic wavefunction." The missing piece was always Pauli repulsion.

D3.2 WHAT IS RUN

The published cure is a one-centre pseudopotential on the MM site, and the parameters are in that paper's
supporting information, held as ct9b01162_si_001.pdf, Tables S1 to S3 for He, Ne and Ar, "fitted to reproduce with
zero electrons the orbital energies" of the relevant shells - that is, pure repulsive potentials with no
electrons, which is exactly a Pauli wall. THEY ARE NOT USED HERE. Table S2 for Ne states ncore = 10 and lmax = 3
but then lists a d local block together with a d-f term, which is internally inconsistent in TURBOMOLE's ECP
format, and converting it to ORCA's NewECP form would require guessing which label is wrong. That is deferred to
its own step, with the parameters recorded here so the deferral is explicit and not a loss of the reference.

What is run instead needs no new capability and no guessed parameter: REPLACE THE POINT CHARGE WITH A REAL ION,
which has electrons and therefore has Pauli repulsion by construction.

   M0  bare substrate, charge -2, 118 electrons                        ALREADY COMMITTED (qscan C1/C5 bare_R, bare_TS)
   M1  substrate + a bare +1 POINT CHARGE at site 1690                 ALREADY COMMITTED (qscan C1/C5 qp1000_R, qp1000_TS)
   M2  substrate + Na+ as a QM ATOM at site 1690, charge -1, 128 e     NEW
   M3  substrate + K+  as a QM ATOM at site 1690, charge -1, 136 e     NEW
   M4  substrate + a GHOST Na at site 1690 (basis functions only, no
       nucleus, no electrons, no charge), charge -2, 118 e             NEW
   M5  substrate + a GHOST K at site 1690, same                       NEW

at both committed geometries and at both def2-SVP and def2-SVPD in vacuo: 16 new single points of a few minutes.
M0 and M1 are read out of the committed qscan outputs archive rather than re-run, so the comparison is against the
same numbers Diagnostic 2 reported.

   ddE(model) = [E_TS(model) - E_TS(bare)] - [E_R(model) - E_R(bare)], kcal/mol, each model against the bare
   substrate at the same basis. The ion's own energy cancels in the difference of differences.

   WHY TWO IONS. Na+ has an ionic radius of about 1.02 A and K+ about 1.38 A, against about 1.70 A for the van der
   Waals radius of a guanidinium carbon. Na+ therefore UNDER-represents the wall a real arginine presents, and K+
   less so. If the effect grows from Na+ to K+ that is the signature of Pauli repulsion and it tells us how far a
   point charge is from standing for a group of that size. Na+ alone would give only a lower bound.

   WHY THE GHOSTS. Adding an ion adds basis functions, and basis functions alone lower the substrate's energy
   differentially between R and TS. M4 and M5 carry the same basis functions with no nucleus, no electrons and no
   charge, so they measure that contribution on its own. Without them the comparison between M1 and M2 would
   confound Pauli repulsion with basis-set superposition.

   WHAT IS NOT RUN, and deliberately: a guanidinium fragment, which is the group the design is meant to stand for.
   It requires choosing a position AND an orientation, and that is a modelling decision to be taken deliberately
   rather than folded into a diagnostic. It is the natural next step if this one finds an effect.

D3.3 WHAT WOULD COUNT AS WHICH ANSWER, STATED BEFORE THEY RUN

The quantities are ddE(M1) through ddE(M5) at each basis, and the basis-corrected ion effect

   ion(X)  =  ddE(real ion X)  -  ddE(ghost X)

which is what the ion does beyond providing basis functions, against ddE(M1), what a bare point charge of the same
monopole does.

  THE GHOST CONTROL MUST PASS FIRST. If |ddE(ghost)| exceeds 0.30 kcal/mol at either basis, the basis-set
  contribution is not negligible and no comparison below is clean; the result is then reported as inconclusive
  with the ghost values given, and a counterpoise treatment is required before this question can be answered.

  THE POINT CHARGE IS AN ADEQUATE SURROGATE AT +1 e if |ion(Na) - ddE(M1)| and |ion(K) - ddE(M1)| are both under
  10 per cent of |ddE(M1)| at def2-SVPD. The q = 1.00 anomaly of Diagnostic 2 would then NOT be a Pauli-repulsion
  artefact, something else would be responsible, and the measured charge bound would stand as an empirical
  property of the substrate's response rather than as a defect of the charge model.

  THE POINT CHARGE IS NOT AN ADEQUATE SURROGATE AT +1 e if either differs by more than 10 per cent. The anomaly is
  then explained, the correct remedy is a Pauli wall for the electrons and not a lower charge bound, and Stage 7
  must not adopt the 0.25 e cap as a design constraint - it would be concealing a model defect behind a
  restriction on the design space.

  AND THE SIZE TEST, which is what bears on back-designing to residues: if |ion(K)| differs from |ion(Na)| by more
  than 10 per cent, the effect depends on the size of the wall. A single point charge then cannot represent a
  guanidinium at this distance at any magnitude, independently of what the barrier does, and the representation
  itself has to change rather than its parameters. If they agree within 10 per cent the effect is insensitive to
  the wall's size over this range, which would be evidence that a point charge plus a single transferable
  pseudopotential is enough.

No threshold here is new in kind; the 10 per cent is stricter than decision O6's 15 per cent method-and-basis
figure and is used as a yardstick for reporting, not as a criterion anything passes or fails. Nothing is accepted
or rejected on the strength of it.

D3.4 WHAT THIS DOES NOT TEST

Whether an ion is a good model of a residue - it is not, and D3.2 says why. The relaxed geometry, where a real ion
would move and a point charge would be approached; Finding 4 says the Lennard-Jones wall's entire value is there
and this diagnostic is at fixed geometry only. Any charge magnitude other than +1 e, since an ion's charge is not
adjustable. Negative charge, for the same reason. More than one site, and more than one frame.

DIRECTION

None. The outcome decides whether Stage 7's charge bound is a measured property of the substrate or a workaround,
and D3.3 fixes which before the runs go in.
