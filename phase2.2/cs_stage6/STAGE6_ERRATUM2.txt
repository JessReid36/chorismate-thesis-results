arrangements were generated and committed (results 307e129) and before any Stage 6 job was submitted.
STAGE6_CRITERIA.txt stays as committed (results b04a17b); this file corrects an arithmetic slip in its COST section
and records a change to a resource request that the criteria does not fix. NO DECISION, THRESHOLD, TOLERANCE,
CRITERION, ARRANGEMENT OR REPORTED QUANTITY CHANGES.

E6.4 THE COST, CORRECTED BY A FACTOR OF ABOUT EIGHT

STAGE6_CRITERIA.txt's COST section reads, as STAGE6_ERRATUM1.txt E6.3 already corrected its first sentence:

   "... Stage 6 as specified is 7 arrangements x 2 sides (R and TS) plus the frequency jobs, about 16-20 jobs of
   that size: of order 60-120 core-hours, requested as 8 cores and 24 GB each."

"60-120 core-hours" is wrong. It is the JOB-HOUR count, not the core-hour count, and the two differ by the 8 cores
each job requests. One job alone exceeds the stated budget for the whole stage: WP4's armb_freecart took 6 h 42 m on
8 cores, which is 53.6 core-hours.

The stage as generated (results 307e129, phase2.2/cs_stage6/relax/) is:

   7 arrangements x 2 geometries x 1 relaxation   14 jobs   about 6-7 h each    8 cores   about 750-780 core-hours
   7 arrangements x 2 geometries x 1 single point 14 jobs   about 5 min each     8 cores   about 9 core-hours
                                                  --------                                 ---------------------
                                                  28 jobs                                  about 760-790 core-hours

plus the frequency calculation that rides in each of the 7 TS relaxations, which armb_freecart did not run and whose
cost is therefore not measured here; a frequency job on 24 atoms at this level is of the same order as the
optimisation, so the honest figure is OF ORDER 800-1100 CORE-HOURS, or 100-140 job-hours at 8 cores.

The job count is also sharper than "16-20": it is 28, because every arrangement gets a frozen single point as well as
a relaxation at each geometry, and the frequency job shares the TS relaxation's submission rather than being its own.
Nothing follows from the correction except planning: no criterion, tolerance or threshold refers to the cost.

E6.5 THE WALLTIME THE SCRIPTS REQUEST

STAGE6_CRITERIA.txt fixes the request as "8 cores and 24 GB each" and says nothing about walltime. The first
generation of the inputs took WP4's job.pbs as its template and so asked for 168:00:00 for EVERY job, the frozen
single points included. That is wrong for them in the same way the SP package's memory request was wrong: PBS routes
by the resources asked for, so a job that runs for five minutes while asking for a week queues behind week-queue
work for no reason. SP_AMENDMENT3 is the precedent for correcting a resource request that no pre-registration fixes.

   the 14 frozen single points   168:00:00 -> 02:00:00
   the 14 relaxations            168:00:00, unchanged

02:00:00 is generous by a factor of about twenty: the A-matrix job ran 16 single points of this same 24-atom
substrate at this same level in about 75 minutes of wall time, so about 5 minutes each, and the Stage 6 single points
differ only by having MM spheres present, which cost essentially nothing. The relaxations keep WP4's own request
because armb_freecart, the run STAGE6_ERRATUM1.txt names as the protocol, took 6 h 42 m and a design with more
spheres may need more cycles than one sphere did; a shorter request would risk a job being killed for the sake of
queue position, which is the wrong trade for a six-hour job.

The 8 cores and 24 GB are NOT changed. Both are what the criteria fixes and both are measured rather than guessed:
armb_freecart ran this exact job on 8 cores with 24 GB.

E6.6 THE INPUTS ARE PATCHED, NOT REGENERATED, AND WHY THAT IS THE CAREFUL CHOICE

The obvious way to apply E6.5 is to amend cs6_arrange.py and regenerate. That was tried first and it was WRONG, for
a reason worth recording because it applies to any later regeneration of an optimiser output.

Regenerating re-runs cs_opt_milp.py. Its "auto" strategy gives the full search a 120 s probe and falls back to the
LP-guided search if the full search has not finished in that time (OPTIMISER_DECISIONS_20261020.md and the code's
own notes: the fallback exists so the search adapts to the machine it is on). The DESIGN is the same either way -
on a check of the regenerated output against the committed one, every site, charge, a, L and score of all seven
arrangements was identical, as were every combined.xyz, every site.ORCAFF.prms and every job.inp - but what the
record SAYS about how the design was found is not:

   committed (results 307e129)   S6-10   strategy full   certified gap 0.00000
   a later run on the same files S6-10   strategy lp     certified gap 0.21594

Both are valid. "full" means the search closed the gap itself and the design is provably the best n = 10 design
this score and these rules admit anywhere on the grid; "lp" means only that the design is within 0.216 kcal/mol of
it. The committed line is the stronger claim AND it is the one that was actually proved. Regenerating would replace
a proved record with a weaker one for no reason except that the second run was slower, which is exactly the kind of
drift a pre-registered record exists to prevent.

So the 14 single-point job.pbs are PATCHED in place, by patch_walltime.py, and ARRANGEMENTS.txt is not touched. It
records the sha256 of the script that generated the arrangements, and that script - c51d160250989479... - is the one
that generated them; the amended script changes only the walltime line it writes.

WHAT MUST NOT HAVE CHANGED, stated before the patch runs, every item a stop:
  - exactly 14 files change, all of them */[RT]*_sp/job.pbs, each in exactly one line, its walltime line;
  - each patched file equals byte for byte what the amended cs6_arrange.py's own job_pbs_text would write for it,
    so the patch and the generator agree and neither is taken on trust;
  - every combined.xyz, every site.ORCAFF.prms, every job.inp, all 14 relaxation job.pbs and ARRANGEMENTS.txt are
    byte-identical, re-checked by sha256 after the patch;
  - INPUT_CHECKSUMS.sha256 is updated for exactly those 14 entries in its existing order, and every other entry is
    re-verified against the file on disk.

E6.7 A LIMITATION THIS UNCOVERED, RECORDED FOR STAGE 5 PRODUCTION

cs_opt_milp.py's recorded "strategy" and "certified_gap" are not reproducible across runs, because the fallback is
decided by a wall-clock probe. The design is reproducible; the account of how it was reached is not. Nothing in
Stage 6 depends on it - the gap is reported, not used in any criterion, and Stage 6 is not regenerating - but a
production run whose certified gaps are a reported result should fix the strategy explicitly rather than leave it
to "auto", or record the probe and the machine alongside the gap. This is a limitation of the record, not of any
design, and no result reported anywhere changes because of it.

DIRECTION

None. The arrangements, the charges, the sites, the protocol and every measured quantity are untouched. This file
corrects a cost figure that nothing depends on and an unregistered resource request that would have cost queue time.
