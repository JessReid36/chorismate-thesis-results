1. WHAT HAPPENED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Two jobs, one per leg, submitted 13 November. Both ended on 14 November with

   Exit_status = 1

and not with the scheduler's walltime message. Neither reached its walltime: 168 hours
were granted and 17 and 11 hours were used. From the PBS accounting and the archived
output:

   leg    node      walltime     cput        mem used    vmem        ended
   B_R    comp064   17:09:40     101:01:24   159.2 GB    171.0 GB    Sat 07:17:51
   B_TS   comp034   11:01:17      80:41:56   166.9 GB    178.7 GB    Sat 07:16:15

   B_R  started Fri 14:06:32, last wrote job.out at 05:50
   B_TS started Fri 20:13:18, last wrote job.out at 05:41

THE TWO ENDS ARE 96 SECONDS APART, ON DIFFERENT NODES. Two independent jobs on two
different machines, started six hours apart, do not stop within a minute and a half of
each other for a reason local to either. What they share is the filesystem they were
writing to.

Each leg stopped writing output roughly ninety minutes before it died, and neither
left a message in job.out. B_TS's last recorded CCSD iteration times, in seconds:

   iteration 11      413.98
   iteration 12      521.18
   iteration 13     5042.88
   iteration 14     6983.53

The step from 12 to 13 is a factor of 9.7. Nothing in the chemistry changes between
two CCSD iterations; nothing in the arithmetic changes. What changed is how long it
took to read the integrals back.

NONE OF THIS IS COMMITTED AS A RESULT. A failed job is not committed; a note about it
is, and this amendment is that note. The archived output lives outside git at
chorismate-hpc-archive/d36a1_attempt2_io, eleven files with a sha256 for each.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. WHY IT FAILED. THE SWITCH WAS NEVER SET
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Attempt 2's whole purpose was to stop ORCA writing PNO integrals to disk. It did not
work, and the reason is that %maxcore is not the control for it. Attempt 2 reported,
in its own output, the same line attempt 1 reported:

   PNO-Integral Storage                    ... ON DISK

192 GB of %maxcore across 16 cores made no difference, because %maxcore governs how
much memory each process may use for its own working arrays and not where the DLPNO
integrals are held. The control for that is a separate keyword, documented in the
ORCA 6.0 manual under MDCI, section 7.14.4:

   %mdci
        StorageType Shared
   end

and the manual's own words for what it does and what it costs:

   "For larger systems and tighter thresholds the disk I/O of a DLPNO calculation may
    become challenging."
   "In this case, it might be usefull to keep some integrals in memory, if enough RAM
    is available."
   "ORCA will try to store certain much-used integrals in shared memory."
   "If the amount of memory is not sufficient, ORCA will fall back to on-disk storage."
   "NOTE: This flag will only work if all processes work on the same node."

   source: https://www.faccts.de/docs/orca/6.0/manual/contents/detailed/mdci.html
   read 14 November 2026

That block was absent from attempts 1 and 2. This is my error and not the cluster's:
attempt 2 was built on the assumption that %maxcore would move the integrals into
memory, the assumption was never checked against the manual, and a week of two jobs
was spent on it.

SO THE INTEGRALS WENT TO DISK, AND THE DISK WAS THE WRONG ONE. Both jobs ran with
their working directory under /home, so that is where the intermediates went:

   B_R    74 GB
   B_TS   42 GB
   total 116 GB

Those three figures were measured by du on hpc1 on 14 November, while the two run
directories still held their intermediates. They are not in the archive, because what
was archived is the text output and not 116 GB of regenerable scratch, and they cannot
be re-read once d36a1's scratch is cleared.

/home on this cluster is NFS exported from comp047, shared by every user, and it was
already 90 per cent full when attempt 2 started. Measured on 14 November:

   62 TB total, 60 TB used, 2.4 TB available, 97 per cent

116 GB of coupled-cluster intermediates, read and rewritten on every CCSD iteration,
travelling over a network filesystem that is nearly full and shared with everyone
else. That is what the factor of 9.7 is. That is why two jobs on two nodes stopped
within 96 seconds of each other.

WHAT IS NOT CLAIMED HERE. I cannot show the exact error the filesystem returned,
because neither job wrote one: both went quiet about ninety minutes before exit and
the last thing in each job.out is an ordinary line of ORCA output. The sequence above
is read from the PBS accounting, the two output files and df. That a shared NFS volume
at 97 per cent is what ended both jobs is the reading those measurements support; it is
not a message either job printed.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. WHAT CHANGES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
                           attempt 2                 attempt 3
   working directory       /home/.../d36a1/B_x       /scratch on the compute node
   %mdci StorageType       not set                   Shared
   %maxcore                12000                     8000
   PBS ncpus               16                        16, unchanged
   PBS mem                 240gb                     240gb, unchanged
   walltime                168:00:00                 168:00:00, unchanged
   job layout              one job per leg           unchanged

TWO INDEPENDENT FIXES, AND ATTEMPT 3 DOES NOT DEPEND ON EITHER ONE WORKING ALONE.

FIRST, THE INTEGRALS ARE ASKED TO STAY IN MEMORY. The %mdci block above is added.
The manual's note that it works only if all processes are on one node is satisfied:
the request is select=1 and has been since attempt 2.

SECOND, IF THEY DO NOT FIT, THEY GO TO LOCAL DISK AND NOT TO NFS. The job now stages
its input into /scratch on the compute node, runs there, and copies the output back.
Measured on comp050 on 14 November:

   /scratch        1.9 TB total, 1.5 TB available, writable
   $TMPDIR          70 GB total,  38 GB available

/scratch is the volume to use and $TMPDIR is not: 38 GB does not hold 74 GB. This is
the structural point of attempt 3. The manual says ORCA falls back to on-disk storage
when memory is short, so a fallback must be assumed rather than hoped against; after
this change a fallback writes 74 GB to a local disk with 1.5 TB free instead of to a
shared network volume at 97 per cent. ON DISK IS NO LONGER A FAILURE. If attempt 3
reports ON DISK and finishes, that is a success and the result will say so.

WHY %maxcore GOES DOWN AND NOT UP. The manual does not say whether the shared segment
is counted inside %maxcore or allocated on top of it. Attempt 2 already used 159 to 167
GB of its 240 GB request without any shared segment at all. If the segment is allocated
on top, 192 GB of %maxcore plus a shared segment would exceed the 240 GB PBS limit and
the job would be killed for memory, which is a worse outcome than a disk fallback
because it looks exactly like the failure we have just spent a week diagnosing. 8000
on 16 cores is 128 GB and leaves roughly 110 GB of the request for the segment. If
instead the segment is counted inside %maxcore, 128 GB is still far more than the 40 GB
attempt 1 ran in. Either way the job completes; this choice trades a little speed for
not being killed. The PBS memory request is deliberately not raised: this is a shared
cluster and 240 GB is what attempt 2 already held.

THREE GUARDS, EACH ONE FROM SOMETHING THAT WENT WRONG.

   (a) The job checks free space on /scratch before it starts and exits immediately
       with the node name and the figure if there is less than 300 GB, rather than
       discovering it seventeen hours in. 300 GB is three times what attempt 2 wrote
       in its single largest leg plus headroom. A node that fails this check is not a
       reason to change anything: resubmit, and PBS will usually place the job
       elsewhere.

   (b) The storage mode is written to a file on /home within the first minutes, so
       ON DISK or IN MEMORY is visible while the job runs instead of in the
       post-mortem. Attempt 2 printed that line too; nobody could see it until the
       job was dead.

   (c) job.out is copied back to /home every ten minutes, not only at the end. Both
       dead jobs left their last ninety minutes unrecorded, and B_TS's ballooning
       iteration times were the one real warning either job gave.

The runs go in a new directory, d36a2, so d36a1's committed inputs stay exactly as
they are and remain the record of what attempt 2 tried.

4. WHAT DOES NOT CHANGE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The method line is untouched, byte for byte:

   ! DLPNO-CCSD(T) def2-TZVPD AutoAux TightSCF TightPNO

TightPNO stays. AutoAux stays. def2-TZVPD stays. The charge, the multiplicity and the
geometry stay, byte for byte from d33a/sp_42436_{R,TS}/substrate.xyz.

%mdci StorageType and %maxcore are storage directives. They decide where numbers are
held while they are being used. They do not decide what the numbers are, and G36.8
below is the control that proves it rather than the assertion that it is so.

Arms C and H are NOT re-run. They finished, they are valid, and their outputs on hpc1
are the ones the harvest will read. The harvest script must now read arm B from d36a2
and arms C and H from d36; that is a change to the harvest and not to this benchmark.

Every reading R36.1 to R36.5, every gate G36.1 to G36.10 and every limitation D36.1 to
D36.8 stands unchanged.

6. WHAT THIS AMENDMENT DOES NOT DO
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A36.6  It does not adopt a production level of theory. D36 supplies evidence for that
       amendment; it is not that amendment. A36.1 is unchanged.
A36.7  It does not make the benchmark more likely to favour any functional. Nothing
       changed can move the number, and G36.8 is the test of that claim.
A36.8  It does not add the def2-SVPD CCSD(T) pair alongside, and it does not add an
       F12 pair. Both were considered and dropped: this is a shared cluster, the
       footprint of two more sixteen-core jobs is not justified before the first
       number exists, and running a cheaper basis in parallel would not tell us
       whether the expensive one can finish. If attempt 3 fails as well, def2-SVPD
       becomes the plan and the reason for it will be a measurement rather than a
       guess.
A36.9  It does not promise attempt 3 will finish.
A36.10 It does not change the harvest. y87 is superseded because arm B has moved
       directory; rebuilding it is a separate step and is not licensed by this
       document.

ON AMENDMENT 1'S A36.3. Amendment 1 said that if attempt 2 failed, the next step was a
method change needing its own amendment. Attempt 3 is not a method change, and the
reason is section 2: attempt 2 did not fail because the method was too expensive, it
failed because the switch that was supposed to keep its integrals out of a near-full
network filesystem was never set. Spending the method on a fault in the job script
would be the wrong trade and would also corrupt the benchmark. A36.3 is therefore not
followed, deliberately and on the record. IT APPLIES TO ATTEMPT 4. If attempt 3 fails,
the next amendment is a method change - loosening TightPNO, or dropping the (T), or
def2-SVPD - and none of the three is chosen here in advance.

7. A CORRECTION TO AMENDMENT 1
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Amendment 1, section 2, says of the compute nodes:

   "Node-local /scratch is 50 GB with 27 GB free and so cannot hold them either"

THAT IS WRONG AND THE ERROR IS MINE. 50 GB is the /scratch volume on the login node.
On a compute node /scratch is a different filesystem: 1.9 TB total with 1.5 TB
available, measured on comp050 on 14 November by a job submitted for the purpose.
Amendment 1 is not edited; this paragraph is the correction, and the sentence quoted
above should not be relied on by anyone reading that document.

The consequence of the error is not small. Had the figure been right, attempt 2 would
have run in /scratch and the 116 GB would never have gone near /home. The claim was
made from the login node, which is the only place I had looked, and was not checked on
a compute node before it was written down. "I could not find it" would have been the
honest form; "it cannot hold them" was not.
