Stage 6, DIAGNOSTIC 19, NOTE 1 (2026-10-31).

Two defects in how DIAGNOSTIC 19's result was READ and INVENTORIED. No run is repeated and no
input changes. DIAGNOSTIC19_RESULT.txt is NOT edited: it stands as the record of what was
reported, and this note is the entry a reader of it should be sent to.

NOTHING IN THE PRE-REGISTRATION CHANGES. D19.3's criteria are untouched and every verdict it
fixed in advance is the same before and after the correction. That is the test this note had to
pass to be a note rather than an erratum, and the script that wrote it refuses to continue if
any verdict moves:

   VERDICT: WEAK OR SPLIT
   VERDICT: UNSTABLE AT FULL FIELD
   VERDICT: DISAGREE


N1.1  THE STABILITY READER REPORTED ENERGIES AS EIGENVALUES

cs6_d19.read_stab, as committed at 3ffca3f, searched a 4000-character window starting at the
first case-insensitive match of "stability analysis" and took the most negative float in that
window to be the lowest eigenvalue of the stability matrix. The window runs well past the
stability block into the SCF and energy sections, so the number it reported was a total energy.
DIAGNOSTIC19_RESULT.txt therefore contains

   stab_lam000_R   STABLE    lowest eigenvalue -92671.056490
   stab_lam000_TS  STABLE    lowest eigenvalue -92646.794150
   stab_lam100_R   UNSTABLE  lowest eigenvalue -836.087135
   stab_lam100_TS  UNSTABLE  lowest eigenvalue -836.099053

and none of those four numbers is an eigenvalue. -836.0871 and -836.0991 are that run's own
total energy.

The verdicts beside them are nevertheless correct, and they are correct by luck rather than by
construction: the window happened to contain ORCA's verdict sentence, and the text match on it
happened to be unambiguous. A reader that can report a total energy as an eigenvalue is not one
whose verdicts should be trusted without checking, and all four were checked by hand against the
raw output before this note was written.

WHAT ORCA ACTUALLY PRINTS, per analysis:

   -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------
                                  WAVEFUNCTION STABILITY ANALYSIS
   -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   ...
   The eigenvalues of the stability matrix:
      E( 0) =   0.05617271 Eh
      E( 1) =   0.09119369 Eh
      E( 2) =   0.09759674 Eh

   The stability analysis shows that the wavefunction is stable

or, where an instability is found,

   The stability analysis indicates that the wavefunction is unstable
   Restart requested: Orbitals will be transformed and reconvergence attempted

THE SECOND OMISSION IS THE LARGER ONE. Because the old reader looked only for a verdict and a
float, it never noticed that ORCA performs the analysis AGAIN after reconverging. Both lambda =
1.00 runs have TWO analyses, and DIAGNOSTIC19_RESULT.txt reports neither the first one's
eigenvalues nor the second one's existence.

THE CORRECTED READING, generated by the corrected reader from the same committed outputs:

   GATE 1, the zero-field stability controls
      stab_lam000_R   analysis 1 of 1  STABLE    E(0..2) +0.05617271  +0.09119369  +0.09759674
      stab_lam000_TS  analysis 1 of 1  STABLE    E(0..2) +0.01728066  +0.04008551  +0.05376300
      GATE 1 passed.

   READING 2, is the production wavefunction stable at full field?
      The verdict is on the FIRST analysis, which is the solution the production runs use:

      stab_lam100_R   analysis 1 of 2  UNSTABLE  E(0..2) -0.01083954  -0.00844369  +0.00317256

         total energy -836.093032827 -> -836.099084675 Eh  (-3.7976 kcal/mol)
      VERDICT: UNSTABLE AT FULL FIELD

At full field the restricted solution carries TWO negative eigenvalues in each state. It is a
saddle point, not a minimum. At zero field every eigenvalue is positive in both states, which is
what GATE 1 was for and why it is reported first.

WHAT THIS NOTE DOES NOT DO. D19.3 states of the restart that the reconverged energy "is not the
production energy, is not compared with one, and is used for nothing but this verdict". This
note obeys that sentence. It does not compare the reconverged energy with any committed
production energy, it draws no barrier from it, and it makes no claim about what the existence
of a lower stable solution implies. Those questions are larger than a note and belong to a
diagnostic pre-registered for them.


N1.2  INVENTORY.tsv FILES 58 SCRATCH FILES AS INPUTS

The stability analysis writes job.grho_cis*.tmp, job.fxc_cis*.tmp and job.C1.tmp beside each
stab run. y37's categoriser knows .gbw, .densities, .densitiesinfo and .bibtex as
PC-archive-only, and job.out, job.property.txt and *.pbs.out as committed text, and files
everything else as committed_input. These 58 files are neither inputs nor committed: this
repository's .gitignore excludes *.tmp and git ls-files finds none of them. They are in the PC
archive, with their sha256 in HPC_MANIFEST.sha256, and their category should read pc_only.

The error is confined to the category column of INVENTORY.tsv. Every one of the 58 files is
archived and checksummed, the archive's member count and sha256 in PC_ARCHIVES.tsv are right,
and OUTPUTS.sha256 covers exactly the committed text outputs. Nothing is missing from the record;
one column in it is wrong.

THE CATEGORISER IS NOT UNDER VERSION CONTROL. y35 and y37 were delivered as bundles and were
never committed to the code repository, so unlike cs6_d19.py there is no committed file to
correct. That is itself a gap in the record and is stated here rather than left implicit.


N1.3  WHAT IS DONE ABOUT IT

cs6_d19.py's read_stab is corrected in the commit this note accompanies, and the corrected
reading is reproduced above from the committed outputs. DIAGNOSTIC19_RESULT.txt and
INVENTORY.tsv are left exactly as committed. Rewriting either would alter a record this project
relies on being unaltered, which is a worse fault than the one it fixes.

A reader who finds "lowest eigenvalue -92671.056490" in DIAGNOSTIC19_RESULT.txt, or a .tmp file
filed as committed_input in INVENTORY.tsv, should be sent here.
