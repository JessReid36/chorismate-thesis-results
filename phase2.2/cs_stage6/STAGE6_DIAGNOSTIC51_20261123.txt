STAGE 6 - DIAGNOSTIC 51 - PRE-REGISTRATION
The calibration of D50 done again, with the integration grid measured
================================================================================
Written 23 November 2026, after D50's result (results commit bd2095f5) and D50 NOTE 1 (results
commit 6e0f1c42), and before any run of D51. Claude told Jessica that morning that D50's word NONE
had two causes and that its own rule was badly made, and sketched what should come next. Jessica,
23 November 2026, 09:53: "okay yes and also adress those other issues you listed ie the poorly
designed test and none issue". Claude then looked into it (section 1) and proposed this diagnostic
as the first of two steps. Jessica, 10:25: "yes draft." No committed file used the number 51 for a
diagnostic.

It adopts no rule and opens or closes no size, no distance and no design. D50's result stands as it
was committed, its word NONE included. AMENDMENT 9, A9.4 and the results of D44, D47, D48, D49 and
D50 stand whatever this shows.

THIS DOCUMENT, ITS RULE AND WHAT IT EXPECTS WERE WRITTEN WITH D50'S RESULT KNOWN, and after Claude
had computed the same calibration in a second program. Section 7 gives what is expected, in
numbers. What D51 adds is the measurement in ORCA, the program that the runs of the dianion use.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. WHY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT D50 SAID. Its rule (R50.3) asked that Hartree-Fock and the functional each hold one electron
at a bare charge within 0.05 eV of the exact value, at every size from 0.25 e up. Its result:
"THE FUNCTIONS FOR THE CAPTURE TEST: NONE. No set has a treatment that is adequate at every size
from 0.25 e up." Its pre-registration had fixed what follows: "THE RULE GIVES NONE: there are no
functions for the test. The result shows whether the functions fall short (HF) or the way the
functional is computed does (CAM against HF: the auxiliary basis or the integration grid), and
the next step mends that one."

WHAT THE RESULT SHOWS OF THAT. The functions do not fall short: Hartree-Fock lies within 0.008 eV
of exact from 0.25 e up in set X2 (R50.2). The auxiliary basis is not it: treatments A and E, with
74 and 47 auxiliary functions, give the same value to 0.00002 eV at all twenty sizes
(d50_calibration.tsv). The grid was not varied in D50. Claude's report of the result to Jessica
said so and read what remained as the functional's own error. That was half of it.

WHAT WAS READ AFTERWARDS IN D50'S COMMITTED OUTPUTS (DERIVED, post hoc; D50's reader did not read
this line). ORCA prints how many electrons its integration grid of the functional adds up to,
N(Total). There is one electron. With treatment A:

   q, e      0.10    0.15    0.20    0.25    0.30    0.40    0.50    0.60    0.75    1.00
   set X2    0.7957  0.8405  0.8831  0.9203  0.9498  0.9848  0.9970  0.9998  1.0001  1.0000
   set X3    0.5691  0.7231  0.8332  0.9021  0.9441  0.9845  0.9970  0.9998  1.0001  1.0000

Treatment E prints the same counts. So on a lone ghost atom ORCA's default grid (DefGrid2: 3586
points on that atom) does not reach the outer part of so diffuse an electron below about 0.5 e. In
D49, with the def2-TZVPD functions alone, every count is 1.0000.

In D50's runs of the dianion, where the grids of 24 atoms and of the ghost atoms overlap, the
count falls short of 118 electrons by 0.0002 to 0.0005 in 22 outputs, the plain controls among
them, and by 0.0082 in the two runs that took electrons (B_n11_q0.25 at R, treatments A and E).

WHAT CLAUDE COMPUTED (in PySCF 2.15.1, a second program; exact integrals, no RI and no COSX; a
grid that counts the whole electron. COMPUTED BY CLAUDE: not ORCA, not a measurement and not part
of the record. The script is cs6_d51_expect_pyscf.py, its numbers cs6_d51_expected.py, both
committed with this document.) Its Hartree-Fock values are ORCA's committed ones to 0.000005 eV at
all twenty (set, size). With the functional, B in eV:

                    itself minus exact  minus the full-grid value  minus the full-grid value
   0.10   0.1361    +0.0320             -0.1079                    -0.1646
   0.15   0.3061    +0.0402             -0.0813                    -0.1150
   0.20   0.5442    +0.0472             -0.0568                    -0.0729

   0.75   7.6532    +0.0312             +0.0000                    +0.0000

So D50's word has two causes, and they have opposite signs.
   THE FUNCTIONAL. CAM-B3LYP itself holds one electron at a bare charge 0.05 to 0.06 eV too
   strongly from 0.20 to 0.60 e, in a complete basis on a full grid. No functions and no grid
   change that. This is what D50's rule met at 0.40, 0.50 and 0.60 e, where ORCA's values are
   right to 0.006 eV.
   THE GRID. Below 0.40 e ORCA's values are too low, by 0.02 to 0.04 eV at 0.25 and 0.30 e,
   because part of the electron is not counted. D50's word ADEQUATE at 0.20, 0.25 and 0.30 e came
   from the two errors cancelling. With the full-grid values D50's own rule would have read
   NOT ADEQUATE at 0.30 e as well.

A model of ORCA's radial grid, built as ORCA's manual describes it and fitted to ONE number (the
count of set X2 at 0.25 e), gives back D50's twenty energies with treatment A to 0.0005 or 0.0013
eV at worst (two models, section 7) and its twenty counts to 0.025. So the grid of the functional
accounts for what D50's outputs print. The manual (ORCA 6.0, section 7.3, read on the web on 23
November 2026: a pointer, not part of the record) gives the number of radial points of an atom as
15 x IntAcc - 40 + b x ROW; IntAcc is 4.388 for the default grid and 4.959 for DefGrid3.

WHAT WAS WRONG WITH D50'S RULE. It held one number, the functional's value in the set, against the
exact value, and so asked three things at once: whether the functions can hold the electron,
whether ORCA's grid adds the functional up rightly, and how far the functional itself lies from
exact. The third is a property of the working level that no functions mend, and the second was
not looked at. Claude wrote that rule. D51 reads the three apart (section 3).

WHY ONE ELECTRON ONLY, AND NO RUN OF THE DIANION HERE. The pre-registration of D50 sends its two
outcomes to two things: this one, and "The next diagnostic then puts charges together first". That
one is some sixty runs of the dianion, ten minutes to an hour each. They should not rest on a grid
setting that ORCA has not been seen to take, or on functions whose word is NONE. D51 is minutes of
one job and settles both first.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. WHAT IS RUN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
200 runs in one job (K1), written by cs6_d51_prepare.py from committed files through the committed
cs6_d50_prepare.py. Every run: one electron, a ghost hydrogen atom 0.01 A from a bare point charge
q, carrying set X2 (def2-TZVPD hydrogen and an s and a p function at each of 0.035 and 0.012; 17
functions) or set X3 (also 0.004; 21 functions), at the ten sizes of D50: 0.10, 0.15, 0.20, 0.25,
0.30, 0.40, 0.50, 0.60, 0.75 and 1.00 e. For each set and size:

   HF     D50's committed Hartree-Fock input, byte for byte.
   g2     D50's committed input with the functional and auxiliary treatment A, byte for byte.
          ORCA's default grid.
   g3     g2 with the keyword DefGrid3.
   i08    g2 with IntAcc 8 for the grid of the functional.
   i12    the same with IntAcc 12.
   i20    the same with IntAcc 20.
   i40    the same with IntAcc 40.
   c40    i40 with the three grids of the exchange (COSX) at IntAcc 40 as well.
   n2     the functional with no RI and no COSX (the keyword NORI in place of def2/J RIJCOSX, and
          no auxiliary block under the ghost atom); ORCA's default grid.
   n40    n2 with IntAcc 40.

THE LADDER is g2, g3, i08, i12, i20, i40: the same run with more and more radial points in the
grid of the functional and nothing else changed. c40, n2 and n40 stand beside it.

Each input is D50's with at most one line changed and one block added. The block, here for c40,
stands before the line of the point charges:

      %method
        IntAcc 40.0
        IntAccX 40.0, 40.0, 40.0
      end

and the first line of n40 is

      ! UKS CAM-B3LYP def2-TZVPD NORI TightSCF

The prepare script refuses unless what it forms without a change is the committed input of D50 for
all forty (set, size, HF or g2). Treatments E and J are not run again: E equals A (above), and J
is NOT ADEQUATE at 0.25 e in D50 for a reason of its own. No run of the dianion is made.

Whether ORCA takes IntAcc 40, three values of IntAccX, or NORI with these inputs has not been
tried where this was written. A form it refuses fails in seconds and is reported.

THE JOB is D48's committed run_E1.pbs with its names and its runs changed and nothing else, as
D50's run_K1.pbs was: 8 cores and 48 GB asked, each run stopped after 900 seconds. HOW LONG, AN
ESTIMATE: D50's 80 runs of this kind took 8 minutes (its committed log). 200 runs, some on grids
some twenty times larger: under an hour.

IF THE JOB IS CUT SHORT it is collected as it stands, and the runs that did not finish are NOT
MEASURED. It is not submitted again: a run made a second time would start from the orbitals its
first attempt left behind and not from its own starting guess (D50 NOTE 1, N1.6).

3. THE READINGS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
27.211386245988 eV per hartree, 13.605693122994 eV per Rydberg.

A RUN IS MEASURED if the committed reader of outputs, cs6_d40.py, reads it as D50's reader read
its runs of one electron: it ends normally, its SCF converged, it reports one electron, one final
energy and one point charge. Otherwise it is NOT MEASURED, or DID NOT CONVERGE. A rung that asks
for an IntAcc is NOT AS ASKED, and is not used, if the IntAcc ORCA prints for that grid is not the
one asked for to 0.0015.

B is minus the energy of the one electron, eV. Exact, in a complete basis, is 13.605693122994 q^2.
THE COUNT of a run is the last N(Total) ORCA prints: the electrons its grid of the functional adds
up to. It should be 1.

R51.1  THE RUNS. For Hartree-Fock and for each rung: how many of its 20 runs are measured, the
       IntAcc ORCA prints, the number of grid points; every run that is not measured, and why.
R51.2  DOES THE CLUSTER GIVE D50'S NUMBERS AGAIN? The 20 Hartree-Fock runs and the 20 runs of
       rung g2 are D50's inputs byte for byte. Each energy against the committed energy of the
       same input in d50: THE SAME if within 0.000001 hartree. If all 40 are the same the rule
       below is applied. If not, the result says so, prints the ladder, and applies no rule.
R51.3  THE LADDER. For each set and size: the count and B on each rung, with what Claude's two
       models of the grid gave beforehand and the full-grid value set beside them.
R51.4  THE GRID AT EACH SIZE. THE TOP is rung i40 if it is measured, otherwise i20. THE NEXT is
       the rung of the ladder below the top. CONVERGED if both are measured and their B agree to
       0.005 eV; NOT CONVERGED if they do not; not decided otherwise. THE LOWEST RUNG THAT SERVES:
       the first rung of the ladder whose B, and that of every rung above it up to the top, lies
       within 0.005 eV of the top one.
R51.5  THE FUNCTIONS. For each set and size: Hartree-Fock against exact; ADEQUATE if within 0.05
       eV. Hartree-Fock is exact for one electron and uses no grid, so this reads the functions
       alone. It is D50's own test for Hartree-Fock.
R51.6  THE FUNCTIONS FOR THE FUNCTIONAL. Where the grid is CONVERGED in both sets: B with the
       functional in set X2 minus B in set X3, each on its top rung. HOLDS if within 0.05 eV;
       DOES NOT HOLD if not; not decided where a grid is not converged.
R51.7  THE RULE. Set X2 is ADEQUATE at a size if R51.2 gave D50's energies again, its
       Hartree-Fock value is ADEQUATE (R51.5) and the functional HOLDS (R51.6). NOT ADEQUATE if
       Hartree-Fock is NOT ADEQUATE or the functional DOES NOT HOLD. Otherwise not decided.
          THE FUNCTIONS FOR THE CAPTURE TEST: SET X2 with auxiliary treatment A if X2 is ADEQUATE
          at every size from 0.25 e up; NONE if it is NOT ADEQUATE at one of them; NOT DECIDED
          otherwise.
          THE GRID FOR A GHOST ATOM: the highest of the lowest rungs that serve (R51.4) over the
          sizes from 0.25 e up in set X2; none of the ladder if at one of them no rung serves.
R51.8  THE FUNCTIONAL AGAINST EXACT, reported and not part of the rule: B in set X3 on rung n40
       and on the top rung, minus exact, at each size, with what Claude computed for a complete
       basis beside it.
R51.9  WHAT RI AND COSX ADD: i40 minus n40, c40 minus i40 and g2 minus n2 at each set and size.

The tolerances. 0.05 eV is D50's. 0.005 eV, for the grid, is a tenth of it. Set beside the
smallest B the rule uses, 0.85 eV at 0.25 e, they are 6 per cent and 0.6 per cent.

WHY THE GRID IS READ BY B AND NOT BY THE COUNT. The far tail of the electron adds little to the
energy. By Claude's models a count within 0.001 of 1 is not reached at 0.25 e in set X3 on any
rung here, while B on the two highest rungs agrees to 0.003 eV. The count is printed beside every
B so that both can be seen.

5. WHAT EACH OUTCOME SENDS THE NEXT STEP TO, DECIDED NOW
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
D51 adopts nothing. D50's result stands whatever it shows.
   D50'S ENERGIES ARE NOT GIVEN AGAIN (R51.2): something in ORCA or on hpc1 is not as it was on
      22 November. Nothing is built on the ladder before that is understood.
   ORCA REFUSES A RUNG: reported. If i40 is refused the top is i20. If i20 is refused too, the
      grid is not decided at any size and the rule gives NOT DECIDED; the grid is then extended
      another way, under a short pre-registration of its own.
   THE GRID IS NOT CONVERGED at a size from 0.25 e up: IntAcc does not reach far enough on a lone
      ghost atom. The same: another way, under its own pre-registration. No run of the dianion is
      built on a rung of this ladder.
   THE RULE GIVES SET X2 AND A RUNG: the next diagnostic places set X2 with treatment A at each
      positive charge and gives each ghost atom the IntAcc of that rung. That the setting serves
      on the dianion is not shown here: the next pre-registration reads the count of every run
      of the dianion and says beforehand how far it may lie from the number of electrons.
   THE RULE GIVES NONE BY THE FUNCTIONS (Hartree-Fock NOT ADEQUATE at a size from 0.25 e up): it
      contradicts D50's Hartree-Fock runs of the same inputs, so R51.2 will have failed first.
   THE RULE GIVES NONE BECAUSE THE FUNCTIONAL DOES NOT HOLD IN X2 WHAT IT HOLDS IN X3: set X2 is
      too small for this functional at that size, and X3 has no larger set beside it here. A
      larger set is then measured before any run of the dianion.
   THE FUNCTIONAL AGAINST EXACT (R51.8): reported. Whatever it is, it is the working level's own
      error for one electron at one centre and not a fault of the functions. The next
      pre-registration states it as a limit: a run that takes electrons with a gain in energy
      not larger than a few times that error is not settled at this level alone.
   RI OR COSX ADD MORE THAN 0.005 eV at a size from 0.25 e up (R51.9): the ladder with RI and COSX
      and the rungs without are then not the same thing, and the next pre-registration says which
      it follows and why.
   THE COUNTS OR THE ENERGIES LIE FAR FROM CLAUDE'S MODELS: the model of the grid is wrong or ORCA
      does not build its grid as its manual says. The measured ladder stands; the model is not
      used again.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. WHAT THIS CANNOT SAY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
D51.1  One electron and a bare charge say what the functions, the grid and the functional do for
       one electron at one centre. They do not say what a run of the dianion does. The p
       functions are not tested at all.
D51.2  The grid of a lone ghost atom is not the grid of a molecule. A rung that serves here is
       the setting to try on the ghost atoms of the dianion. In D50's dianion runs the default
       grid already missed far less than here (section 1).
D51.3  The rule's third reading holds set X2 against set X3, not against a complete basis. By
       Claude's computation X3 lies within 0.0003 eV of a complete basis from 0.20 to 0.40 e
       with this functional, and within 0.008 eV at every size from 0.20 e up (the most at 1.00
       e, where the def2-TZVPD functions near the centre are the limit, in both sets alike).
       That is not a measurement. What the two sets share, the third reading cannot see; R51.5,
       Hartree-Fock against exact, does.
D51.4  The functional's error for one electron at one centre is not its error for an electron
       shared among several charges, nor for the last electron of the dianion.
D51.5  It does not test D50's finding on the dianion. By section 1 the grid's fault makes an
       electron at the charges look less bound than the functional has it, so it works against
       capture; what a larger grid changes in those numbers is NOT KNOWN. Nor does it turn D50's
       run of +0.25 e at site 1124 into a test of capture. D50.3: "A run that DID NOT TAKE with
       these functions is a test of capture at 0.25 e only if R50.3 finds the set adequate with
       the treatment that run used." R50.3 gave NONE, and that stands.
D51.6  Nothing here was computed with a wall, a second functional, or more than one charge.
