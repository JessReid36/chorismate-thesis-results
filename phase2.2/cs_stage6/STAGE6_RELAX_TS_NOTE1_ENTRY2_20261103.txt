job.pbs ends with

which is harmless for one job and removes another job's working files when two share a directory.

   THE CONTROL. 423866 finished at 11:26:06 and its cleanup deleted job.gbw. 423868 was still
   running, and two seconds later its output says

   - the module was called with job.gbw as its argument and the file had just been removed.

   THE DESIGN. 423867 finished at 11:26:04 and its cleanup deleted job*.tmp. 423869 ran on for
   another 82 seconds, completed a full self-consistent field ("Finished LeanSCF after 64.8 sec"),
   and then died at the end of the run on

      !!!                        FATAL ERROR ENCOUNTERED               !!!
      !!!                           CANNOT OPEN FILE                   !!!
      !!!               Filename: job.citations.tmp                    !!!

THIS IS A DIAGNOSIS, NOT A PROOF. It is consistent with every timestamp above, with the cleanup

What it is NOT is an input problem, and that much IS established rather than inferred:

So AMENDMENT 4's three changes are accepted by ORCA 6.0.1. Whether they converge to a first-order
saddle is a separate question and is not answered here.


E2.3  Exit_status AND orca_exit ARE NOT EVIDENCE OF ANYTHING

All four of the jobs above carry Exit_status = 0 from the scheduler, and all four failed. The
control's own job.pbs.out reports

   orca_exit=0 end=Tue Nov  3 11:26:08 SAST 2026

in the same directory whose job.out says "ORCA finished by error termination in LEANSCF". The
design's reports orca_exit=64, so the two failures do not even agree with each other.

A number from this project is therefore never licensed by an exit code. cs6_relaxed.py requires the
literal "****ORCA TERMINATED NORMALLY****" banner and, for a relaxation, "THE OPTIMIZATION HAS
CONVERGED" and exactly one stationary-point evaluation; it reads the exit codes and prints them as
an observation beside the run, and uses neither.

E2.5  TWO WRONG FIGURES IN NOTE 1

   (i) NOTE 1's closing section says "these eight lines are this note's own record of what was
   added" above a list of SEVEN sha256 lines - three for the control, four for the design. The
   count of files is seven, as that same note's own commit check ("$N staged, expected 7") requires.
   "Eight" is wrong.

   (ii) NOTE 1's COST section says "which puts these two jobs at the order of forty hours each". It
   reached that by applying WP4's per-OPTIMISATION-CYCLE time to numerical-Hessian DISPLACEMENTS,
   and the project's own record contradicts it. The two anchors, both from committed files:

      WP4's armb_anch                 46 cycles in 5 h 58 m on 8 cores  =  466.96 s per cycle
      18c_reduced_region/ts_reduced    612 displacements inside a total of 4 h 09 m 35 s on 16
                                       cores  =  at most 24.47 s per displacement, about 48.94 s
                                       scaled to 8 cores

   a factor of 9.5 between them. They are not the same quantity: an optimisation step moves far
   enough that the self-consistent field is nearly restarted, while a Hessian displacement is a tiny
   perturbation whose field converges in a few iterations from the one before it. Applying the first
   rate to 288 displacements gives 37.36 h, which is where "forty hours" came from, and it is the
   wrong rate for that term.

   Decomposed instead - cycles at WP4's rate, the 288 displacements of two Hessians at
   ts_reduced's -

      50 cycles   10.40 h        100 cycles  16.89 h
      195 cycles  29.21 h        400 cycles  55.80 h

   all inside the week queue's 168 h. These are ESTIMATES from two measured anchors and are labelled
   as such; the jobs themselves will say.

   NOTED IN PASSING, AND NOT A CORRECTION TO ANYTHING: STAGE6_CRITERIA.txt's own COST section
   estimates "of order 60-120 core-hours" for "about 16-20 jobs of that size" while naming armb_anch
   as the size, and armb_anch is 47.73 core-hours by itself, so 16 to 20 of them is 764 to 955
   core-hours - between 6.4 and 15.9 times the stated range. The pre-registration is not edited and
   no verdict rests on this figure. It is recorded because the same mis-estimate was repeated in
   NOTE 1 from the same source.


E2.6  AN UNCOMMITTED OCTOBER OUTPUT HAS BEEN OVERWRITTEN

relax/S6-C/R_opt already held a converged relaxation. STAGE6_RELAXATION_STATE_20261028.txt R1
records it: "ran 21 October 2026. 95 geometry optimisation cycles. THE OPTIMIZATION HAS CONVERGED.
FINAL SINGLE POINT ENERGY -836.193685389471 Eh". That output was never committed - R5 lists the
relax directory's 24 outputs under "result committed: -" and says collection was outstanding.

Job 423855, submitted on 3 November 2026 into the same directory, has truncated and is rewriting
that job.out. The trajectory, the final geometry and the optimisation history of the October run are
gone; the energy and the cycle count survive only because the 28 October note wrote them down.

This is exactly the loss the rule of 18 October 2026 exists to prevent, and it is recorded rather
than smoothed over. Two consequences:

   1. 423855 is deterministic and starts from the same committed input, so letting it finish
      restores a complete output set. Stopping it would leave neither.
   2. When it converges, its FINAL SINGLE POINT ENERGY against the committed -836.193685389471 Eh
      is a reproducibility check of the whole QM/MM setup across thirteen days and a different
      compute node, at no cost. If the two agree it is worth reporting as such. IF THEY DISAGREE,
      that matters more than the relaxation does, and it is reported before anything is built on
      either number.
