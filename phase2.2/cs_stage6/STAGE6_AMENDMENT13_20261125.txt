The result: "Runs measured: 222 of 224. The 27 designs: SURVIVOR: 18; NOT A SURVIVOR: FAILS: 7;
NOT A SURVIVOR: OPEN: 2." Survivors were found at 3, 4, 5, 6, 8 and 10 charges and at no larger

survivor is that of S_n10_k1, -13.0239 kcal/mol at the working level and -12.8928 at def2-TZVPD,
where the barrier without charges is +26.8248 and +25.7003. Among the 18 survivors the measured

WHAT STOPPED THE LARGER DESIGNS, as d56_designs.tsv holds it. The energy to remove the last
electron at R, +1.2486 eV without charges at the working level, is +1.72 to +1.84 eV with three
charges, +0.20 to +0.24 eV with ten (+0.13 to +0.17 at def2-TZVPD) and -0.01 to +0.05 eV with
twelve (-0.07 to -0.01 at def2-TZVPD): the three designs of twelve charges fail on the fifth leg
alone, their four legs being RELIABLE at both levels. The designs of fifteen charges fail at
def2-TZVPD on the four legs, with a dipole at R of 3.33 to 3.43 times the control's. Of the
designs of twenty charges one fails in the same way and two are OPEN, a run of each having
reached its cap.

So on these 27 designs the first thing to give way as charges are added is the hold on the last
electron, and the search of D56 has no term for it beyond the rule that the charges sum to zero
or more (A11.5, item 6). It has no term for the force a design puts on the substrate either
(A12.5, (i)). D56 is one frame, frozen, in vacuo; no design has been relaxed.

HOW SHARP THAT IS. The line of the fifth leg is at zero, it was fixed before the runs, and the
words of D56 stand. At R the designs of twelve charges lie between -0.07 and +0.05 eV of it,
four of their six readings below it; those of ten clear it by 0.13 to 0.24 eV. Between the two
basis sets the same energy differs by 0.06 to 0.07 eV for these six designs. So these runs do
not resolve where between ten and twelve charges the hold is lost, or whether it is lost within
that range at all; what they show beyond doubt of that size is the fall, from +1.2486 eV without
charges to about zero.

A13.3  WHAT JESSICA ASKED, AND CLAUDE'S PROPOSAL

On 24 November, told that the published line of work nearest to this one began with frozen paths
and moved to relaxing inside the search, she asked "would we be able to add it inside the
search?" and then "shall we compile that and run 3 and 4 parallel? how did dittner establish
their threshold?" Claude answered that a relaxed path for every candidate is not affordable at
this level of theory and does not fit a search that solves a linear model, and proposed what
follows. It is CLAUDE'S OWN PROPOSAL, IT IS UNTESTED, AND NO PAPER HELD USES IT IN THIS FORM:

   To first order the energy of the substrate with a design is its energy without, plus the sum
   over the charges of each charge times the substrate's own electrostatic potential at that
   site. The change of any quantity that is a difference or a derivative of energies is then a
   sum over the charges too, and can be a row of the search's linear program:

   (F) THE ADDED FORCE. The derivative, with respect to the position of each atom of the
      reactant, of the potential at each site. It is had from the potential of the bare
      substrate at geometries in which one atom is moved a small step along one axis: 24 atoms,
      3 axes, both ways, 144 single points at R, each giving the potential at every site, as
      D33's stage A gave the map the search already uses. That is 144 calculations and not the
      count of decision 2, because the map comes from moving the atoms and not from placing a
      charge at each site. The electrons' response to the step is in it; their response to the
      charges is not.

   (E) THE HOLD ON THE LAST ELECTRON. The potential at each site of the substrate with one
      electron removed, less that of the substrate itself, at R and at TS: to first order, how
      much a unit charge at that site changes the energy to remove the electron. It needs the
      two control runs with one electron removed that D56 already made, and no new calculation
      of the energy if their files are still on hpc1.

WHAT IS NOT KNOWN: whether first order is near enough for either. The lowering itself is 3 to 17
per cent larger than first order among D56's survivors. Each map is therefore checked against
direct measurements before it is used, and is not used if it fails its check (A13.4, items 3 and
4).
