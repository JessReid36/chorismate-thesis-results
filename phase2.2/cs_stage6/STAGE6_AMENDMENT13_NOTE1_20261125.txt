N1.1  THE RELAXATIONS ALREADY IN THE RECORD

AMENDMENT 13, A13.1: "D56 is one frame, frozen, in vacuo; no design has been relaxed." That is
true of D56's designs. It leaves out what the record holds on relaxing a substrate beside
charges, which Claude had not re-read when it wrote A13.4 item 5:

   1. Bare point charges in a geometry optimisation, in the earlier generation of this work:
      ATTEMPT_INVENTORY_20261104.md, section 2.6, records eight such runs, none converged, and
      "6 of 8 imploded to 0.01-1.06 A atom-to-charge"

   2. The committed charge model gives a charge in a relaxation a shell.
      CHARGE_MODEL_DECISIONS.md lists among its arms "(a) bare +1, run as a QM/MM site with zero
      LJ (control, expected to collapse)" and says "the bare LJ sphere at def2-SVP stays the
      production representation." The sphere is a point charge that is also an atom of the force
      field with the Lennard-Jones term of an Amber nitrogen (L_f42436.tsv: "sphere R* 1.824 A
      eps 0.170 kcal/mol").

   3. That protocol ran on 3 and 4 November on the control and on one design of ten spheres
      (relax/S6-C and relax/n10_A; outputs committed). STAGE6_CRITERIA.txt: a "TS that does not
      return exactly one imaginary mode is reported as such and is not used for a relaxed
      barrier." Formed again from the committed outputs by a13_note1/note_numbers.py:
      "S6-C/TS_opt2: imaginary modes in the last frequency block: -306.38"; "n10_A/TS_opt2:
      imaginary modes in the last frequency block: -187.48, -50.00" So no relaxed barrier
      follows for that design. The same program gives the nearest distance from a substrate atom
      to a sphere: "n10_A/R_opt: 10 spheres; nearest substrate atom to a sphere 2.85 A at the
      start, 2.72 A at the end"; "n10_A/TS_opt2: 10 spheres; nearest substrate atom to a sphere
      2.92 A at the start, 2.68 A at the end" That design stood on an earlier grid, at another
      level of theory, with charges other than D56's: it is a warning about relaxing designs of
      ten charges, not a reading of D56's.

A13.4 item 5 says the pilot is relaxed "in vacuo, the charges held where they are." and does not
say what a charge is there. By the committed charge model it is the sphere, and the pilot's
pre-registration is to say so. A bare point charge is the arm the charge model calls expected to
collapse.


N1.2  D56 LEFT THE SHELL'S TERM OUT, AND DID NOT SAY IN WHICH DIRECTION

Jessica asked on 25 November "have we been running the commited charge model or the bare point
charges?" and then "why have we been using the bare charges if we have a commited charge rep?"

The frozen diagnostics since D31 have used bare point charges (D31, D33b, D43, D44, D47, D50,
D53, D54 and D56 write them with %pointcharges; D32 ran the core-potential wall and D34 the
sphere), and D43's pre-registration said so: "Bare point charges throughout: no wall, no
Lennard-Jones term, no continuum." In a frozen run the sphere and the bare point charge are the
same electronic calculation; the shell adds a classical energy, the same for every level of
theory. The grid's sites stand at the shell's contact distances. D56's pre-registration, section
2: "coefficients A_f42436_d33a.tsv, the working level, one frame; L is zero" It gives no reason
and no direction. It is a fifth thing D56's texts did not state, after the three of AMENDMENT
12, A12.5, and the one of AMENDMENT 13, A13.5.

      survivors of AMENDMENT 11, A11.5 item 5; and any frozen run of the pilot's designs that
      the pilot's pre-registration needs and those do not give. On the PC meanwhile: A13.4 items
      7 and 8.
   3. The pilot of A13.4 item 5, pre-registered once the results of step 2 that bear on it are
      committed: the committed sphere protocol; a second arm behind the helium wall only if
      D57's measurement against real ions supports the wall by the reading its own

      a design whose reactant held.
   4. The second search of A13.4 item 6, which waits for the maps of step 2 and not for the
      pilot.
