A12.2  WHAT THE COMMITTED TEXTS HOLD

D56 reads bare point charges only, and its pre-registration ends the word so: "Otherwise it is
NOT A SURVIVOR, with FAILS or OPEN beside it." AMENDMENT 11, A11.5, item 4: "A design that fails
is dropped; none is rescued." Item 5: "THE SURVIVORS, at most the ten with the largest lowering,
ARE RUN WITH FUNCTIONS AT THEIR CHARGES AND BEHIND THE HELIUM WALL under a pre-registration of
their own, and what is found is reported beside each." Item 8: "No further diagnostic on the
charge model, the safe distance or electron capture is run before the search." The search has
since been run: its twenty-seven designs are committed.

AMENDMENT 10, A10.3, item 3, brought the wall forward to be tested and not adopted, as a test
arm: (a) "Arrangements that are measured bare under step 2a of A10.4 may also be run walled, in
the same diagnostic or in one that follows it": "every charge of the arrangement placed as a
coreless core potential that carries the charge, with functions beside each positive charge".
(b) "The parameter sets are the helium set and the neon set." "Both sets are run, so that what
depends on the set is seen." (c) "What a walled run is read by, its controls (the wall with no
charge among them) and its gates are fixed by the pre-registration of the diagnostic that runs
it, before any of its outputs exists." (d) "No design is named, kept or dropped on a walled run,
no ddE of a walled run is quoted as a result, and no search is run walled. Adopting the wall as
the charge model would need a further dated amendment, and before it a measurement of the wall
against something physical, which the record does not hold."

A10.1 holds what the record has measured of a wall. The sets are "one paper, one group, one
fitting recipe". D32's verdict: "THE WALL RESCUES IT UP TO SOME n: rescued or unchanged at n =
[1, 2, 3], still unreliable at n = [5, 10, 20]"; its limits: "it does not validate the wall
against anything physical; no def2-TZVPD arm was run". And: "So the record holds no walled run
at def2-TZVPD and none with a basis function at a charge." The one comparison of a point charge
with a real ion in the record is DIAGNOSTIC 3: Na+ and K+ as quantum atoms at one site against a
bare charge of +1 e, with ghost controls. Its result: "VERDICT PER D3.3: INCONCLUSIVE". No
walled arm was run in it.

A12.3  WHAT THE PAPERS HELD SAY

Read on 24 November from the library (chorismate-refs, fa8adbaf). Web pages are not the record
and nothing here rests on one.

   1.  The wall of every committed walled run is Marefat Khah et al., J. Chem. Theory Comput.
       2020 (acs.jctc.9b01162.pdf and its supporting information). Claude's sweep of the library
       on 2 November (a project note, not a committed file) found no other parameter set of its
       kind. In that paper each wall stands for the electrons of a real atom, and it is tested
       against full quantum calculations of excitation energies.

   2.  Kalka, Novotny and Stare, J. Chem. Inf. Model. 2025, 65, 8653 (ci5c01235.pdf), enzyme
       reactions in point charges at M06-2X/6-31+G**: "both QM and CH methods provide consistent
       interaction energies at sufficiently large separations" (r > 3 A), and they diverge
       closer in. DERIVED on 24 November from D56's committed inputs: the nearest charge of any
       design is 3.02 A from an atom (108 dianion inputs, R and TS). Their charges stand for
       real residues.

   3.  Shi et al., arXiv:2412.17204v2 (2412.17204v2.pdf, added to the library on 24 November for
       this question), clusters of ionic solids in point charges, with diffuse functions on the
       non-metal atoms and ORCA among its programs: "A further region of effective core
       potentials was placed on the positive point charges" within 4 A (6 A) of the quantum
       cluster "to prevent spurious charge leakage". The negative charges are left as points.
       The whole paper was searched: it does not name the core potential, gives no parameters,
       and runs nothing with and without it. It says where its protocol comes from: "The SKZCAM
       protocol was developed in Refs. 284 and 285". It is published as Nature Chemistry 2025,
       doi 10.1038/s41557-025-01884-y (s41557-025-01884-y.pdf, held since 16:48 and read in
       full): the wall is in its Methods in the same words and nowhere in its results. What it
       sets against experiment, 19 adsorption enthalpies, is its whole protocol.

   4.  Its reference 284, Shi et al., J. Chem. Phys. 156, 124704 (2022) (124704_1_online.pdf,
       held since 16:48 and read in full): within 7 A of the quantum cluster the positive point
       charges are capped with the effective core potential "of the corresponding metal ion,
       taken from the Stuttgart/Cologne group", "to avoid electron leakage from the bonded O
       ions at the boundary of the quantum cluster". Of its quantum clusters: "resulting quantum
       clusters are all negatively charged". Nothing in it is run with the wall and without it.
       Its supplementary material (22 pages, sha256 d3a97a28..., attached by Jessica in the chat
       and NOT in the library when this was written) holds the one test near the wall, Table S3
       at PBE with def2-SVP: capped region 7.0 A and point charges to 30 A, 5.501 eV; 7.0 A and
       40 A, 5.513 eV; 10.6 A and 40 A, 5.513 eV. So a wall further out than 7 A changes nothing
       there, and no row has a smaller capped region or none. Its section S15, of 0.6 eV left
       unexplained between its result and another group's: it "could arise from differing
       electrostatic embedding environments (e.g. point charge and ECP regions) between the two
       studies".

   5.  The authors' code, which is not a paper: github.com/benshi97/autoSKZCAM, the address the
       Nature Chemistry paper gives, read at its commit 402a15e6. It writes each capped site
       into an ORCA input as a centre without electrons that carries the point charge, followed
       by one block of numbers (capped_ecp_defaults in src/autoSKZCAM/data.py): N_core 0, lmax
       f, one Gaussian for each of s (exponent 1.732, coefficient 14.676), p (1.115, 5.1757) and
       d (1.203, -1.816), f zero. That is the same kind of object as this project's wall, with
       other numbers. One block serves every capped ion unless the user gives another. Which
       table of the Stuttgart/Cologne group those numbers are is not stated in the code and
       Claude has not checked it.

So: every wall in a paper held stands for a real atom or a real ion with a whole charge. No
paper held puts a wall on a charge of a fraction of an electron that stands for no atom, and
none tests such a wall on the barrier of an anion. By the rule of three full papers, nothing
here is called standard practice: items 3 and 4 are two works of one group.

A12.4  WHAT IS DECIDED

1.  WHICH DESIGNS ARE ALSO RUN WALLED. Once D56's result is committed, at each of its nine
    numbers of charges: of the designs the result calls NOT A SURVIVOR, the one ranked best at
    first order (the smallest rank_at_n in the committed d56/DESIGNS.tsv). At most nine designs.
    Where all three designs of a number of charges are survivors, none is taken at that number.
    This rule is fixed here, before any output of D56 is collected, and it is applied by a
    program, not by choice. Claude asked whether it should be only that one design or every
    design that is not a survivor; Jessica answered "1. yes", which Claude reads as the one
    design. It is the smaller of the two.

2.  HOW THEY ARE WALLED. By A10.3, item 3, (b) and (c): the helium set and the neon set are both
    run, and the wall with no charge is a control. Where the wall stands is item 5: each
    positive charge of the design is placed as a coreless core potential that carries the
    charge, and each negative charge stays a bare point charge. Dianion and monoanion, R and TS,
    at D56's two levels, so that the walled reading is by the same five legs and the same
    tolerance as the bare one. No basis function is placed beside a charge, because D56 has none
    and the two readings are to differ in the wall alone. The test with functions stays where
    A11.5, item 5, put it. At most 24 runs for a design: 8 with each set and 8 controls.

3.  WHAT A WALLED READING IS. It is reported beside the design's bare reading and it is nothing
    more. A10.3, item 3 (d), stands as written, and so does A11.5, item 4: no design is named,
    kept or dropped on a walled run, and none is rescued by one. The word SURVIVOR is read on
    bare charges, as D56's pre-registration fixed it.

4.  THE WALL IS MEASURED AGAINST REAL IONS, which (d) asks for before the wall could be adopted.
    In the same diagnostic: at one site, a charge of +1 e as a bare point, behind the helium
    wall, behind the neon wall, behind the published block of A12.3, item 5, each of the three
    with no charge, and Na+ and K+ as quantum atoms with their ghost controls, as DIAGNOSTIC 3
    built them; R and TS; D56's two levels. The published block is here because Jessica asked
    "should we not do it that way too - using the core potential of the matching metal ion from
    the Stuttgart/Cologne group?" and, to Claude's proposal that it be run in this measurement
    only, where a matching real ion exists, answered "yes, yes, yes" (the first of three
    answers). A10.3, item 3 (b), names the helium and the neon set; for this measurement alone
    the published block is run beside them, and it is placed on no design. D3's verdict stands
    and is not read again. WHAT IT CAN SAY: whether, for a whole charge at one site, the walled
    charge or the bare charge lies nearer the real ions. WHAT IT CANNOT SAY: anything of a
    charge of 0.25 e, for which no real ion exists. Whether a walled reading may ever count for
    a design is for a dated amendment written after that result.

5.  THE WALL STANDS ON THE POSITIVE CHARGES ONLY. Jessica: "im happy to do just positives." and,
    to Claude's question where the wall stands, "2. 2. positive only." A10.3, item 3 (a), has
    "every charge of the arrangement placed as a coreless core potential that carries the
    charge, with functions beside each positive charge"; for every walled run from here on, the
    runs of A11.5, item 5, among them, that is replaced as item 2 says. THIS IS HER DECISION AND
    IT RESTS ON THE PRACTICE OF TWO WORKS OF ONE GROUP, NOT ON A TEST. The works of A12.3, items
    3 and 4, wall their positive charges only, beside quantum clusters that are negatively
    charged as the substrate here is; neither concludes that this is sufficient and neither
    tests it, and by the rule of three papers it is not called standard practice. Told so by
    Claude and asked whether it stands, Jessica answered "yes, yes, yes" (the second of three
    answers). Every walled run in the record to date walls every charge, so no committed walled
    number is like for like with a run under this item.

6.  ONE WALLED DESIGN IS ALSO RUN WITH EVERY CHARGE WALLED, so that what depends on where the
    wall stands is seen. Of the designs of item 1, the one with the most charges; where two have
    as many, the one ranked better. Both sets, the wall with no charge as control, the same
    states and levels: at most 24 further runs. If item 1 takes no design, this is not run. It
    is reported beside the two other readings of that design and decides nothing. Jessica's
    answer to this proposal of Claude's: "yes, yes, yes" (the third of three answers).

7.  ONE DIAGNOSTIC, ITS OWN PRE-REGISTRATION. The walled runs of items 1, 2 and 6 and the
    measurement of item 4 are DIAGNOSTIC 57. (The number 55 was drafted and not used.) Its
    readings, controls, gates and what each outcome sends the next step to are fixed in its pre-
    registration, committed before any of its outputs exists. D32's result records that a walled
    run's dipole is not read as a bare run's is; the pre-registration says how it is read. No
    input of D57 is formed before D56's result is committed, since item 1 needs it.

8.  NOTHING OF D56 CHANGES. Its runs, its reader, its gates, its word and its outcomes (a) to
    (d) are as committed. "No setting of section 2 is changed with this result in hand except by
    a dated amendment that says what is changed and in which direction." and "No run is made
    again in its folder." Neither is touched: D57's runs are new runs in new folders, and no
    setting of D56's search is changed.

9.  HOW THIS STANDS WITH THE STOPPING RULES. D54's pre-registration: "No diagnostic on electron
    capture follows D54." A11.5, item 8, bars a further diagnostic on the charge model "before
    the search", and the search has been run. D57 places no basis function at a charge, so it is

    changes no design's word. It is here because Jessica called it unfair to judge an

A12.5  WHAT D56'S TEXTS DID NOT STATE

Jessica asked on 24 November: "were there any rules that you omitted from the optimiser?" Claude
compared the search of D56 with OPTIMISER_DECISIONS_20261020.md. What was changed and is stated
in AMENDMENT 11 or in D56's pre-registration: the budget set where it cannot bind and a cap of
0.25 e in its place; one frame and no penalty for spread; three designs kept for a number of
charges and not ten; a fixed list of numbers of charges; the sum of the charges zero or more; L
zero; every design measured in full on one frame in place of the re-scoring of the best twenty
on every frame. THREE THINGS WERE LEFT OUT AND NOT STATED. They are Claude's omissions from
texts Jessica committed, and they are recorded here because those texts are not edited:

   (i)  Decision 2, the reactant-gradient term: "If Stage 6 can fix no threshold, G_add,R is
        reported for every design without a penalty, and this is stated as a limitation of the
        short list." D56's inputs compute energies only, so the gradient a design adds at R is
        neither computed nor reported. Direction of the omission: toward a lower barrier, since
        a design that pulls hard on the substrate is not marked.

   (ii)  Decision 10, the null distribution of random designs under the same rules: "Each
         design's percentile in (i) and (ii) is reported." D56 has none, so it cannot say that
         the search does better than random placement. Direction: none on the barrier.

   (iii)  Decision 9: "Report the electrostatic part of each design's frozen change in reaction
          energy" from the product-side potentials. D56 does not report it. Direction: none.

None of the three changes which designs the search placed: decisions 2 and 10 act after the
search and decision 9 puts no rule in it. PROPOSED BY CLAUDE AND NOT DECIDED: the gradient at R
for each survivor, reported without a penalty, in the pre-registration that A11.5, item 5, calls
for; and the null distribution at first order under a short pre-registration of its own.
