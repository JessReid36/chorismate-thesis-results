read them, and what was planned in between

25 November 2026. Written after D56's result was committed (results 81f73560). It edits nothing,
runs nothing and adopts nothing. It is a disclosure. Claude told Jessica on 24 November 2026,
when the looks were made, that they would be recorded in a dated note.


N1.1  WHY THIS NOTE

While the ten jobs of D56 were running, Jessica asked whether the outputs that existed could be
previewed, and two looks were made, at 20:19 and at 20:36 on 24 November. Claude told her from
them which way the designs were leaning, and between 20:39 and 21:03 she and Claude talked about
what should follow D56. The result and the files committed with it do not say so. This note says
what was looked at, when, what was said from it, what was planned after it, and why it could not
change the result. Two things Claude said from the first look were wrong or unfounded; they are
corrected in N1.5.

preview the results from those that have left the que"

N1.4  WHAT CLAUDE TOLD JESSICA FROM THEM

After LOOK 1, in its reply to the printout, Claude said: "Everything below is my unofficial
reading of the preview; only H1 gives results." Then: "3 to 10 charges (18 designs) look healthy
so far."; "12 charges look likely to fail the binding check."; "15 and 20 charges look likely to
fail at def2-TZVPD."; that the lowering was larger than the first-order score; and that the
margin by which the last electron is bound falls as charges are added.

At 20:23 Jessica asked "is it looking promising though?" and Claude answered "Yes, cautiously.
On this unofficial preview it is the most promising thing Stage 6 has produced, though three
caveats could still shrink it." It named the caveats: the four legs had not been read; the
numbers are of frozen geometries on one frame in vacuo; and no comparison with random designs
has been made. It also set the lowering beside a figure of the literature: "For scale only, the
Claeyssens 2011 paper in your project files puts the enzyme's own transition-state stabilisation
at about 7 kcal/mol; that is a different kind of calculation, so it is not a like-for-like
comparison." No comparison of that kind is made in D56's result, and none is made here.

After LOOK 2, in its reply to the printout: "This is a good preview: 16 designs carry the word
SURVIVOR so far, and for 3 to 10 charges the electrons stay on the substrate by a wide margin.
It is still unofficial, and H1 decides." Then: that the designs of 12 charges failed on the
fifth leg alone and those of 15 by electrons moving toward the charges at def2-TZVPD; that
S_n10_k1 had the largest lowering among the designs that looked sound; that by the rule of
AMENDMENT 12 the walled runs would go to the best-ranked failed design at 12, 15 and 20 charges
if H1 agreed; and "I expect the wall may help at 15 and not at 12." That last is an expectation
that no run has tested.


N1.5  THE LOOKS AGAINST THE RESULT, AND WHAT CLAUDE SAID FROM THEM THAT WAS WRONG

Every change of the barrier that the looks printed (51 in each) equals the committed
d56_designs.tsv to the three decimals printed, and every word of the four legs that LOOK 2
printed is the committed one. LOOK 2's tally of 16, 6 and 5 became 18, 7 and 2 in the result:
S_n06_k3 and S_n08_k3 became SURVIVOR when their last runs ended, S_n20_k3 FAILS, and S_n20_k1
and S_n20_k2 are OPEN. The looks are the source of none of it: every number of the result was
formed by the committed reader from the outputs, under rules fixed before the jobs began.

   1. After LOOK 1 Claude said: "One flag lower down: design n06_k2 has an unstable
      one-electron-removed state at the transition state, which the binding check does not
      allow." That was wrong. Block LOOK marks any output in which the word occurs; the
      committed reader takes the last verdict of the stability analysis, which for that run is
      stable. The result gives S_n06_k2 the fifth leg PASSED at both levels and the word
      SURVIVOR. Claude corrected it after LOOK 2: "One correction to the first look: it flagged
      n06_k2 as unstable. The reader uses ORCA's final stability verdict, which is stable, so
      that design passes."

   2. After LOOK 1 Claude said of the runs that had not ended: "The other three (one at 15
      charges, two at 20) may have hit their time limit." The one at 15 charges had not: the
      committed log has "TZ_S_n15_k1_mo_R orca_exit=0 normal=1 Tue Nov 24 20:26:08 SAST 2026";
      it was running. Of the two at 20 charges, TZ_S_n20_k1_di_R had been stopped at its cap at
      18:39:41 and TZ_S_n20_k2_di_R was running and was stopped at its cap at 20:24:29.

Neither statement entered the result, a rule or a decision. They are corrected here because they
were said.


N1.6  WHAT WAS PLANNED AFTER THE LOOKS AND BEFORE THE RESULT

This is the part of the evening that the looks could have shaped, and it is recorded for that
reason. With the unofficial numbers known, Jessica asked: at 20:39 "do we not need to do walled
runs for the survivors as well?"; at 20:46 "okay hypothetically design 10 passes, then what"; at
20:49 "what is the likely hood of 4 passing? what did ditner and hark and behrends do? static or
relaxed?"; at 20:54 "would we be able to add it inside the search?"; at 20:58 "shall we compile
that and run 3 and 4 parallel? how did dittner establish their threshold?"

Claude answered from the committed record and the library, and proposed, as its own untested
idea, a map of the first-order force that a unit charge at each site puts on each atom of the
reactant, from 144 single points at displaced geometries, so that a limit on the added force
could be a linear rule inside the search. At 20:58 it set out a plan: single-point jobs run
together once the result was committed (the test of the survivors of AMENDMENT 11 with a run of
the forces for each, the walled runs and the measurement against real ions of AMENDMENT 12, the
144 runs of the map); a pilot, in Claude's words "The pilot relaxation of the best 3-, 6- and
10-charge designs."; and then a new search with a ladder of limits on the added force in two
arms, one with the cap of 0.25 e and one with mixed sizes under the budget. It said: "Committing
the amendment is how you approve the ladder, the two arms and the three-design pilot; you can
change any of them before you do." At 21:03 Jessica answered "ok yes".

WHAT OF THIS WAS FIXED BEFORE THE LOOKS: which designs are run walled (AMENDMENT 12, A12.4,
items 1 and 6) and that the survivors, at most ten, are tested with functions at their charges
and behind the wall (AMENDMENT 11, A11.5, item 5).

WHAT WAS NOT, and was chosen with the unofficial numbers known: that a pilot is relaxed first
and that it is of the designs of 3, 6 and 10 charges (ten being the largest number at which the
looks showed designs passing); that a force is measured for each survivor; the map; and the form
of the new search. None of it is committed, none of it changes a word or a number of D56, and
none of it is in force until an amendment written after the result says so and Jessica commits
it. That amendment is to cite the result and not the looks.

After 21:03 Claude read committed inputs of earlier diagnostics (d33a, d34, d32, d53 and those
of DIAGNOSTIC 3) to see which forms of input have already run on hpc1. It changed nothing.
