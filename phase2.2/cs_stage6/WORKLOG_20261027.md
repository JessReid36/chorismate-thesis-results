## y09 — CPCM removed, and the charge model put on trial (27 October 2026)

- **Jessica's question drove this.** "Does it not defeat the point of having the charges?" Chasing
  it produced a sharper answer than the artefact argument I had given, and then a second finding
  that matters more than the continuum question.

- **CPCM is removed** — `STAGE6_AMENDMENT2_20261027.txt`. Three independent grounds, all
  grep-verified from full papers held in `chorismate-refs`: it is the wrong *kind* of field
  (Fried & Boxer's preorganisation-versus-reorganisation, Warshel's preorganisation term being
  identically zero in a randomly-oriented medium — five sources); the geometry has no precedent
  (Barone & Cossi put explicit charges *inside* the cavity; four sources use that topology; the
  strings "inside the cavity", "outside the cavity" and "dielectric boundary" occur in **zero** of
  the 77 held papers); and it is not a small constant (Claeyssens Table 4: PCM 53.4 against IPCM
  36.0 kcal/mol on *this* reaction at identical QM level). `STAGE6_CRITERIA.txt` untouched.

- **What the amendment deliberately does not claim.** That combining them is formally
  double-counting — no held source says so. That D10's CPCM numbers are wrong as measurements —
  they reproduce D2's independently generated C4 condition to 3.4e-10 Eh. And ground 3's
  −6.6/+5.9/+20.2 figures are marked corroborative, not decisive, because the dielectric
  differential is not an exact decomposition of the total and does not match for S6-2.

- **Diagnostic 11 pre-registered** — the charge model and the minimum stand-off. One charge walked
  outward along a fixed axis at 2.50 / 2.9596 / 3.50 / 4.50 / 6.00 / 15.00 Å, bare point charge
  against the same charge behind a coreless-ECP Pauli wall, 52 single points, no continuum.
  Anchored on Senn & Thiel: the artefact "becomes more severe the closer the point charge
  approaches the QM density and the more spatially flexible the density is", worse with diffuse
  functions. Stage 6 sits at 2.85–2.96 Å in ma-def2-SVP — the worst case on both axes at once.

- **The `dref` rung is byte-identical to D10's S6-P.** Same `substrate.xyz`, same `pc.pc`, same

  certified almost any separation as safe. It is now relative (10 % of |ddE(bare)|, with a
  0.05 kcal/mol numerical floor), requires monotone convergence, and requires two successive rungs.

- **Probe 1 pre-registered** — the explicit-water cost-and-binding probe. Bare substrate only: no

## y13 — Diagnostic 12 pre-registered: what predicts the overpolarisation artefact

- **Read Diagnostic 11's axis definition from the committed source** rather than from memory.
  `cs11_standoff.py:axis()` takes the substrate atom nearest site 1690 and the unit vector to it.
  That atom is **O3 (index 7), at 2.9596 A** — confirmed against `relax/ARRANGEMENTS.txt`.
  My working note had called this "the O D11 used", which was right, but for the wrong reason:
  I had assumed the grid's `on_atom` column named it.

- **The grid records site 1690 as `on_atom=H3`, not O3.** H3 is 3.2110 A away; O3 is 2.9596 A.
  So `on_atom` is not the nearest atom. Checked what it is instead: H3's p100 floor is **3.211**,
  exactly the site's distance to it, while O3's p100 floor is 2.745 and the site clears it.
  `on_atom` is **the atom whose floor binds**. The grid's own design, not a defect.

- **Audited every site against every atom's floor**, not just its labelled one, since that was
  the defect I suspected. 1463 anion-layer sites at q=-1 against the `neg` column, and all 2463
  sites admitting positive charge at their own tightest admitted level. **Zero violations in
  both.** Admission was enforced against all 24 atoms simultaneously. 18.9 % / 16.5 % of sites
  have an `on_atom` that is not the nearest atom, which is the labelling, not an error.

- **Abandoned the per-atom floor table before writing it.** It was the diagnostic Jessica and I
  had agreed on. Three measurements, all at the committed R geometry over the 2463 sites that
  admit q=+1:
  - **C4, C8, C9 are never the nearest atom to any admissible site**; C5 and C7 own fewer than
    ten each. Their rows could never be filled by measurement.
  - At fixed nearest-atom distance (0.2 A bands) the whole-molecule term `sum 1/d^2` still
    varies by **57–88 %** of its median.
  - For **199 sites (8.1 %)** the nearest atom differs between R and TS. A charge would change
    rows mid-reaction.
  A Lennard-Jones floor is pairwise and survives all three; overpolarisation is a response of
  the whole density and has no pairwise decomposition to hang a row on.

- **Checked the axis rules I had considered, and discarded them.** A centroid-outward axis fails
  for C1 (another atom is nearer at every rung). A grid-site-direction axis works for C10/H2/O2
  but not for the atom D11 used. Then the decisive one: maximising, over 40000 directions, the
  ratio (2nd-nearest distance)/(distance to the target atom), **no atom reaches 1.25 at 4.50 A**
  and C4/C9 never reach **1.00 at any rung**. D11's own axis reaches 1.00 by its outermost rungs.
  Any further per-atom ladder would have been measuring the molecule, not the atom.

- **Rebuilt the question around real sites.** D12 measures `d_shield = ddE(bare) - ddE(wall)` at
  36 committed grid sites and asks which of five pre-registered descriptors predicts it.
  Adequacy needs **both** |Spearman rho| >= 0.80 **and** a threshold separating pass from fail
  with zero misclassifications; the separation is the operative test, because a descriptor can
  correlate well in the bulk and still misorder sites at the boundary — which is precisely where
  a screen is read. Unit-tested that case: rho = -0.900 with one misordered site is correctly
  rejected.

- **The sample is frozen in the pre-registration and in the harness.** 36 sites, 17 of 17
  populated bins of the (d_nearest, sum 1/d^2) plane. Across the grid those two descriptors
  correlate at **r = -0.710**; a sample inheriting that could not tell them apart, so the
  selection minimises |r| subject to bin coverage and reaches **r = +0.0000**. All 36 admit
  q = +1, so charge is constant and cannot confound. Site 1690 is forced in.

- **Site 1690 is three gates at once.** It is D10's S6-P, D11's `dref`, and a site whose answer
  is already known: D11 measured `d_shield = +3.9642` against a tolerance of 0.386, a **FAIL**.
  So the sample contains a site of each class before anything runs, which the separation test
  requires. Verified the generated inputs reproduce it: `pc.pc` and `job.inp` are **byte-identical**
  to D10's committed `S6-P_R_vac`, and the `substrate.xyz` **coordinate block** matches
  (sha256 `5740f38f...`); the files differ only in the xyz comment line, which carries the run
  name and generating path and which ORCA ignores. D11's own `dref` differs from D10 in the same
  single line, so this matches precedent. Corrected the docstring I had inherited from D11, which
  said "byte-identical" of the whole file.

- **Discovered the `d_` columns are not reactant distances.** They are the per-atom **minimum
  over R, TS and P** — verified on all 2626 sites, max deviation 0.00005 A. Correct for a

## y15 — Diagnostics 12 and 13 collected, and cross-checked against each other

- **Both results re-measured from the committed outputs by this bundle**, not copied from a
  scratch directory, so each committed result is provably what the committed outputs produce.
  The bundle refuses before committing anything if either run directory holds the wrong number
  of outputs (282 and 114) or if any run did not terminate normally.

- **A cross-check that neither diagnostic contains.** D13's q = 1.00 arm repeats D12's main arm
  exactly: the same +1 charge, bare and behind a Pauli wall, at the same eight positions, at R
  and TS. The two input sets were generated independently by two harnesses written a day apart,
  and nothing in either compares them. `cs_crosscheck.py` does.

- **What it can and cannot show, stated before it ran.** It is a harness check, not physics.
  Agreement shows only that the two generators placed the same charge in the same place and that
  ORCA returned the same number twice. Disagreement shows one of them is wrong and names the
  quantity. Each diagnostic's own gates stand either way.

- **Its tolerances are the diagnostics' own.** GATE 0 is run separately on both run directories;
  the two controls must agree to 1e-6 Eh; every shared ddE to 0.01 kcal/mol, which is what D12
  and D13 use for their own gates. Since the two sets of inputs are numerically identical ORCA
  problems, real agreement should be at the 1e-6 kcal/mol level, not merely inside the tolerance
  — a result near the tolerance would itself be a finding.

- **The cross-check reuses D13's own constants and output readers**, extracted from
  `cs13_qscale.py` through the AST rather than retyped, so it cannot disagree with the
  diagnostics about what it is reading.

- **Five negative tests, all fired before the bundle was built**: a shared ddE perturbed by
  1e-4 Eh in one harness; the two controls made to differ by 1e-5 Eh; a D13 input file altered
  after its manifest; a D12 output removed; and the measure directory pointed inside a run
  directory. One early test did not fire and the cause was my own sequencing — I had restored one
  run directory and not the other — not the guard.

- **The commit message and the section below are written by the bundle, not by hand.** The verdict
  lines are read out of the three result files at commit time, so the committed message states
  what was measured rather than what was expected. This matters here because the bundle was built
  before either job finished.
