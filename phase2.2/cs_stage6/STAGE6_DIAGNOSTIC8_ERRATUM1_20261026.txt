STAGE 6 - DIAGNOSTIC 8, ERRATUM 1, 26 October 2026

STAGE6_DIAGNOSTIC8_20261026.txt is a pre-registration and is not edited. DIAGNOSTIC8_RESULT.txt is
a committed result and is not edited. This erratum records what in that result stands and what is
withdrawn, and why.


E8.1  WHAT IS WITHDRAWN

Diagnostic 8's stage 3, and readings (b), (g) and (h) in their entirety.

The reason is printed in D8's own result. D8's charge detector matched NOTHING in this corpus. It
looked for an Ne> centre on an unprefixed line carrying four numbers. ORCA prints the charge of such
a centre only in the input echo, where every line is prefixed '|  30> ', and prints the centre in
the coordinate block with its position alone, three numbers and no charge. The pattern therefore
never fired, and the three readings built on it are empty of content:

  (b) "There are none" is not a finding. It is what the reading prints when the detector finds
      nothing, whatever the corpus contains. There are in fact 101 such runs.
  (g) "0 of 1521 point-charge single points have recoverable charges" is the same artefact. The
      .pc matcher also failed: it looked for <stem>.pc beside each output, and the 488 committed
      charge files are named env.pc, pc.pc, orca.pc and per-frame names, never job.pc.
  (h) could not run, having no charge vectors to run on.

Stage 3 is withdrawn for a consequence of the same defect. 101 runs that carry charges entered its
CONTROL pool, where they set the thresholds every charged run is judged against. D8's own
pre-registered specificity check detected this and printed it: 49 of 469 charge-free controls
classified as something other than CLEAN, against D6.4(a), which states that zero is the only value
that licenses the rest of the table. By that rule, which D8 inherited deliberately and did not
weaken, stage 3's control medians, its verdict counts, its onset reading and its tables are not
licensed and must not be quoted. The onset figure in particular - "LOWEST CHARGE COUNT AT WHICH A
def2-SVP SINGLE POINT IS STRAINED OR COLLAPSED, stage 3: 3" - is withdrawn.

This is the failure mode Erratum 4 E4.6 named, arriving by a route E4.6 did not name. E4.6 required
the walker and the charge detection to be fixed in the same change, because fixing the walker alone
would admit charged runs as controls. D8 fixed them in the same change, as required, and the charge
half of that change did not work. The hazard was correctly identified and the guard against it
correctly fired.


E8.2  WHAT STANDS

Everything at stages 1 and 2, and the gate.

  - THE GATE PASSED against the real corpus. D8 rebuilt the entire text of DIAGNOSTIC6_RESULT.txt
    from its stage 1 and it was byte-identical to the committed file. D8's reproduction of D6.3 is
    faithful over all 1680 runs D6 saw, and the committed record still matches the committed corpus.
  - Specificity 0 of 352 at stage 1 and 0 of 350 at stage 2. Both licensed.
  - E4.5 CONFIRMED. D6 classified 1680 of 2095 completed runs. 415 were never opened.
  - E4.4(ii) CONFIRMED. D6's five categories sum to 1726 against 1680 runs kept. Point-charge
    geometry optimisations fall from 47 to 1 once the categories are made disjoint: 46 of the 47
    were QM/MM runs counted in two categories at once.
  - E4.4(i) CONFIRMED, and to the digit Erratum 4 predicted. The def2-SVP control gap median moves
    3.278 to 3.260 when two charge-free geometry optimisations leave the control pool.
  - NO VERDICT CHANGED on the runs D6 already had. D6's classification of the runs it could see
    stands unaltered.

Diagnostic 9 re-checks all of this: its gate 2 requires it to reproduce, line for line, exactly the
stage 1 and stage 2 rows of D8 tables (a) and (d) that this erratum leaves standing.


E8.3  ONE READING IN STAGE 1 THAT IS LICENSED AND HAS NOT BEEN REMARKED ON

D8 reading (f), stage 1, band ">=30 charges" at def2-SVP: worst gap -5.974 eV. A negative
HOMO-LUMO gap is the LUMO lying below the HOMO. That run is inside the corpus D6 saw and the row is
licensed. It is recorded here because nothing in Stage 6 has mentioned it.
