WHAT STAGE 6 PRODUCES
1. G0 and kappa for the preorganisation term of decision O2, or a statement that no threshold could be fixed and the
   term is reported without a penalty (O2's own fallback).
2. The size of the relaxation problem: how far the substrate moves when a frozen design is imposed on it, against a
   no-sphere control, and what that costs in compute (PHASE22_PLAN_UPDATE_20261009.md section 5, "size of the
   relaxation problem").
3. Whether 2 and 3 spheres together collapse onto the substrate or break its bonds, and whether their effects add at
   fixed geometry (the plan's three criteria for this stage).
Nothing Stage 6 produces is a barrier that is quoted as a result. The catalytic verdict remains the relaxed NEB-CI
chain of SP_AMENDMENT2.txt A2.5.

WHEN IT RUNS
Now, in parallel with the SP package (Jessica, 21 October 2026). Stage 6 does not wait for Stage 5 production and
does not read any new package result: it runs on frame 42436, whose results were seen and disclosed in
SP_AMENDMENT2.txt. Its jobs are 8 cores and 24 GB (below), so they do not compete with the package's 16-core,
64-250 GB jobs for the nodes the package needs.

WHY NOT THE DEVELOPMENT SET. Its reactant is NEB band image 0 and its TS is the climbing image; neither is a
stationary point. C4 re-optimised those band reactants and found dE_reopt of -1.84 to -11.56 kcal/mol, which is why
C4 rejected image 0 as a reactant definition. A relaxation from such a start is dominated by the substrate falling
towards its own minimum, and a TS relaxation from a climbing image without a TS search slides downhill. A threshold
measured there would not mean the same thing in production.

THE LIMITATION THIS LEAVES, AND WHAT IS DONE ABOUT IT. Stage 6 calibrates on ONE frame. Decision O2 already records
that applying a 2-3 sphere threshold at up to 20 spheres is an extrapolation; this adds a second, from one frame to
the ensemble. Fixed here in advance: once Stage 5 production exists, G0 is CONFIRMED on the two lowest-numbered
complete frames other than 42436, by re-measuring the same pairs and reporting whether G0 would have changed. It is
NOT re-tuned. If it would have changed by more than the spread of the pairs it was fitted to, that is reported as a
limitation of the short list, and the designs already chosen are not re-ranked.

THE GRID
Built by the committed cs5_grid.py, with the committed floors.tsv, on frame 42436's own R, TS and P - the Stage 5
rule and resolution, applied to one frame. On the PC; no HPC, no new single points. Written to
cs_stage6/sites_f42436.tsv with L to cs_stage6/L_f42436.tsv, and labelled a Stage 6 input, NOT Stage 5 production:
the production grid's surface is the union over every complete frame, so a one-frame grid is tighter than production
and its site numbering is its own. The A matrix for this frame is not rebuilt; the arrangements are placed by the
rule below from the development A matrix's ranking of the corresponding sites, and the frozen effects Stage 6 needs
are computed directly from the single points it runs (below), not from an A column.

THE ARRANGEMENTS (placed by rule, never by reading a barrier)
  S6-2   two spheres: the most barrier-lowering admitted site of this grid, then the next that is at least 3.25 A
         from it, charges at the budget's optimum for those two fixed sites (B as the code's default, 2.0 e^2, which

  S6-3   three spheres by the same rule;
  S6-5, S6-10, S6-20  the committed optimiser's own best design at n = 5, 10 and 20 on this grid

  S6-C   the no-sphere control, relaxed from the same start. This is what WP4 lacked and what makes every number

  S6-P   one bare point charge of +1 at S6-2's first site, as the comparator for what the sphere model buys
         (Jessica, 21 October 2026).
The ranking rule, the spacing and the budget are fixed here; no arrangement is chosen or rejected by the effect it
produces.

THE RELAXATION
WP4's protocol (phase2.2/wp4_s13_rerun/armb_anch/job.inp), which is committed and has run:
  ! B3LYP D3BJ def2-SVP def2/J RIJCOSX TightSCF QMMM Opt
  %maxcore 3000 / %pal nprocs 8 / %scf MaxIter 250 / %geom MaxIter 500
  QMAtoms {0:23}; ActiveAtoms = the substrate only; the spheres fixed in the force field; Cartesian coordinates.
The reactant side uses Opt from r_irc/final.xyz. The TS side uses OptTS from ts/final.xyz followed by frequencies; a
TS that does not return exactly one imaginary mode is reported as such and is not used for a relaxed barrier. The
control S6-C runs the same two jobs with no spheres present. This is Behrens' one-embedding scheme with the embedding
frozen, which PHASE22_PLAN_UPDATE_20261009.md section 4 item 3 already adopted for validation.

THE QUANTITY
For a design D at a geometry X, over the N = 24 substrate atoms:
  g_i(D) = the gradient on atom i with the design's spheres present at X
  g_i(0) = the gradient on atom i with no spheres at the same X
  G_add(X) = sqrt( (1/3N) sum_i |g_i(D) - g_i(0)|^2 )     kcal mol-1 A-1, an RMS
G_add,R and G_add,TS are reported for every arrangement. Decision O2 penalises the reactant one; the TS one is
recorded because it costs nothing once the gradient exists and because both GOCAT authors check all three stationary
points.
THE QUANTITY IS NAMED BECAUSE THE PUBLISHED FIGURES ARE NOT COMPARABLE. Dittner penalises the gradient NORM above
10 kcal mol-1 A-1 and Behrens the RMS above 10; for 24 atoms those differ by sqrt(72) = 8.49, so the same printed
figure is about eight and a half times apart in strictness (LIBRARY_ERRATUM2_20261021.md item 2). Both use the TOTAL
gradient; the ADDED form here is this project's own, adopted because our frames are enzyme geometries in vacuo where
the bare substrate already carries a gradient. Neither published threshold is carried over. For scale only, not as a
bound: a genuinely relaxed GOCAT design reaches gradients "around 1 kcal mol-1 A-1" (Dittner thesis ESI, printed
p. 361) and his frozen-path designs sat at about 11.3 on the norm (printed p. 303).

WHAT IS MEASURED PER ARRANGEMENT
  ddE(frozen)   the design's effect at the fixed geometries, from single points at R and TS with and without the
                spheres - the quantity the MILP's score estimates;
  ddE(relaxed)  the same after the substrate is relaxed with the spheres frozen, R by Opt and TS by OptTS;
  Loss          ddE(frozen) - ddE(relaxed), with the control's own change subtracted so that Loss is the design's
                share and not the substrate's descent from a geometry that is not its own minimum;
  G_add,R and G_add,TS;
  the forming C1-C6 distance before and after, against the control's;
  the substrate's centroid displacement, rotation and internal RMSD, as WP4 reported them;
  the closest sphere-to-substrate-atom distance;
  the largest change in any substrate bond;
  the TS's imaginary mode count after relaxation;
  first-order additivity at fixed geometry: the sum of the one-sphere effects against the measured effect of S6-2 and
  S6-3 (single points only, no relaxation).

THE TWO SURVIVAL CRITERIA, REPORTED SEPARATELY
A design "survives" is reported under both of these, never as one number:
  (i)  EFFECT: ddE(relaxed) is still negative, and Loss is within the tolerance below;
  (ii) GEOMETRY: the relaxed point is still a stationary point of the right kind - no collapse, bonds intact, and for
       the TS exactly one imaginary mode.
Why both: Dittner's carry-over of PM7 designs to PBE0 succeeded for 83-100 % of designs when the question was only
whether the barrier was still lower, and for 2.8-80 % when the stationary points were also required to still be
stationary, the whole gap being the residual gradient (thesis Tables 6.2 and 6.3, printed pp. 164-165). G0 IS FITTED
ON THE STRICTER OF THE TWO, i.e. on arrangements that meet (ii) as well as (i).
FAILURE, both taken from WP4 so neither is invented here: a sphere-to-substrate-atom distance under 1.2 A is
collapse; a substrate bond moving by more than 0.15 A is broken integrity. A collapsed or broken arrangement is
reported and excluded from the fit, with the exclusion stated.

THE TOLERANCE, AND WHERE IT COMES FROM
No published source states a tolerance of the form "at least X per cent of a predicted effect must survive a fuller
treatment"; 21 papers were read in full to establish that, and the nearest things to acceptance criteria are Ryde's
protocol with no number (Methods Enzymol. 2016, section 4 item 7) and two noise-floor arguments (Kastner 2006
p. 459; Cooper & Kastner, Table 1 discussion). The tolerance here is therefore proposed, and the analogy it rests on
is named rather than implied:
  LOSS MUST BE BELOW 15 % OF |ddE(frozen)|.
15 % is not a new constant: it is the fraction decision O6 already requires for the method-and-basis uncertainty
("The fraction to use (for example 15 %) is fixed in the pre-registration"), so the project carries one such number,
fixed once, used in both places. The analogy supporting that value, with every figure checked against the paper:
  - a single frozen or minimised structure sits about 10-15 % from the sampled answer in three different enzymes:
    PHBH 112.3 against 101.5 kJ/mol (Kastner 2006, Table 1, p. 458), TIM 21.9 against 19.8 kcal/mol (Zhang 2000
    p. 3491 against Hu 2007 p. 399), cyclophilin A 87.5 against 75.9 kJ/mol (Cooper & Kastner, Table 1);
  - a frozen-geometry first-order electrostatic model of exactly this project's form, Delta = sum q (V_TS - V_R),
    retains 86.7 %, 98.6 %, 94.2 %, 94.8 % and 92.1 % of the ab initio effect where the effect is of appreciable
    size (Sokalski 1985, Table 2, printed p. 406, column d), an error of 1 to 13 %;
  - this project's own Stage 4c measured 10-31 % with the ranking preserved.
THE FLOOR CLAUSE, which Sokalski's data forces: at his one weakly-interacting site the true effect is
-0.59 kcal/mol and the model gives -2.20, so the absolute error of 1.6 kcal/mol is unremarkable while the ratio is
meaningless. Therefore, if |ddE(frozen)| is smaller than 3.2 kcal/mol - the absolute method-and-basis figure the
records already give, per arrangement across bases (STAGE4C, plan update) - the arrangement's survival is NOT
ASSESSABLE. It is reported as such and does not enter the fit for G0.
REPORTED ALONGSIDE: the frame spread, so a reader who prefers the noise-floor rule of Kastner and of Cooper &
Kastner can apply it to the same numbers. On one frame the paired per-frame interval does not exist, so what is
reported is the absolute method-and-basis figure and the relaxation's own convergence tolerance.

HOW G0 AND kappa ARE FIXED
The form is decision O2's, which is Behrens' Eq. 3.2 (printed p. 21), one-sided and quadratic:
    the re-scoring objective gains   kappa * max(0, G_add,R - G0)^2, summed over frames
(Notation: this project's kappa is Behrens' alpha. In his thesis kappa weights a different term, the
energy-stabilisation penalty of his Eq. 3.3, "usually chosen at around 10^4" - not a value for ours.)
Then, in this order:
1. TEST whether G_add,R predicts Loss at all, over the arrangements that meet both survival criteria and are
   assessable: Spearman rank correlation, reported with its confidence interval. This is the test decision O2 asks
   for.
2. IF IT PREDICTS (the interval excludes zero, in the direction that more pull means more loss):
   G0 = the largest G_add,R whose measured Loss is within the tolerance;
   kappa = the value for which kappa * (G_max - G0)^2 equals the Loss measured at G_max, the largest G_add,R
   observed. The penalty is then an estimate of the effect a design is expected to lose, in the score's own units,
   rather than a free scale factor, and kappa needs no separate choice.
3. IF IT DOES NOT PREDICT: decision O2's fallback, unchanged. No penalty; G_add,R is reported for every design; the
   short list carries the limitation.

DIRECTION
The term Stage 6 calibrates acts AGAINST a lower frozen barrier: it penalises designs that pull hard. Within the
stage, a larger G0 or a smaller kappa is the more permissive choice, towards a lower barrier. Nothing here is chosen
by the barrier any design reaches: the geometries are fixed in advance, the arrangements are placed by rule, the
tolerance and its floor are fixed before any relaxation runs, and kappa follows from the measurement.
For context, and to be stated with the results rather than left implicit: a gradient penalty on a fixed path is a
keep-away term. Behrens, printed p. 67: "The penalty employed to do this is obviously minimized when the charged
particles are further away from the center, weakening the interaction and additional gradient introduced." Because
decision O2 applies the term at re-scoring and not inside the MILP, the pool still contains the close-contact
designs; the bias affects the ranking, and that is stated with the short list.

WHAT STAGE 6 CANNOT SETTLE
1. One frame; the confirmation rule above is the mitigation, not a fix.
2. 2-3 spheres, used at up to 20. Including n = 5, 10 and 20 narrows it, but those designs come from a one-frame
   test grid.
3. Relaxation is not validation. Stage 6 relaxes the substrate with the spheres frozen at fixed geometries; the
   verdict is A2.5's relaxed chain.
4. The spheres stay frozen. Dittner's own view is that the honest fix is relaxing the path inside the search
   (J. Chem. Phys. 152, 114106, p. 114106-6: once the path relaxes, "all criteria referring to gradient norms ...
   are all per constructionem not present anymore"), which this project does not do and cannot afford. Stage 6
   measures the size of the error that choice leaves, which is the most that can be claimed for it.
5. How much of the achievable catalysis a frozen-path search can find at all. Dittner's best static designs reached
   24-41 % of the effect his relaxed-path designs reached (thesis Table 7.1, printed p. 203, with the barriers of
   printed pp. 185, 192 and 195). That is a limitation of this project's whole approach and is stated in the
   write-up, not something Stage 6 can remove.
For the floor of the comparison, and worth keeping with the null distribution of decision O10: none of 1000 random
designs was catalytic at PBE0 in Dittner's test, at either 3 or 10 charges (thesis Table 6.2, printed p. 164).

COST
WP4's armb_anch, the same protocol on one sphere, converged in 46 cycles and 5 h 58 m on 8 cores with 24 GB. The
spheres sit in the force field and not the QM region, so 2 or 3 cost essentially the same as 1. Stage 6 as specified
is 7 arrangements x 2 sides (R and TS) plus the frequency jobs, about 16-20 jobs of that size: of order 60-120
core-hours, requested as 8 cores and 24 GB each.
