### Question 1 — are the charges even or saturated

- Neither. Distinct |q| magnitudes per design: S6-2 has 1, S6-3 has 3, S6-5 has 3, S6-10
  has 5, S6-20 has 7. Only S6-2 is uniform, and only because both its sites sit at a bound.
- They are **proportional to −a**, which is the analytic interior optimum of decision 3's
  L2 budget. q/(−a) over S6-20's twenty sites runs 0.0905 to 0.1413, mean 0.1062; the
  closed form predicts the ratio at 0.1047 and Σaq at −19.5611 against the committed
  −19.51748, i.e. **0.22 per cent**. The designs where it agrees least (S6-2 at 12.9 per
  cent, S6-5 at 17.6) are exactly those with a site pinned at a bound, as the derivation
  says to expect.
- **But at the best sites the charge is not a decision variable at all.** 18 of the 20
  top-ranked single sites in `ARRANGEMENTS.txt` admit `[1.00, 1.00]` — lower bound equal to
  upper. That is why S6-P, S6-2, S6-3 and S6-5 all carry a +1.00 e charge; the site admits
  nothing else. S6-10 and S6-20 use none of the top twenty, because Finding 5's one pocket
  plus the 3.25 Å spacing admits at most one of them.
- Consequence, and it changed the plan: a per-site cap below 1.00 e makes those eighteen
  sites infeasible rather than restrained, and `STAGE5_GRID_DEV` records the floors as
  monotone in q, so the cap and the layer cannot be varied independently.
- Noted from the same closed form: the best score is −√(B·Σa²) over the chosen sites, so
  any site with a ≠ 0 improves it. **Under a pure L2 budget "more charges is better" is an
  algebraic identity, not a chemical finding.**

### Question 2 — why n > 5 goes STRAINED

- The gap falls monotonically with Σ|q| (3.40 → 0.61 eV from n = 0 to 20) while Σq² is flat
  from n = 2 (2.000 → 2.048). So Σq² cannot be what moves it.
- Mechanism read off the raw file rather than inferred: with a single +0.80 e at 2.96 Å the
  HOMO falls 2.94 eV and the LUMO falls **6.05 eV**. The LUMO is pulled down twice as hard
  and the gap closes from 3.573 to 0.472 eV. That is the second-order Stark response — the
  low-lying virtuals have most amplitude where the Coulomb well is deepest — amplified by a
  point charge with no Pauli wall for electrons.

### The literature pass

### The field calibration — the part that does not depend on Diagnostic 6

- Computed E = 14.39965·q/r² V/Å at every substrate atom from the committed sphere centres,
  and projected onto each reacting bond. The script identifies the bonds from the geometry
  rather than by index and recovers the breaking O–C at 1.495 Å and the forming C–C at
  3.225 Å, matching Finding 7.
- Along the breaking C–O the designs give 0.82 to 1.21 V/Å. Siddiqui & Dubey
  (doi:10.1039/d1cp03978d) measure **wild-type BsCM at 0.20 to 0.53 V/Å** on the same bond,
  with R90A at 0.133 and R90G at 0.095, so Arg90's own share is roughly 0.1 to 0.2.
- Site 1690 is modelled on Arg90 and puts +1.00 e at 2.96 Å in vacuo: **1.64 V/Å, about
  eight times Arg90's share.** Cross-check: a guanidinium +1 e at 4–5 Å gives 0.58–0.90 V/Å
  in vacuum, or 0.14–0.22 at ε_r = 4, which brackets the measured value — so the enzyme
  number and Coulomb's law agree and the difference is distance plus screening, both of
  which this model removes.
- **The useful field saturates and the total does not.** From S6-5 to S6-20 the charge
  nearly doubles, E∥(C–O) falls from 1.214 to 1.152, E∥(C–C) rises one per cent, and the
  mean over all 24 atoms climbs from 1.244 to 1.633.

## y04 — Diagnostic 8: the audit re-run over the corpus it should have had (26 October 2026)

Session notes, one bullet per step. The measured output is `DIAGNOSTIC8_RESULT.txt`; the rule it
applies is `STAGE6_DIAGNOSTIC8_20261026.txt`, committed before the script was run. Nothing here is
a result.

### What this step is, and what it deliberately is not

- Erratum 4's open item 1 was "fix and re-run `cs6_audit.py`". It is not done that way. Editing a
  script whose output is a committed, cited result file would silently restate what D6 found, and
  D6 is correct about the corpus it could see. **New code, new pre-registration, new result file.
  `cs6_audit.py` and `DIAGNOSTIC6_RESULT.txt` are untouched.**
- Re-running a diagnostic needs a pre-registration of its own. `STAGE6_DIAGNOSTIC8_20261026.txt`
  was written and committed **before** the script ran, in a commit that carries no result.
- One variable changes and it is the corpus. D6.3's rule is applied unchanged, with every threshold
  imported from `cs6_audit.py` rather than retyped. Whether D6's two unanchored criteria — the
  1.0 eV gap floor and the 2× cycle ratio — should be replaced by SCF stability analysis and FOD is
  the next question and was kept out of this one. Changing the corpus and the rule together would
  have made the comparison uninterpretable.

### Why the fixes are staged, and why two of them are inseparable

- Erratum 4 names four defects. They do not pull the same way, so netting them would hide both
  directions:
  - **E4.4(i)** the control pool included charge-free *geometry optimisations*, against D6.1's own
    statement that a charge-free control is not like-for-like once the geometry has moved. Every
    threshold is anchored to those medians.
  - **E4.4(ii)** the four categories are not disjoint — a QM/MM run that is also an optimisation is
    counted twice — so D6's printed census does not sum to the number of runs kept.
  - **E4.5** the walker opens `*.tar.xz` only; 415 completed runs in loose `.xz` were never read.
  - **E4.6** a run carrying its charges as `Ne>` centres reports no point-charge file, so it was
    filed as a charge-free **control**.
- **E4.5 and E4.6 are one stage, not two.** The walker adds runs, most of which carry `Ne>` charges;
  fixing it alone would push those into the control pool and make the audit *more permissive*. That
  is E4.6's binding constraint and it decided the shape of the script.

### The gate, which is the part that protects the result

- Stage 1 of `cs8_audit.py` is D6 as published. Before any new reading is computed the script
  **rebuilds the entire text of `DIAGNOSTIC6_RESULT.txt` from stage 1 and compares it byte for
  byte** with the committed file. On a mismatch it prints the first differing line and stops, and
  no result is written.
- This exists because `verdict()` could not be imported — it is a closure inside `cs6_audit.main()`
  — so it had to be reproduced, and a reproduction asserted to be faithful is worth nothing. The
  gate is what makes the assertion checkable: if every number D6 printed comes back out of this
  script, the later stages differ from D6 only where a named defect was corrected.
- If the gate fires the harness runs `cs6_audit.py` itself and says which of the two causes it is:
  the corpus has drifted since D6 was run, or this script's copy of D6.3 is wrong. The second is
  mine to fix. The first is a larger finding than Diagnostic 8.

### What was added beyond fixing the defects

- **The charges themselves.** Two committed sources, neither inferred: `Ne>` centres printed in a
  run's own coordinate block, and a `.pc` file committed beside the output. ORCA does not echo a
  `.pc` file into its output, so most point-charge runs have no recoverable magnitudes and **none is
  guessed**; the untestable proportion is reported rather than filled in.
- A `.pc` file is attributed to an output only on an unambiguous match — same stem, or a directory
  holding exactly one output and exactly one `.pc`. The first draft used "any single `.pc` in the
  directory" and in rehearsal it attributed one job's charges to a second job in the same archive.
  **A wrong charge vector is worse than no charge vector**, so the rule was tightened.
- **The peak field**, computed per run from that run's own charges and its own final coordinates.
- **A sha256 signature** over the exact charge vector and the exact geometry to four decimals. If
  two runs share a signature and are classified differently, no function of the charges and the
  geometry can separate the verdicts, and the classifier would be partly reading run-to-run
  variation. That is worth knowing before any threshold is fitted, so it is looked for explicitly.

### The pre-registered test of the one surviving hypothesis

- Erratum 5 left peak local field standing as a hypothesis on seven points, two frames, one basis —
  a bracket, not a threshold. D8.4(h) fixes the test **and the criterion that kills it** before the
  corpus is scanned: healthy is CLEAN or POLARISED, sick is STRAINED or COLLAPSED, and the
  hypothesis survives only if `max(peak |E| over healthy) < min(peak |E| over sick)`. Any overlap
  falsifies it. Per basis, because a threshold needing a different value at each basis would not be
  a property of the design.
- If no basis has both a healthy and a sick run with recoverable charges, **the test does not run**,
  and that is reported as a fact about the record — not as support for the hypothesis.

### Rehearsal

- Three passes against a bare-repo clone, never the live repos. The corpus for rehearsal is
  synthetic: ORCA-shaped outputs built to exercise every branch — controls, charge-free
  optimisations polluting the control pool, `.pc`-file designs, a QM/MM run that is also an
  optimisation, `Ne>` runs inside an archive, `Ne>` runs in loose `.xz`, a committed `.pc` file, a
  plain `.out` outside any archive, a non-converged run and a `.pbs.out` batch log.
- The gate was tested the only way that means anything: the fixture's `DIAGNOSTIC6_RESULT.txt` was
  generated by **the real `cs6_audit.py`**, and `cs8_audit.py` had to reproduce it byte for byte.
  It did, first time, and again after every subsequent change.
- A second fixture carries two runs with an identical charge vector and geometry and opposite
  verdicts, so the falsification branch is exercised and not merely written. Both fixtures were run
  end to end; both left the bare origins unmoved and both repos clean.
- **22 negative tests, all passing**: manifest tamper; dirty repo; ahead of origin; wrong branch;
  criteria sha mismatch; Erratum 5 absent; `cs6_audit.py` absent; two `DIAGNOSTIC6_RESULT.txt`;
  result already present; a *different* Diagnostic 8 pre-registration already present; only one of
  the pre-registration and the script in place; work-log section already present; the gate firing
  three different ways — corpus and record disagreeing, a trailing difference, and a deliberate
  fault planted in `cs8_audit.py`'s own copy of D6.3 — each checked to name the right cause; the
  append path; the resume path; and the code repo staying clean.
- **Two faults the rehearsal caught, both now fixed.** The `.pc` mis-attribution above. And:
  importing `cs6_audit` wrote a `__pycache__` directory into the code repo and left it dirty, which
  is a side effect on a repo this script only reads and would have made the next run refuse.
  `sys.dont_write_bytecode` is now set before the import, the harness runs python with `-B`, and it
  checks the code repo is still clean before committing anything.
- **The limitation to carry:** the fixture is synthetic. The field arithmetic, the signature
  grouping and every guard are exercised, but no rehearsal here can prove the `Ne>` and `.pc`
  patterns match the real ORCA builds in this record. The gate is the protection: if the reader
  behaves differently on the real corpus, the stage-1 rebuild will not match and nothing commits.

### The retraction, and what it cost to find

- `WORKLOG_20261026.md` and `STAGE6_ASSESSMENT2` §12, both committed earlier the same day, state
  that a Dittner quotation was fabricated and "does not exist". **It exists.** The search failed on
  the `ﬁ` ligature (U+FB01) in "speci**ﬁ**cally", and the accompanying claim that ligatures had
  been collapsed was false when written.
- The sequence is worth recording because it repeated twice more in one evening. A plain
  substring screen over the committed documents reported about ten attributed quotations as
  unmatched; on inspection most were two-column line breaks, and two more were OCR
  (`mol21` for `mol⁻¹`, `5` for `=`). **Three separate false accusations from one habit: trusting
  a negative result from a string search over PDF-extracted text.**
- So the fix is not "be more careful". `cs_citecheck.py` is committed with the erratum. It
  normalises ligatures and soft hyphenation, splits elided quotations and checks each fragment,
  falls back to n-gram coverage when exact matching fails, excludes the document a quote came from
  so nothing matches itself, and reports what it cannot match as UNMATCHED rather than as absent.
  The harness refuses to commit if the Dittner quotation is still reported unmatched.

### What the reading established

- **No norm budget exists in the GOCAT literature.** Complete constraint set: fixed N_Ch; box
  bounds `[−1, +1] e`; inter-charge `r_min` (1 Å default, 0.1 and 3.0 Å variants); charges on a vdW
  surface or a 7.5 Å sphere; `Σq = 0` in neutral runs. A mechanical sweep for lasso, ridge,
  sparsity, cardinality, Tikhonov, ℓ1, ℓ2 returns nothing. **Σq² ≤ B is this project's own.**
- **The `Ne>` ECP is Marefat Khah's published Ne "ecp-all", parameter for parameter** — but fitted
  on neutral rare-gas atoms and placed on MM atoms assumed to have "no dangling bonds that might
  serve as electron acceptors". A ±1 e centre is exactly such an acceptor.
- **A compact basis conceals spill-out rather than preventing it**, and GOCAT escapes the problem
  only by running semi-empirical minimal basis. B3LYP/def2-SVP is in neither safe regime. That is
  both the novelty and the risk, and E7.9 says so.
- **D6.3's four indicators have no literature backing** as spill-out diagnostics. The documented
  signatures are orbital localisation on an environment site and collapsed excitation energies.
  Galbraith's **HOMO-composition** test is the anchored replacement, and it is a composition test,
  not an energy test.
- The enzyme field comparison was rebuilt from library sources after three of its four numbers were
