Stage 6, DIAGNOSTIC 24 (3 November 2026).

*** THIS IS NOT A PRE-REGISTERED DIAGNOSTIC. ***

Every other numbered diagnostic in this directory carries the line "Committed before any of its
runs exist." This one cannot. The runs were executed first, in a conversational exploration of
whether a smeared-charge representation would retire the spill-out problem, and this record was
written afterwards from the completed log. It is therefore EXPLORATORY and its status is a scoping
measurement, not a result. If any number below is to be cited in the thesis, D24 must be
re-registered and re-run under the convention the other diagnostics follow. Nothing here may be
quoted as a Stage 6 result in its present form.

The log is d24_smear.log (rows 1-10) and d24_smear_tail.log (rows 11-12), reproduced verbatim in
D24.4. The driver is d24_smear.py. Both are external to the repository as committed and are
attached alongside this record.


D24.1  THE QUESTION

Is the def2-SVP / def2-SVPD discrepancy that DIAGNOSTIC 5 identified a Coulomb-singularity artefact
of representing the design sites as mathematical point charges?

If it is, then replacing each point charge with a Gaussian-smeared charge density of finite width
should reduce the inter-basis gap, because smearing lifts the 1/r singularity at the site centre and
removes the unphysically deep well into which a diffuse basis function can collapse. If it is not,
smearing will leave the gap where it is, and the leakage must be happening somewhere other than at
the site centres.

This question was NOT the fix D5 named. D5.3(c) states that if spill-out is supported "a charge
model with a Pauli wall for electrons is needed before any high-field number means anything", and
D5.4 states explicitly that D5 "does not test a charge model with a Pauli wall for electrons, which
is the fix if spill-out is supported". D24 tests a NON-WALL alternative to that fix. It is a test of
whether the cheaper option would have been sufficient.


D24.2  WHAT WAS MEASURED

ENGINE. pyscf 2.14.0, local, no HPC. B3LYP, density fitting, conv_tol 1e-8, max_cycle 200.
External charges through pyscf.qmmm.itrf.mm_charge, whose `radii` argument replaces each point
charge with a Gaussian of that width; radii=None gives point charges. This is a DIFFERENT CODE AND A
DIFFERENT DISPERSION TREATMENT from the ORCA 6.0.1 B3LYP-D3BJ/def2-J/RIJCOSX pipeline that produced
every committed Stage 6 number. See D24.5.

GEOMETRIES. The committed 24-atom chorismate dianion at frame 42436, reactant and transition state,
read from cs_stage6/frame_42436/reactant_qm.xyz and transition_state_qm.xyz. Charge -2, singlet.
Fixed geometry throughout; nothing was relaxed.

CHARGE VECTORS. Three committed vectors spanning and exceeding the measured domain:
    1.3000e_n10   10 sites, sum|q| = 1.3000 e   inside the domain
    2.8800e_n10   10 sites, sum|q| = 2.8800 e   at the edge, and the vector at which D7 Reading 3

    5.6433e_n20   20 sites, sum|q| = 5.6433 e   outside, and above the 4.32 e instability onset

CHARGE MODELS. point (radii=None), and Gaussian at sigma = 0.25, 0.50, 1.00 Angstrom.

BASES. def2-svp and def2-svpd. 52 SCF solutions in total.

METRIC. ddE = [E(TS,q) - E(TS,0)] - [E(R,q) - E(R,0)], in kcal/mol, which is offset-invariant: any
constant error common to the four energies cancels exactly. The reported quantity is

D24.5  WHAT THIS CANNOT TEST

It cannot say anything about the ORCA pipeline's absolute numbers. Different code, different
dispersion treatment, and an unreconciled 0.3842 Eh offset on the bare energies.

It does not test a Pauli wall. The ECP arms of DIAGNOSTIC 23 do that. D24 tests only whether
smearing alone would have substituted for one.

One frame, one design geometry, fixed nuclei, three charge vectors, three widths. It cannot
localise the gap in sum|q| between 1.3000 e and 2.8800 e, where the D5.3(d) boundary now falls.

It says nothing about def2-TZVPD, so it cannot say the def2-SVPD numbers are themselves converged -
the same limitation D5.4 records.

It says nothing about additivity, about Loss, or about whether b varies between sites.


D24.6  READINGS

READING 1. SMEARING DOES NOT CLOSE THE GAP. At 1.3000 e the gap falls from 0.5184 to 0.4807 as
sigma goes from 0 to 1.00 Angstrom, a reduction of 7.3 per cent. At 2.8800 e it does not fall at
all: 1.0847 at point, 1.0927 at sigma = 1.00, having risen to 1.1222 at sigma = 0.50. At 5.6433 e
the reduction from point to sigma = 0.25 is 1.3 per cent against a gap of 13.5 kcal/mol. No width
tested brings any failing cell to the D5.3(d) criterion.

READING 2. THEREFORE THE LEAKAGE IS NOT AT THE SITE CENTRES. A Gaussian of width sigma is
numerically indistinguishable from a point charge at distances beyond roughly 3 sigma. The design
sites sit on the van der Waals union surface, further from the QM density than 3 Angstrom. The
smeared potential therefore differs from the point potential only in a region the QM density barely
occupies, and the measurement is consistent with that: the effect is small, and it is largest at the
smallest sum|q| where the density is least distorted. The inter-basis discrepancy is not a
singularity artefact. It is diffuse-tail over-polarisation in a region with no repulsive wall.

This is what the authors of the pseudopotential method state in print. Marefat Khah, Reinholdt,
Olsen, Kongsted and Hattig (acs.jctc.9b01162) list Gaussian-smeared charge densities among the
approaches that "can cure" electrostatic singularities and charge penetration, and then rule the
class out for this problem: "However, these methods do not capture the effects of the Pauli exchange
repulsion on the electronic wavefunction." D24 is a measurement of that sentence on this system.

READING 3. THE D5.3(d) BOUNDARY, STATED CORRECTLY. D5.3(d) asks for the largest point at which
|ddE(def2-SVPD) - ddE(def2-SVP)| <= 1.0 kcal/mol. Of the three vectors run:

    1.3000 e   0.5184   PASSES
    2.8800 e   1.0847   FAILS  (marginally, by 0.0847)
    5.6433 e  13.5154   FAILS  (by an order of magnitude)

The boundary therefore lies BETWEEN 1.3000 e and 2.8800 e and has not been localised. It is NOT at
or above 2.8800 e. Any statement that the committed def2-SVP numbers are basis-converged up to
2.8800 e, or "to about 2.9 e", is wrong: 2.8800 e fails the criterion. Localising the boundary needs
a scan between the two, which D24 did not run.

Note what this costs. 2.8800 e is the vector at which D7 Reading 3 matched S6-10 against S6-20 to
0.0116 kcal/mol - the total-field-only result that the design argument rests on. That match is a
def2-SVP result and D7 Reading 3 is unaffected by anything here. But the def2-SVP number AT that
vector is not basis-converged by D5.3(d), and the two facts must be reported together.

READING 4. THE DOSE-RESPONSE IS MONOTONIC AND CORROBORATES D20. 0.52, 1.08, 13.52 kcal/mol at
1.3000, 2.8800 and 5.6433 e. The collapse between the second and third is far steeper than linear
and brackets the 4.32 e instability onset DIAGNOSTIC 20 reports from an independent measurement.
Two instruments, two quantities, same boundary region.


D24.7  OUTSTANDING

Two cells of the twelve are not computed: 5.6433e_n20 at sigma = 0.50 and sigma = 1.00. The run that
would have produced them was stopped at its execution time limit after completing ten. They sit at
the vector that is already outside the domain of validity and already failing by an order of
magnitude, and the two cells adjacent to them move the gap by 1.3 per cent, so they are not expected
to change any reading above. THEY ARE NONETHELESS MISSING AND ARE NOT TO BE REPORTED AS COMPLETE.
When they land they are to be appended here as D24 NOTE 1 with the log, not substituted into D24.4.

Also outstanding: the 0.3842 Eh bare-energy discrepancy of D24.3, which must be diagnosed before
re-registration.


D24.8  WHAT FOLLOWS, AND WHAT DOES NOT

What follows: smearing is not a route to retiring the def2-SVPD problem, and the cheaper substitute
for a Pauli wall does not work on this system. The wall arms of DIAGNOSTIC 23 remain the only tested
remedy in the record.

What does not follow: nothing about whether the committed def2-SVP numbers are right. D24 measures
the DISAGREEMENT between two bases. It does not establish which of them is closer to the truth, and
D5.4's limitation stands - without def2-TZVPD neither basis is shown to be converged.
