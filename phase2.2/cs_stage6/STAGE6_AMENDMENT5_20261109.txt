STAGE 6 - AMENDMENT 5: the wavefunction stability criterion
9 November 2026. Decided by Jessica on 9 November 2026.

Nothing already committed is edited by this amendment and no committed verdict is rewritten by
it. It changes what the criterion means from here on, states its scope exactly, and names the
one reader whose use of the first verdict is CORRECT and must not be changed.


A5.1  THE CONTRADICTION

Two questions have been answered by one field.

   (i)  Is the solution that the reported numbers came from a stable solution?
   (ii) Did the first SCF solution need correcting, and did the correction move anything?

Both have been read off stability["first"], the verdict of the first stability analysis. That
was sound while the production head carried no STABPerform, because then the first solution
was the only solution and the two questions had the same answer. DIAGNOSTIC 20's
pre-registration says so at line 84:

   "The verdict of a run is the FIRST stability analysis, which by GATE 3 is the production
   solution. A second analysis, where ORCA reconverged, is reported with it."

DIAGNOSTIC 31 put STABPerform on by default. From that point the two questions separate: the
reported numbers come from the reconverged solution, so (i) is answered by the LAST verdict,
while (ii) still needs the first. Carried over unexamined, the single field answers (ii) and
is then used as though it answered (i).

The consequence is recorded in STAGE6_DIAGNOSTIC32_ERRATUM1_20261109.txt.


A5.2  THE RULE FROM HERE

A reader that issues a RELIABILITY verdict on a run - whether its reported quantities may be
used - judges stability on the LAST stability analysis in that run, which is the solution
every reported quantity was computed from.

A first-pass instability that was then corrected is NOT a reliability flag. It is recorded, as
a fact about the SCF landscape at that condition, with the first verdict, the number of
negative eigenvalues and the lowest eigenvalue. It may be reported, counted and discussed. It
may not by itself make a condition UNRELIABLE.

A run whose LAST verdict is unstable - no restart, or a restart that stayed unstable - is
flagged, as before. No run in the present record is in that state: across all 208 runs that
carry a stability analysis, in d19, d20, d21, d22, d30, d31 and d32, zero end unstable.

Any reader that applies this must expose both fields. cs6_d30.py and cs6_d31.py already record
"first" and "last"; cs6_d32.py records only "first" and must be given "last" before it is used
again.


A5.3  WHAT THIS DOES NOT TOUCH, AND WHY

DIAGNOSTIC 30 IS EXEMPT AND ITS READER MUST NOT BE CHANGED. cs6_d30.py decides arm B with

   unstable = [k for k, c in armB["conditions"].items() if c["stability"]["first"] == "UNSTABLE"]
   mat      = [k for k, b in bars.items() if b["material"]]
   if not unstable:  "ALL STABLE"
   elif not mat:     "UNSTABLE, IMMATERIAL"
   else:             "UNSTABLE, MATERIAL"

DIAGNOSTIC 30's question is (ii), not (i): it asks whether a first-pass instability exists and
whether correcting it moves the barrier. Its use of the first verdict is therefore correct. If
the last verdict were substituted, both of its restarted runs would read stable, "unstable"
would be empty, the first branch would fire and "mat" would never be consulted - turning its
committed verdict UNSTABLE, MATERIAL into ALL STABLE and erasing the finding that the
reconvergence moved a barrier by more than TOL at 5.6433e_n20. This amendment does not apply
to a diagnostic whose question is (ii).

DIAGNOSTIC 19 AND DIAGNOSTIC 20 ARE NOT IN ERROR. Both pre-registered the first-verdict rule
with the justification quoted in A5.1, which held when they were written. Their verdicts stand
as committed and this amendment is not retrospective on them.

DIAGNOSTIC 21 AND DIAGNOSTIC 22 ARE UNAFFECTED IN FACT. None of their 48 runs restarted, so
the first and last verdicts are the same verdict and no reading of theirs can differ.

DIAGNOSTIC 31 IS UNAFFECTED IN ITS VERDICTS. cs6_d31.py gates on the first verdict and so
falls under A5.2, but re-reading its committed tree under the corrected rule changes 0 of its
30 condition verdicts and leaves the iso-total families unchanged, because every one of its
conditions is independently flagged on relocation. Its reader is corrected for correctness,
not to change a result.

phase2.2/cs6_audit.py IS NOT AMENDED. It contains no stability logic. The rule lives in the
individual readers, duplicated, which is how it drifted.


A5.4  WHAT IS NOT DECIDED HERE

This amendment does not re-read anything and issues no verdict. DIAGNOSTIC 32's committed
verdict stands. The recomputation in ERRATUM 1 is post hoc, is labelled as such there, and is
not promoted to a verdict by this amendment - a re-reading whose outcome is already known
cannot be pre-registered, and none is claimed.

It also does not address the binding constraint the erratum exposes. At n >= 5 the SCF needs
level shifting or TRAH, which makes the gap leg unreadable, so those conditions are
INCONCLUSIVE under the corrected rule rather than RELIABLE. Obtaining a verdict there needs
either a convergence route that does not shift, or a criterion to stand in for the gap. That
is a new diagnostic and it is pre-registered when it is designed, not here.
