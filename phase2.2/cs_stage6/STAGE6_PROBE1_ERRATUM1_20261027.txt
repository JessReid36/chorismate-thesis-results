THE BINDING READING IS WITHDRAWN. THE COST MEASUREMENT STANDS.


P1E.1  WHAT WAS WRONG

PROBE1_RESULT.txt reports a column headed "bound?" whose entries are the sign of the B3LYP HOMO
energy:

   run        QM atoms   HOMO / eV   bound?
   w00_R            24     +1.2269   NO
   w06_R            42     +0.2926   NO
   w12_R            60     -0.0754   yes
   w20_R            84     +0.0424   NO
   w06_TS           42     +0.3055   NO

THAT COLUMN IS NOT A BINDING TEST AND MUST NOT BE READ AS ONE. It is withdrawn. The HOMO values
themselves are retained as reported quantities.

The sign of a DFT HOMO is not the criterion for whether an anion is electronically bound. This
project established that five days before the probe was written, and acted on it. Diagnostic 2's
committed result, 22 October, on the vertical detachment energy at the reactant geometry:

   C1  def2-SVP      VDE  -0.3167 eV   -> METASTABLE to electron loss at this condition
   C3  ma-def2-SVP   VDE  +0.5123 eV   -> BOUND at this condition
   C5  def2-SVPD     VDE  +0.5013 eV   -> BOUND at this condition

and its own words beneath them:

   "A positive VDE means the species is bound despite a positive HOMO, and Finding 8's second half
    must be withdrawn as an artefact of reading Koopmans' theorem off a DFT orbital (D2.2, and
    Rienstra-Kiracofe et al. 2002 p. 246 / Galbraith & Schaefer 1996 p. 863, both held in
    chorismate-refs)."

STAGE6_ERRATUM7_20261026 E7.10, written the day before the probe, says the same thing from the
source: Galbraith & Schaefer "warn that a positive HOMO energy is not the test, since 'the physical
significance of the orbitals obtained from density functional methods is questionable'."

So a finding had already been withdrawn in this project for exactly this error, and the erratum
recording the authority for it had been written the previous day. Probe 1 repeated it anyway.


P1E.2  THE SUBSTANTIVE CONSEQUENCE, WHICH IS LARGER THAN THE PROBE

At ma-def2-SVP in vacuo - the basis Diagnostic 10 and Diagnostic 11 run in - THE BARE CHORISMATE
DIANION IS BOUND, VDE +0.5123 eV, measured and committed on 22 October.

Three statements elsewhere in the record rest on the HOMO instead and are superseded on the right
quantity. None of them is deleted; each is corrected here.

1. SOLVATION_DECISION_NOTE.md section 2, 29 September: "The bare chorismate dianion is
   electronically unbound in vacuum at both basis sets." Written on HOMO energies, before
   Diagnostic 2 existed. On the VDE the species is bound at ma-def2-SVP and at def2-SVPD, and
   metastable only at def2-SVP.

2. STAGE6_DIAGNOSTIC10 and its result: the reading that "the continuum did its job" because the
   control's HOMO moved from +1.2269 eV to -4.3036 eV. The continuum did lower the HOMO; that is
   not evidence it bound anything that was not already bound. D10's VERDICT is unaffected - it
   turns on sign reversal and ranking, not on binding - and STAGE6_AMENDMENT2's three grounds for
   removing CPCM are unaffected, since none of them is a binding argument.

3. PHASE22_TESTING_PLAN_20261027.md defect 3, "Unbound dianion, control HOMO +1.2269 eV.
   Established, unresolved." WITHDRAWN. It was never established on the right observable. The
   corrected plan is committed with this erratum.

THE SEQUENCING CONSEQUENCE. The environment step was blocking because the density had to be bound
by something and the continuum had just been removed. On the VDE there is nothing to fix at
ma-def2-SVP, so explicit water is no longer a precondition for proceeding. A gas-phase reference is
independently defensible for this reaction - Mulholland (b508181e): "We use the gas-phase profiles
as a convenient and meaningful reference" - and Li & Chan 2025 practise it. The move to explicit
water remains worth making on Shaik's screening argument, which is a separate case and not one
this erratum settles.

A STING WORTH RECORDING. The condition the surrogate's a coefficients were fitted in, def2-SVP in
vacuo, is the one condition of the three where the dianion is METASTABLE (-0.3167 eV). Adding
diffuse functions makes it more bound, not less.


P1E.3  WHAT STANDS

The cost measurement, which is what the probe was asked for, and which is unaffected by the
observable question:

   24 QM atoms   1 m 38 s        2.2 GB peak against a 24 GB request
   42 QM atoms   3 m 23 s
   60 QM atoms   5 m 03 s
   84 QM atoms   7 m 24 s

Near-linear in atom count: 3.5 times the atoms for 4.5 times the time. Explicit water at this
level is cheap. Eight single points in a twenty-water shell is about an hour. These are single
points on fixed geometries; relaxation in a shell is a different and much larger cost and is not
measured here.

The shell construction also stands and is reusable: six waters between 2.592 and 2.949 A with the
seventh at 4.192 A, a 1.243 A gap defining the hydrogen-bonded first shell by rule rather than by
choice. And the observation that the real first shell is mostly protein - 63 non-water against 17
water atoms within 3.5 A - stands and remains the reason substrate-plus-waters is a device and not
a model of the active site.


P1E.4  THE REPLACEMENT

STAGE6_PROBE2_20261027.txt asks the question that is actually open: Diagnostic 2 measured the VDE
of the BARE substrate, and nobody has measured it with a designed charge present. Dreuw & Cederbaum
(cr0104227) give the reason to care - "at short distances the monoanion-point charge system
represents a resonance state that can in principle not be correctly computed with bound state
methods" - and the designs carry negative charges near a dianion.


P1E.5  THE STANDING LESSON

Two errors of the same shape in two days: a failed grep read as absence (E7, and again on 27
October over a hyphen), and now a disqualified observable used because it was the convenient one in
the output. Both were already documented in this project's own record before they were repeated.

THE RULE: BEFORE A DIAGNOSTIC IS WRITTEN, THE OBSERVABLE IT TURNS ON IS CHECKED AGAINST THE
ERRATA. A quantity this project has already withdrawn a finding over does not re-enter as a verdict
column.
