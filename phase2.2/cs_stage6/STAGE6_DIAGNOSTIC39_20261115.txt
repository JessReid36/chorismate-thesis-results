1.  WHAT IS OPEN
==============================================================================

Diagnostic 38 decided which of its two arms could be read, and it decided it on the
Hartree-Fock highest occupied orbital. B1 at -4.8267 eV was interpretable; A1 at
+0.3539 eV was not, and G38.10 reported it NOT INTERPRETABLE rather than dropping it.
That was the right rule to have written in advance, and the orbital energy is the only
thing a bound-state coupled-cluster output offers for the purpose. It is a
Koopmans-style proxy.

The observable this project actually uses for boundedness is not the orbital energy. It
is the vertical detachment energy, and three committed probes measure it:

   STAGE6_PROBE1_ERRATUM1_20261027.txt, the bare dianion at three bases
   vde_probe/PROBE2_RESULT.txt, a single external charge of each sign
   vde_designs/PROBE3_RESULT.txt, four committed designs

Every one of those runs is DFT. The inputs are checked, not recalled:

   qscan/VDE/C5_mono_R/job.inp     ! B3LYP D3BJ def2-SVPD def2/J RIJCOSX TightSCF
   vde_probe/ctl_mono/job.inp      ! B3LYP D3BJ ma-def2-SVP def2/J RIJCOSX TightSCF
   vde_designs/S6-2_mono/job.inp   ! B3LYP D3BJ ma-def2-SVP def2/J RIJCOSX TightSCF

Three searches of this repository for a charge -1 input at a correlated level returned
nothing. I could not find one. So:

   The project's own boundedness observable has never been computed at the level the
   D36 benchmark is built on, and D38's interpretability decision rests on a proxy
   for it rather than on the observable itself.

D39 computes it. Six single points, one geometry, and the dianion halves are not re-run
because D38 already measured them.

STAGE6_PROBE2_20261027.txt states it:

==============================================================================
4.  TWO ROUTES, AND WHY BOTH
==============================================================================

ROUTE M, the difference of two total energies.

   The monoanion is run at charge -1, multiplicity 2, and the VDE is the difference
   against D38's committed dianion. The ORCA 6.1.1 manual supports this directly:

      "The perturbative triples correction referred to as, (T), is also available for
      the open-shell species."

      "The DLPNO-CCSD(T) method is applicable to closed-shell or high-spin open-shell
      species."

      "Since ORCA 4.2, the improved iterative perturbative triples correction for
      open-shell DLPNO-CCSD is available as well."

      "The open-shell versions are about twice as expensive as the corresponding
      closed-shell versions."

   A doublet monoanion is a high-spin open-shell species, so it qualifies. The cost
   statement is why the walltime below is what it is. The version statement is for the
   iterative variant (T1); the plain (T) sentence carries no version, and the cluster
   runs 6.0.1, so plain (T) on an open-shell reference is taken as available there by
   inference from the 4.2 statement rather than from a 6.0-manual quote. Section 9
   pre-registers what is recorded if that inference is wrong.

   This route gives a full CCSD(T) detachment energy, which is the quantity asked for.

   A CORRECTION, RECORDED HERE BECAUSE THE DESIGN RESTED ON IT.  In conversation on
   15 November I said that the manual warns of a "somewhat unbalanced treatment of the
   closed- and open-shell states in some cases" for open-shell DLPNO-CCSD, and that
   this bears directly on a detachment energy because a detachment energy is exactly a
   closed-shell-minus-open-shell difference. The phrase is in the manual. It is not
   about this method. The manual applies it to the older UHF-LPNO scheme, attributes it
   to screening weak pairs with semi-canonical UMP2 pair energies, and says the
   open-shell DLPNO approach was designed to avoid that problem. I had attributed a
   predecessor's documented weakness to its replacement. Route M is in better standing
   than I told her, and this correction is on the record because the two-route design
   below was argued for partly on a claim that was wrong.

   What does survive is general and is not specific to DLPNO:

      "an UHF reference wavefunction suffers from spin-contamination which can spoil
      the results"

   with quasi-restricted orbitals offered as the remedy. That is a real concern for any
   UHF-referenced open-shell treatment, including this one. D39 does not assume it away
   and does not assume it bites. It measures it: every route M output prints the
   expectation value of S squared against the ideal doublet value, and R39.5 reports
   both.

ROUTE X, the detachment computed directly, with no open-shell reference at all.

   IP-EOM-DLPNO-CCSD starts from the closed-shell dianion and computes the ionisation
   as an excitation of the coupled-cluster wavefunction. There is no UHF reference, so
   spin contamination cannot enter. The keyword line is documented in the ORCA 6.0
   manual, which is the version on the cluster:

      ! IP-EOM-DLPNO-CCSD TightSCF NORMALPNO def2-SVP def2-SVP/C def2/J RIJCOSX

   with nroots set in the %mdci block.

   It is CCSD-based. It produces no (T), so it cannot give the CCSD(T) number and is
   not a substitute for route M.

WHAT THE PAIR SETTLES THAT NEITHER DOES ALONE.

   Route M prints E(CCSD) as well as E(CCSD(T)), and so does every committed D38
   dianion output. So route M yields a CCSD detachment energy as well as a CCSD(T) one.
   Route X yields a CCSD detachment energy by a formalism with no open-shell reference.
   Those two CCSD numbers are the same physical quantity computed two ways, one through
   a UHF reference and one not.

   If they agree, the spin contamination that the manual warns about is not affecting
   this quantity at this geometry, and route M's CCSD(T) number carries that support.
   If they disagree, the disagreement is the measurement of the problem and R39.6
   reports it rather than a preference between them.

   This is the reason for the second route. It is a control on the first, not an
   independent estimate competing with it, and section 10 states that it cannot promote
   itself into one.

5.  THE SIX RUNS
==============================================================================

   run    route  charge  mult  embedding charge                  method keyword
   M_N    M        -1     2    none                              DLPNO-CCSD(T)
   M_B1   M        -1     2    +1.000000 e at site 1690          DLPNO-CCSD(T)
   M_A1   M        -1     2    -1.000000 e at site 678           DLPNO-CCSD(T)
   X_N    X        -2     1    none                              IP-EOM-DLPNO-CCSD
   X_B1   X        -2     1    +1.000000 e at site 1690          IP-EOM-DLPNO-CCSD
   X_A1   X        -2     1    -1.000000 e at site 678           IP-EOM-DLPNO-CCSD

Everything else is held at D38's values and is not a choice here, because the dianion
halves of these differences are D38's committed outputs and the basis treatment must be
the same on both sides of every subtraction:

   def2-SVPD AutoAux TightSCF TightPNO, %maxcore 6000, %pal nprocs 16,
   %scf MaxIter 300

The charge files are not regenerated. d38/B1/charge.pc and d38/A1/charge.pc are copied
byte for byte, and a gate requires the copies to be identical to the committed
originals. Nothing about the charge arrangements is re-derived, re-decoded or
re-decided in D39; D38 did that and its gates checked it against each design's own
site.ORCAFF.prms and combined.xyz.

ORDER.  M_N, M_B1, M_A1, X_N, X_B1, X_A1. Route M first because it is the primary and
because M_N is the one run with an exact-basis committed comparator, so the first number
out of the job is the one that can be checked hardest. X_N before the other two X runs
for the reason in section 9.

SEQUENTIAL, ONE JOB.  Each run's scratch is cleared before the next starts, so the
footprint is one run at a time.

SCRATCH, SIZED FROM A FIGURE THIS BUNDLE HAD TO CORRECT FIRST.  D38's result reports
its scratch peak as 11 MB and says that figure "is what a later job should use". It is
not the peak. The measurement was taken after ORCA returned, so it recorded the residue
a finished run leaves behind. d38/PROGRESS.txt, committed in the same commit, samples
every ten minutes and records used=31G during the first run and 27 to 29 GB at the
start of each of the others. STAGE6_DIAGNOSTIC38_RESULT_ERRATUM1_20261115.txt, in this
bundle, withdraws the instruction and states the corrected bound:

   31 GB <= true peak < 43 GB, per run, closed-shell def2-SVPD on this system

D39 sizes from that and not from 11 MB. The manual puts open-shell runs at "about twice
as expensive as the corresponding closed-shell versions", so the floor is set at 150 GB
asked for and 100 GB as the lowest a candidate may offer and still be used. That is
above twice the corrected upper bound, and a candidate below it is refused in the first
minute of the job rather than discovered three hours in.

   This rules out $TMPDIR on this cluster, which held 38 GB on comp050 and 43 GB on
   comp056. D39 therefore needs /scratch-small-local to be genuinely available on
   whichever node PBS gives it. On comp056 it was full; on comp050 it had 1.5 TB free.
   If the node PBS picks cannot offer 100 GB the job stops at once with the node named,
   which is a resubmission and not a failed calculation.

D39 also fixes the measurement rather than inheriting it: the background watcher that
writes PROGRESS.txt carries the maximum it sees while each run is alive, and the
post-run measurement is still taken but is reported separately and under its own name,
so the peak and the residue cannot be confused again.

==============================================================================
9.  CONTINGENCIES, WRITTEN BEFORE ANY OUTPUT EXISTS
==============================================================================

These are recorded outcomes. None of them is a failure to be worked around, and none
licenses changing a run to get a number.

C39.1  OPEN-SHELL (T) NOT AVAILABLE IN 6.0.1.  If a route M run terminates with the
       triples unavailable for an open-shell reference, or terminates normally without
       printing E(CCSD(T)), then R39.3 is not produced and the detachment energy is
       reported at the CCSD level only, from R39.4. Both sides print E(CCSD), so that
       number exists either way. The result says plainly that the CCSD(T) detachment
       energy was not obtained and why, and the inference in section 4 is recorded as
       having been wrong.

C39.2  ROUTE X REFUSING THE AUXILIARY BASIS.  The documented example names an explicit
       correlation-fitting set, def2-SVP/C. D39 uses def2-SVPD with AutoAux instead,
       because the dianion side of every subtraction is D38's committed output and that
       is what D38 used; matching it is not a preference but a condition of the
       comparison being exact. The def2 series has no def2-SVPD/C, so substituting a
       named set would mean choosing one, and that choice is not made here. If X_N
       refuses the auxiliary basis or refuses AutoAux, X_B1 and X_A1 are not attempted,
       the result records the refusal with ORCA's own message, and route M stands alone
       with its spin contamination reported from R39.5 but uncontrolled. X_N runs
       before the other two X runs for this reason.

C39.3  SCF FAILURE ON AN UNBOUND SPECIES.  If a run's SCF does not converge in 300
       cycles, that run is reported as not converged with its cycle count, and no
       energy is read from it. Its system then has no detachment energy from that
       route. The diagnostic continues for the other systems.

C39.4  A1's DETACHMENT ENERGY IS EXPECTED TO BE NEGATIVE, AND IS NOT A CLEAN
       OBSERVABLE.  D38 measured A1's dianion highest occupied orbital at +0.3539 eV
       and G38.10 called it NOT INTERPRETABLE on that ground. If the detachment energy
       confirms it, the number is the size of the problem rather than a property of a
       bound state: it is a bound-state method's description of what Dreuw and
       Cederbaum call a resonance, and basis-set dependence of such a number is
       expected rather than anomalous. R39.8 will report it with that label attached
       and the result will not place it beside the bound values without it. A negative
       A1 does not retract G38.10; it is the observable agreeing with the proxy. If
       instead A1 comes out bound, that is the observable disagreeing with the proxy,
       and the result says so and says what follows for D38's reading: the proxy was
       the wrong test, G38.10 excluded an arm it should not have, and that is a finding
       about D38's rule rather than a licence to reinterpret D38's numbers here.


==============================================================================
10.  WHAT D39 DOES NOT DO
==============================================================================

NO BARRIER (D39.1).  Six single points at one geometry. No transition state is run,
nothing is subtracted along a reaction coordinate, and the harvest carries no
hartree-to-kcal factor so that nothing of the sort can be assembled from it. G39.12
enforces that.

IT REVISITS NO D34 VERDICT (D39.2).  ADD-s1690_qp10000 and ADD-s678_qm10000 keep
whatever reliability classification D34 gave them. A detachment energy is not a
reliability screen and does not feed one.

NO DESIGN CONCLUSION (D39.3).  Nothing here says either arrangement is a good design,
a better design, or a design worth pursuing. Two arrangements were chosen because D38
already ran their dianions, not because they are candidates.

IT DOES NOT LICENSE THE ddE TEST (D39.4).  D38 licensed that, by showing the embedding
charge reaches the correlated step. D39 neither extends nor withdraws that licence.

ROUTE X CANNOT PROMOTE ITSELF (D39.5).  Route X is a control on route M's open-shell
reference and nothing else. If the two CCSD numbers disagree, that is reported as a
disagreement. Route X's number is not substituted for route M's, and route M's is not
adjusted toward route X's.

ONE GEOMETRY, AND IT IS THE REACTANT (D39.6).  Every committed VDE in this project is
at the reactant and so is this one. Nothing here says whether boundedness changes along
the reaction coordinate. Probes 2 and 3 both state the same limitation and it is
unchanged.

ONE BASIS (D39.7).  def2-SVPD only. Probe 1 measured a sign flip between def2-SVP and
def2-SVPD on this very quantity, so basis dependence here is known to be large and is
not characterised by a single-basis run.

IT DOES NOT COMPARE TO EXPERIMENT (D39.8).  E7.11 forbids it and nothing here is a
free energy or a barrier in any case.

NOTHING WAS SHAPED (D39.9).  The method, basis, auxiliary treatment, SCF settings and
geometry are D38's, unchanged, because the dianion halves are D38's outputs. The two
arrangements are D38's. The convention and the constant are the committed probes'. The
comparators are quoted from committed documents with their bases named. The only new
choices in D39 are the charge and multiplicity of the monoanion, which the convention
fixes, and the decision to run a second route as a control, which section 4 argues for
and section 10 limits.
