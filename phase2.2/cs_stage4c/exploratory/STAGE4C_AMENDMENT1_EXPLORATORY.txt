Stage 4c Amendment 1 - EXPLORATORY note (10 October 2026). Not a pre-registered test: written after
STAGE4C_AMENDMENT1_REPORT.txt was seen. The verdicts stand as recorded there: check 0 PASS; L1 against L3, corrected:
k 0.849, residual 1.831, rho 0.976, signs agree -> RANKING ONLY; representation check, L1 against L0, corrected:
k 0.990, residual 0.284, rho 1.000, max |diff| 0.739 -> PASS. Every number below is in
STAGE4C_AMENDMENT1_EXPLORATORY_REPORT.txt (cs4c_amend1_explore.py on the committed outputs).

1. The zero-charge term behaves as a steric term, and removing it works. In all 18 cases (six site sets, three
   bases) it has the same sign as the sphere's LJ term, at 18-56% of its size. Marefat Khah et al. 2020 introduce
   the pseudopotentials to put the environment's Pauli repulsion on the electrons, which LJ potentials represent
   only mechanically, on the nuclei. So the uncorrected pseudopotential levels carried part of the steric effect
   inside the QM energy, where L1 has none (L1 keeps it outside, in LJ); an energy using both would count part of
   it twice. With the term removed, the representation check moves from residual 0.996 (max 1.72) to 0.284 (max
   0.74). L0 and L1 differ only in the representation, so at def2-SVP the corrected pseudopotential sphere reproduces
   the bare sphere's electrostatic and polarisation effect within 0.74 kcal/mol for these eight arrangements.
   What this cannot show: the same at def2-SVPD, where the bare sphere cannot be run (it traps density; Stages 4 and
   4b). The part not removed by the correction (the charge-pseudopotential cross term, residual 0.28 here) could be
   larger with diffuse functions. The def2-SVPD check (rule 2) is the best available, not directly validated.

2. Correction to STAGE4C_EXPLORATORY.txt item 3 and item 6 (second bullet), and to the plan's Stage 4c update
   ("make catalytic arrangements slightly less catalytic"). Those were read from uncorrected values. Corrected,
   diffuse functions at fixed representation (L2 against L0):
   - the average stands: k 0.952, about 5% (0.94 uncorrected);
   - the uniformity does not. E3, E5 and E6 become less catalytic by 1.2-1.3 kcal/mol (19%, 4%, 7%); E2 and E8 move
     by under 0.1; E1 becomes slightly more catalytic (0.29); E4 and E7 become less anti-catalytic (0.62 and 2.42;
     16% and 13%). Counted strictly, five of the six catalytic arrangements still move up, two of them negligibly;
   - the apparent uniformity came from the zero-charge term itself depending on the basis: z(L2) - z(L0) is +0.48 for
     S1 and +0.90 for S8, which the uncorrected comparison added to E1, E2 and E8.
   Corrected reading: diffuse functions change the barrier change by about 5% on average, unevenly - from under 1%
   to about 19% per arrangement - and not always in the same direction.

3. Where the diffuse effect sits (a grouping chosen after the numbers were seen). Using the committed nearest-atom
   column of arrangements.tsv: the five arrangements with a sphere whose nearest substrate atom is a C10 carboxylate
   oxygen (O5 or O6; E3-E7) carry it - RMS of L2 corr - L0 corr 1.48 kcal/mol, max 2.42. The three without (E1, E2,
   E8; spheres nearest H3, the ether O3 and the hydroxyl O4) give 0.17, max 0.29. This fits the general guidance
   that anions need diffuse functions (Ryde 2016; Rappoport & Furche 2010, who add that their moderate augmentation
   may not suffice for small anions), since the dianion's formal charges sit on its two carboxylates. Limits: eight
   arrangements in six site sets; four of the five share site 232 and the fifth (E4) uses site 189, so "next to the
   C10 carboxylate" cannot be told apart from "contains site 232 or 189". A hypothesis for the final-design check,
   not a finding.

4. Near-ties. All eight keep their order at def2-SVPD (rho 1.000), but the gap between E3 and E2 shrinks from 2.37
   kcal/mol at def2-SVP to 0.59 (E3 is next to the C10 carboxylate, E2 is not). Production against the check level
   (L2 corr - L1) differs by up to 3.16 kcal/mol (E7) and by up to 23% (E3). Stage 4c's arrangements were chosen to
   be widely spaced; an optimiser's top ~10 will not be. Designs closer than about these differences can swap at the
   diffuse level. This supports reporting both levels on every final design (rule 2) and is for the design-validation
   pre-registration to settle: how ties within the stated basis uncertainty are reported.

5. L3. After the correction, E8 overtakes E5 at L3 (gap 0.81 kcal/mol), the only change of order (rho 0.976). E5's
   L3 reactant is in a different electronic state (STAGE4C_EXPLORATORY.txt item 2), so this is not a basis-
   convergence result. Without E5: L1 against L3, k 0.921, residual 1.045, rho 1.000. def2-TZVPD is not used
   further (Amendment 1).

6. Reading taken forward (the pre-registered consequences are applied in the plan update; this note adds only):
   - at def2-SVP the corrected pseudopotential sphere is a validated stand-in for the bare sphere; at def2-SVPD it is
     the best available but not directly validated, and rule 2's numbers are reported with that caveat;
   - the basis sensitivity depends on the arrangement; spheres at the C10 carboxylate may be the most sensitive
     (hypothesis; final designs report both levels);
   - "diffuse functions make all six catalytic arrangements less catalytic" is withdrawn; the ~5% average stands.
