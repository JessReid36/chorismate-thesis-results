Stage 4c - EXPLORATORY note (9 October 2026). Not a pre-registered test: written after Stage 4c's results and the
orbital and Loewdin diagnostics were seen. Stage 4c's verdict stands as recorded in STAGE4C_REPORT.txt (check 0 PASS;
L1 against L3: k 0.845, residual 1.655, rho 1.000, signs agree -> RANKING ONLY). Every number below is in
STAGE4C_EXPLORATORY_REPORT.txt (cs4c_explore.py on the committed outputs).

1. A design error in Stage 4c, corrected by Amendment 1. The pre-registered quantity is ddE from the QM energy, with
   the sphere's steric term (LJ) excluded from L1 and reported separately. In L0, L2 and L3 each sphere's Ne-type
   pseudopotential repels electrons even at zero charge, and that repulsion differs between reactant and TS, so their
   ddE carries a steric term that L1's does not. L1 and L0 (same basis; only the representation differs) disagree by
   up to 1.72 kcal/mol (E7), RMS 1.0. Stage 4b's B2 (the pseudopotential changes a by 0.49% and b by 1.6%) could not
   show this: its symmetric sums cancel any charge-independent term.

2. E5 at def2-TZVPD: a different electronic state in the reactant. From def2-SVPD to def2-TZVPD every other
   arrangement's reactant and TS shift together (within 0.6 kcal/mol); E5's reactant shifts by -9.80 and its TS by
   -2.95. In that reactant only: HOMO-LUMO gap 0.30 eV (0.94 at def2-SVPD), 47 SCF cycles, and the ether oxygen O3
   gains about 0.28 e more than at the smaller bases (Loewdin shift against the bare substrate -0.425 e, against
   -0.150 at def2-SVPD and -0.123 at def2-SVP), taken from the C10 carboxylate (O5, O6, C10). E5 puts +1 spheres
   3.07 A from O3 and 2.60 A from H3, and a -1 sphere 2.58 A from O5. Its TS at def2-TZVPD resembles def2-SVPD's
   (O3 -0.242 against -0.218). Loewdin charges depend on the basis (Davidson & Chakravorty 1992), but the reversal
   between reactant and TS at one basis does not. Whether the state is physical charge transfer or a residual trap or
   method failure cannot be settled from these data. Rappoport & Furche 2010 warn that basis-set convergence "may be
   severely impacted by ground-state instability problems". Without E5, def2-SVPD and def2-TZVPD agree within
   0.59 kcal/mol (residual 0.20).

3. Diffuse functions shrink the barrier change. At fixed representation (L2 against L0) diffuse functions make all six
   catalytic arrangements less catalytic (by 0.19-1.64 kcal/mol) and both anti-catalytic ones less anti-catalytic;
   overall k 0.94. Stage 4b's larger basis effect (def2-SVPD's curvature about 37% above def2-SVP's) was measured on
   the total energy at one geometry; the design uses the TS-minus-reactant difference, where most of it cancels
   (site 117's differential curvature at L1 is -0.99 kcal/mol). The plan update's framing of def2-SVP's polarisation
   deficit as the open question is corrected in its Stage 4c section.

4. Polarisation at production level. Beyond the first-order (A-matrix) prediction, polarisation adds 1.0 kcal/mol
   (10%) of lowering for one +1 sphere at site 117 - consistent with s17's 7.5-14.3% at the same site over five
   frames - and 4.2-7.2 kcal/mol (21-31%) for the three-sphere arrangements E8 and E5; it removes 4.7 (20%) of E7's
   raising. The ranking was kept among these eight, which are widely spaced; near-ties were not tested.

5. Electronic diagnostics. At production level (L1) all gaps are 1.28-3.12 eV, the smallest for E5 (1.28 R, 1.40 TS);
   L0's gaps are within 0.05 eV of L1's. Net-negative arrangements (E3, -1; E6, net -0.5; E7, net -1) put the HOMO at
   +3.9 to +6.5 eV at def2-SVP and +3.1 to +5.4 eV with diffuse functions; at def2-TZVPD their gaps narrow to
   0.43-1.63 eV (def2-SVPD 1.47-2.17) because the LUMO falls, the pattern expected for an electron not bound in
   vacuum, yet their ddE agree between def2-SVPD and def2-TZVPD within 0.27 kcal/mol. A positive DFT HOMO is not by
   itself proof of an unbound electron (Galbraith & Schaefer 1996), and dianion binding can change when diffuse
   functions are added (Dreuw & Cederbaum 2002); def2-SVP binds such electrons because it is compact. This is a
   limitation of designing a dianion in vacuum (D1), which the CPCM check of final designs addresses.

6. Reading taken forward (Amendment 1; the plan's Stage 4c section):
   - ranking and sign hold across representation and basis for these arrangements; for near-ties this is untested;
   - like-for-like, diffuse functions change the barrier change by about 6%, making catalytic arrangements slightly
     less catalytic: def2-SVP slightly overstates the lowering;
   - the largest discrepancy is not basis convergence but a change of electronic state in the most strongly
     polarising arrangement - the kind an optimiser maximising first-order lowering will choose - so final designs
     need electronic screens at every level used;
   - def2-TZVPD adds nothing over def2-SVPD where both behave and is the less stable of the two; further diffuse-level
     work uses def2-SVPD.
